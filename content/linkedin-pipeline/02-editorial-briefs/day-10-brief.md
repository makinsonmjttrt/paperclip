# Day 10 Brief

**Post date:** Day 10 (Monday)
**Content type:** Opinion
**Theme:** FPZ thought leadership

---

## Topic
The creative industry does not have an AI problem. It has a leadership problem.

## Angle
The tools exist. The talent is findable. What is missing in most creative organisations is leadership that can make consequential, confident decisions about AI adoption. The gap Martyn sees is not technological. It is executive. Companies with strong creative leadership make deliberate AI choices. Companies without it default to whatever the IT team specifies or whatever a consultant recommends. This is the provocation that closes the ten-day sequence.

## Key data point
Anchored in FPZ recruitment intelligence. The observable signal: when Martyn receives an AI creative brief, the brief is authored by IT or digital transformation teams at least as often as by creative leadership. That pattern is itself the data point. Directional claim, clearly framed as Martyn's professional observation, not a cited statistic.

N/A - FPZ proprietary market observation. The post is explicit that this is Martyn's read of the market, not a published figure.

## Why this matters
This is the highest-stakes post in the sequence. It names a failure mode that everyone in Martyn's audience will recognise, and it places the accountability squarely with creative leaders. It will generate strong agreement from those who believe it and strong pushback from those who don't. Both outcomes are good for reach.

## Voice note
Mostly Martyn personal voice, occasional FPZ mention. This is a provocative opinion piece, not a balanced analysis. Martyn takes a clear position: "The bottleneck is leadership, not technology." The post should feel like something worth arguing about. End with an invitation to disagree, not just affirm.
