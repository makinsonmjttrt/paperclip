## Topic
Five human skills commanding salary premiums in AI-era creative teams

## Angle
As AI handles mechanical execution, a specific set of human capabilities has become materially more valuable. This is not a motivational point. It is a hiring and negotiation reality that Martyn observes in offer stages. The five skills are specific, non-obvious, and immediately actionable for anyone managing or joining a creative team.

## Key data point
Framed as Martyn's direct market observation from FPZ placements, with directional language. The underlying principle is supported by McKinsey's research on "uniquely human" skills commanding premiums as AI automates tasks (The Economic Potential of Generative AI, McKinsey Global Institute). The five skills are drawn from observable patterns in what creative organisations are actually paying for.

The five skills (for the Writer's reference):
1. Creative direction under ambiguity - the ability to make consequential aesthetic judgements with incomplete information
2. Cross-functional translation - converting between creative, technical, and commercial language
3. Taste calibration - knowing when AI output is "good enough" vs. when it undermines brand standards
4. Stakeholder management at pace - running creative review processes that AI has accelerated
5. Ethical and brand risk judgement - knowing what AI should not produce, not just what it can produce
