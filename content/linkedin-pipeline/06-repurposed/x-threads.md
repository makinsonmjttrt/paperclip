## Day 1 Thread: AI Fluency is the New Baseline

**1/4** The most in-demand skill on creative job specs is no longer Photoshop. AI literacy now leads the brief.

Not a prediction. It happened.

**2/4** I read dozens of creative briefs every week. Twelve months ago: Adobe suite + five years brand work. Now the first filter is AI fluency.

Can they build a workflow with generative tools? Can they evaluate AI output against brand standards?

**3/4** A senior creative search runs 60-90 days. In that window, Midjourney ships a new version. Runway launches Gen-4. Sora gets shut down entirely.

You cannot spec for tools that reinvent themselves every quarter.

**4/4** If your creative job specs still lead with specific tool names, you are filtering out the people who would add the most value.

Test for learning velocity, not tool familiarity.

## Day 2 Thread: "AI Experience" is Overvalued

**1/4** "3+ years Midjourney experience."

I push back on this every week. Midjourney V5 shipped March 2023. V6 followed December. The interface changed so dramatically that V5 workflows were barely relevant by V6.

**2/4** Runway: Gen-1 to Gen-3 in under 24 months. Different tool each time.

Sora: closed beta to GA to shutdown. All within the span of a typical probation period.

**3/4** You cannot spec for experience with tools that reinvent themselves every quarter.

What you can test for is learning velocity. How fast someone picks up a new tool, evaluates output, and builds it into a production workflow.

**4/4** Briefs that confuse familiarity with capability. The two have never been further apart.

How are you testing for AI capability in creative hiring?

## Day 3 Thread: Holding Companies Are Your Preview

**1/4** WPP: £300M/year AI investment.
Publicis: production workflows restructured around Marcel.
Omnicom: close behind.

When holding companies move this fast, it is advance notice for everyone else.

**2/4** Agencies restructure first because they face margin pressure first. They absorb the disruption, standardise the workflow, then clients ask: why are we not doing this in-house?

**3/4** Mark Read at WPP: AI replacing production hours. Arthur Sadoun at Publicis: redeploying human capacity toward strategy and judgment. On-record from investor calls.

**4/4** If you lead a creative team on the brand side: what will your board ask you to implement once these agency workflows become baseline?

That conversation is coming. The only variable is timing.

## Day 4 Thread: The Chief AI Creative Officer Gap

**1/3** Three clients asked me this month: does our organisation need a Chief AI Creative Officer?

I still don't have a clean answer.

**2/3** The Chief AI Officer title went mainstream 2023-2025. But nearly all sit in tech or digital transformation. They own AI strategy in the abstract.

Nobody owns the intersection of creative standards and AI capability. Who decides when AI output meets the brand bar.

**3/3** That function sits in a gap between the creative director, the CTO, and whoever runs digital transformation. Nobody fully owns it.

If you've solved this, I want to hear how. If you haven't, you're in good company.

## Day 5 Thread: The Salary Barbell

**1/4** Everyone talks about AI compressing creative salaries. That is half the picture.

**2/4** What I actually see: a barbell.

Top end: senior talent with genuine AI fluency commanding material premiums. People who direct AI output and make consequential aesthetic decisions. Companies pay more than two years ago.

**3/4** Other end: mid-level generalist roles under sustained downward pressure. AI handles enough mechanical work that companies consolidate these positions.

WEF Future of Jobs 2025 and McKinsey's GenAI data both flag this divergence.

**4/4** The middle is thinning. The top is paying more. The entry point is narrower.

Your compensation benchmarks from 18 months ago are wrong at both ends.

## Day 6 Thread: The Brief That Changed Three Times

**1/4** Last quarter we ran a search that changed three times before we placed anyone.

Started: senior motion designer. Clear spec. Budget agreed.

**2/4** Two weeks in, client adopted Runway Gen-4. Half the motion work was handled by AI. Brief shifted to a hybrid creative-technical profile.

Three weeks later, another shift. Now they wanted someone to build the entire AI-assisted production pipeline.

**3/4** Different role. Different seniority. Different budget.

We placed someone excellent. But they bore almost no resemblance to the original brief.

**4/4** AI is making creative briefs genuinely unstable. Tools evolve mid-search. What the team can do changes. What they need changes with it.

The firms that thrive will be built for this instability.

## Day 7 Thread: The Canva Effect

**1/4** Canva shipped AI-assisted design to over 170 million users. That is not a product update. It is a hiring reset.

**2/4** The person entering the creative workforce in 2026 has never known design without AI assistance. Their baseline includes capabilities that were senior-level specialisms three years ago.

**3/4** If your junior creatives arrive with AI fluency baked in, what are you actually testing for at interview?

Judgment. Visual literacy. The ability to tell when AI output is on-brand and when it is not.

**4/4** Canva gave everyone the tools. The differentiator is what people do with them.

## Day 8 Thread: Five Skills Commanding a Premium

**1/5** Five human skills commanding a salary premium in AI-era creative teams. I track this through the briefs I handle.

**2/5** 1. Creative direction under ambiguity: making consequential aesthetic decisions with incomplete info. AI generates options. Someone has to choose. Fast.

2. Cross-functional translation: converting between creative, technical, and commercial language in the same meeting.

**3/5** 3. Taste calibration: knowing when AI output meets the brand bar. Pattern recognition at scale, not personal preference.

4. Stakeholder management at pace: AI compressed timelines. Review cycles that took days now take hours.

**4/5** 5. Ethical and brand risk judgment: knowing what AI should not produce, not just what it can. Steepest growth curve in demand right now.

**5/5** McKinsey's GenAI research confirms it: productivity gains concentrate in senior workers who integrate AI into high-judgment tasks.

Which of these five is hardest to assess at interview?

## Day 9 Thread: AI is Reshuffling, Not Killing

**1/4** Everyone talks about AI eliminating creative jobs. The actual data tells a different story.

Search LinkedIn: AI Prompt Engineer. AI Creative Director. AI Production Coordinator. Synthetic Media Producer.

None existed in 2022. All pay well. Most are hard to fill.

**2/4** Computer graphic artist postings: down 33% (Bloomberry, 180M job analysis).
AI Creative Director roles: up from zero to hundreds of active listings.

The market is not shrinking. It is reshuffling.

**3/4** Companies that framed AI as a threat cut headcount. Saved money short term. Then discovered they needed skills that did not exist on their old org chart.

Companies that framed AI as evolution kept teams, invested in upskilling, reached the new capability faster.

**4/4** "AI is killing creative jobs" is a lazy read of a more interesting picture.

What new role title exists at your company that was not there two years ago?

## Day 10 Thread: Leadership, Not Tools

**1/4** The creative industry does not have an AI problem. It has a leadership problem.

The tools exist. The talent is findable. What is missing is leadership that makes consequential, confident decisions about AI adoption.

**2/4** When I receive an AI creative brief, I look at who authored it. Half the time: IT or digital transformation. Not creative leadership. Not the people who understand what the output needs to achieve.

**3/4** When creative leaders don't own AI, decisions default to whoever moves first. Usually someone with technical authority but no creative judgment.

Result: AI adoption shaped by capability rather than intent.

**4/4** Strong creative leadership makes deliberate AI choices. Which workflows. Which outputs. Which decisions stay human.

That clarity is not a technology decision. It is a leadership one.

Tell me where I am wrong.
