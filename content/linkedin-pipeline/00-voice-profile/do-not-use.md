# Do Not Use: Martyn Makinson Voice
*Last updated: 2026-04-02*

These are words, phrases, and patterns that are incompatible with Martyn's voice. Some are self-stated bans. Some are inferred from the absence of these patterns across all available writing samples. Each entry has a reason.

## Self-Stated Bans (Direct from CLAUDE.md and NotebookLM voice rules)

| Banned | Reason |
|--------|--------|
| Em-dashes (—) | Explicitly banned. Use a comma, a full stop, or two sentences instead. |
| "suggests" / "indicates" / "appears to" / "seems to" | Hedge language. Martyn states things directly. |
| "it's worth noting" | Process narration. Cuts to the point instead. |
| "let's explore" / "we're examining" / "we're looking at" | Process narration. He doesn't narrate the analysis, he delivers it. |
| "This isn't X, it's Y" | Corrective construction. He implies contrast, never lectures it. |
| "seeking" / "looking for" / "in search of" / "pursuing" / "hunting for" | Seeking language. Sounds desperate. He attracts, doesn't chase. |
| "crisis" / "panic" / "urgent" / "desperate" | Business clichés. Manipulative. Not his register. |
| "numbers paint a stark picture" | Cliché. The data speaks for itself. |
| Rule-of-three adjective stacking | e.g. "dynamic, innovative, forward-thinking." Never. One adjective at most. |
| Competitor mentions | Defensive positioning. Never name a competitor in public content. |

## Structural Patterns to Avoid

**Questions as openers**
He never opens with a question. Not "Have you ever noticed...?", not "What if I told you...?", not "Is your hiring process working?". Confident declarative statements only.

**Rhetorical devices**
No anaphora ("I believe in X. I believe in Y. I believe in Z."). No extended metaphors. No storytelling arcs that build to a reveal. He states the conclusion first.

**Long wind-ups before the point**
If the point takes more than one sentence to get to, it's too long. He leads with the substance, not the context.

**Excessive social proof stacking**
He mentions "300+ appointments" once. He doesn't list credentials, logos, or client names in public content. One data point, then move on.

**Calls to action in LinkedIn post style**
"Like this post if you agree." / "Save this for later." / "Tag someone who needs to hear this." None of these. If there's a CTA, it's a direct personal invitation.

**Generic closing questions vs. acceptable specific questions**
Banned: "What do you think? Drop a comment below." / "Who else agrees?" / "Share your thoughts."
Acceptable: A specific, personally framed question that invites genuine intelligence from the reader. Must reference the specific context of the post. Must not be answerable with "yes" or "no." Example: "How are you testing for AI capability in your creative hiring?" — specific enough that only the right audience can answer it.
The test: Could any post on any topic end with this question? If yes, ban it. If no, it might be acceptable.

**Inspirational flourishes**
"The future belongs to those who..." / "In a world where..." / "Now more than ever..." None of these.

**Manufactured urgency**
"Don't miss this." / "Act now." / "Limited availability." Not his tone. He's calm, not selling.

**Victim narrative**
Never positions himself or his clients as victims of the market. Observes trends, doesn't dramatise them.

## The Inferred Rules (not self-stated, but confirmed by pattern)

These are inferred from the complete absence of these patterns in available writing:

- He never opens a section by restating what he just said in different words.
- He never tells you what he's about to tell you (no "In this post, I'm going to cover...").
- He never adds emotional amplifiers that he didn't earn with specifics ("incredible", "amazing", "truly remarkable").
- He never hedges a claim and then repeats the same claim more confidently (hedge-then-restate pattern).
- He never closes with a generic question designed to generate engagement ("What do you think? Drop a comment below.").
