The salary conversation in creative hiring has a compression problem. Everyone talks about it. AI reduces the hours, rates come down.

That is half the picture.

What I actually see in offer stages is a barbell. One end: senior creative talent with genuine AI fluency commanding material premiums. People who can direct AI output, maintain brand standards, and make consequential aesthetic decisions under pressure. Companies pay more for them than two years ago, not less.

Other end: mid-level generalist roles under sustained downward pressure. AI handles enough mechanical work that companies consolidate these positions or expect more output per head.

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs 2025 report flagged this divergence at the macro level. McKinsey's generative AI data confirms it: productivity gains concentrate in senior knowledge workers who integrate AI into high-judgment tasks.

The middle is thinning. The top is paying more. The entry point is narrower.

For hiring managers, your compensation benchmarks from 18 months ago are wrong at both ends. For candidates, the premium is in judgment, not execution.
