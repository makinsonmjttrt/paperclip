# Positioning Opportunities
*Where Martyn can differentiate his LinkedIn voice*
*Research period: Last 30 days (March-April 2026)*

---

## The One-Line Opportunity

Every AI executive search firm talks about AI. Nobody talks about what AI actually does inside a creative production pipeline. Martyn is the only recruiter in this space who can speak with production-floor authority about gaming, film, advertising, experiential, and virtual production - and what it means when AI lands in those environments. That voice does not exist anywhere on LinkedIn right now.

---

## Positioning Framework

**What Martyn is not:**
- Another recruiter posting about AI engineering salaries
- Another exec search firm claiming to find Chief AI Officers
- Another creative staffing platform with a salary guide

**What Martyn is:**
The recruiter who knows what it actually takes to deliver when the tools, teams, and workflows are all changing at once. The person who has placed the people building the creative AI stack in gaming, film, advertising, and experiential - and has seen, from the inside, what works and what fails.

**The voice to own:** Production authority. Not analyst. Not cheerleader. The person who has been in the room when the brief changes, the timeline shifts, and the client asks "can you do this with AI now?"

---

## Five Specific Positioning Angles

---

### 1. The Production Realist

**The opportunity:** Every AI content creator on LinkedIn is either a tool evangelist ("here are 10 AI tools you need") or a macro-trend commentator. Nobody is the realist who says "here's where AI has genuinely changed creative production, and here's where the hype is still running ahead of the reality."

**Why Martyn can own this:** He has placed people inside production pipelines across multiple sectors. He knows which roles actually changed and which ones are still the same job with different vocabulary.

**Sample post angle:** "Every studio claims they're AI-native now. In the last 90 days I've briefed on 14 senior creative roles across gaming, VFX, and experiential. Here's what AI-native actually looked like in the briefs - and where it was just language."

**Differentiation signal:** Production specificity. Mention real tools, real workflows, real constraints. The audience will recognise it immediately.

---

### 2. The Cross-Sector Translator

**The opportunity:** Talent is crossing sector lines in ways that the industry hasn't fully named yet. Game engine artists are going to advertising. VFX supervisors are going to real-time content studios. Technical Artists from gaming are the most sought-after people in generative brand content. Nobody is narrating this migration.

**Why Martyn can own this:** FPZ operates across gaming, film, advertising, and experiential simultaneously. This vantage point is structurally impossible for single-sector recruiters to replicate.

**Sample post angle:** "The three best AI-capable motion designers we've placed in the last six months all came from the same background. It's not where you'd expect."

**Differentiation signal:** Cross-sector specificity. Not "the metaverse," not "XR" - the actual production disciplines and what transfers between them.

---

### 3. The Leadership Interrogator

**The opportunity:** The conversation about creative leadership in the AI era is stuck at "CCOs need to embrace AI." Nobody is asking the harder question: what does creative leadership actually look like when the team's core capabilities are being rebuilt? What does it mean to direct work you can't fully evaluate by craft alone?

**Why Martyn can own this:** Senior placement for creative leaders is FPZ's stated focus (76% of roles at senior level). Martyn interviews these people. He hears the real answers.

**Sample post angle:** "I asked a Creative Director candidate last week how they assess AI-generated creative work. The answer revealed exactly why most studios' AI transitions are stalling. Here's what they said."

**Differentiation signal:** Leadership-level specificity. The audience for this is CCOs, ECDs, Studio Heads - exactly the people making hiring decisions.

---

### 4. The Honest Brief

**The opportunity:** Hiring failure in the AI creative space is rampant but nobody admits to it publicly. The mismatch between "we want an AI creative lead" and what studios are actually set up to support is a recognised problem among practitioners but absent from public LinkedIn content.

**Why Martyn can own this:** The recruiter who sees the brief, places the hire, and (sometimes) sees the outcome. This is confidential intelligence that only a recruiter accumulates.

**Sample post angle:** "The brief said: AI-native creative lead, must have generative video experience, strong art direction. What the brief didn't say: the creative team has never shipped an AI-assisted project, the client approval workflow hasn't changed, and the budget assumes AI reduces hours by 40%."

**Differentiation signal:** Brief-level honesty. This resonates with every in-house creative leader who has been through a failed AI hire and couldn't say why publicly.

---

### 5. The Salary Reality Check

**The opportunity:** The market for AI + creative hybrid roles in the UK is genuinely opaque. US data (Aquent's $162k AI PM figure) has no UK equivalent for creative production roles. The gap between what studios advertise and what they pay is real and documented nowhere publicly.

**Why Martyn can own this:** Even directional observations from recent mandates ("we're seeing Technical Artists with generative AI stack command day rates 30-40% above their non-AI equivalent") are more credible than any published survey.

**Sample post angle:** "What is an AI-capable Technical Artist worth in a UK advertising studio vs. a games company vs. a virtual production facility? We've placed all three in the last six months. The range surprised us."

**Differentiation signal:** UK specificity, production sector specificity, contractor vs. permanent nuance.

---

## What to Avoid

- **Thought leadership that could have been written by anyone.** If the post could be published by Christian & Timbers without changing a word, it's not a FPZ post.
- **Competing on AI tool commentary.** This positions Martyn as an enthusiast, not an authority.
- **The "AI is an opportunity for creatives" reassurance play.** The audience is past needing reassurance. They need honesty.
- **US data without UK translation.** The creative sector audience is predominantly UK/European.
- **Recruitment marketing language.** "We're excited to share," "passionate about talent," "transformational opportunity" - immediate credibility loss with senior creative audience.

---

## The Editorial Test

Before any post goes out, one question:

**"Could this have been written by someone who has never placed a senior creative in a studio where AI was already in the pipeline?"**

If yes: rewrite with a production-specific observation.
If no: publish.
