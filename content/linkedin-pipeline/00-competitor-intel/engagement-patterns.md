# Engagement Patterns
*What gets traction on LinkedIn in the AI recruitment / creative tech space*
*Research period: Last 30 days (March-April 2026)*

---

## The Algorithm Shift in 2026

LinkedIn's algorithm now weights engagement quality over volume. The key changes relevant to FPZ:

- **Comments count 2x more than likes** in engagement calculations
- **Dwell time matters** - posts that get read before being scrolled past rank higher
- **"Expertise Signals"** are the new priority - content demonstrating unique knowledge from verifiable industry experience
- The "spray and pray" era is over - AI detection identifies generic content and deprioritises it

**Implication for Martyn:** One post that generates 40 meaningful comments will outperform five posts that each get 20 likes.

## Formats That Work

### 1. Tactical / Teach-in-Public Posts
**What they are:** Step-by-step practical content that gives away real knowledge.
**Example from market:** "How to evaluate a Creative Director for AI readiness in 5 questions."
**Why it works:** High dwell time (people read to the end), generates comments from people sharing their own experience.
**Engagement premium:** Reported 3-5x impressions vs. opinion-only content.
**FPZ application:** Briefing guides, interview frameworks, production readiness assessments.

---

### 2. Specific Data / Salary / Numbers Posts
**What they are:** Concrete figures from real placements or market observation.
**Example from market:** Aquent's salary guide content - AI PM at $162k, Technical Artist at the generative AI intersection.
**Why it works:** Data posts get saved (saves now carry algorithmic weight). Numbers are shareable.
**Engagement premium:** Salary data posts consistently outperform general trend posts.
**FPZ application:** Directional rate observations from recent mandates (don't need to publish exact figures - ranges work). "We've seen day rates for AI-capable motion designers move from X to Y in 18 months."

---

### 3. Contrarian Takes With Evidence
**What they are:** A position that contradicts the prevailing narrative, backed by a specific observation.
**Example from market:** "AI isn't fixing the hiring gap - it's widening it." (sourced from LinkedIn's own data showing 65% of candidates find job search harder despite AI tools)
**Why it works:** Provokes comments from people who agree AND disagree - both drive reach.
**Risk:** Hedge language kills these. The post needs to commit to the take.
**FPZ application:** "Most studios aren't ready for the AI creative hire they think they want." "The Chief AI Officer title is being used to avoid a harder conversation about creative leadership."

---

### 4. First-Person Observation Stories
**What they are:** A specific thing Martyn witnessed or experienced in a recent conversation with a candidate or client.
**Example format:** "Spoke to a VFX supervisor this week who's placed three AI-native artists in the last six months. Every single brief had the same flaw. Here's what it was."
**Why it works:** Specificity creates credibility. "A VFX supervisor" is more compelling than "industry professionals."
**Engagement driver:** People comment to share their own version of the same experience.
**FPZ application:** Pattern observations from candidate conversations and client briefs.

---

### 5. Question-Ending Posts
**What they are:** Posts that close with an open question to the reader.
**Example:** "What's the most honest answer you've heard to 'what does AI-ready actually mean in your studio?'"
**Why it works:** Algorithm treats responses to direct questions differently from passive reads.
**Best practice:** The question must be specific enough that people feel they have something real to say.

---

### 6. The "Nobody Talks About This" Frame
**What they are:** Posts that name the thing the industry is dancing around.
**Example from market:** Posts about creative directors' actual anxiety about AI (not their public "AI is an opportunity" position) get disproportionate engagement.
**Why it works:** Validates what people already think but won't say in a job interview.
**FPZ application:** The gap between what studios say they want in an AI hire and what they actually need.

## Formats That Are Declining

| Format | Why It's Declining | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Carousels | LinkedIn reportedly deprioritising since late 2025 | Multiple reports of engagement drops |
| Generic AI trend summaries | Oversaturated, AI-detection flags as low-effort | Every recruiter is doing this |
| Job listing posts | Treated as ads, algorithmic penalty | Use jobs tab instead |
| "Excited to announce" posts | Immediate scroll-past signal | Unless genuinely newsworthy |
| Lists of tools without context | Influencer/educator territory, not recruiter | Positions you wrong |

## Posting Cadence Signals

**Consistency beats volume.** Data suggests:
- 3-4 posts per week outperforms daily posting for service professionals
- 30 minutes/day strategic commenting on target audience posts drives reach more than extra posts
- Posting at 7:30-9am or 12-1pm UK time reaches professional audience during commute/lunch

**Comment strategy:** Appearing in the comments of industry voices (creative directors, studio CTOs, agency leaders) 10 minutes after they post gives a consistent algorithmic boost. The comment needs to add a substantive perspective, not just validate.

## Content That Drives the Right Engagement (FPZ Specific)

The goal isn't maximum reach - it's reach among people who hire senior creative talent or are senior creative talent in AI-entering studios.

**What drives that audience specifically:**
- Production-specific detail (Unreal, Houdini, real-time pipeline language signals expertise)
- Acknowledgment of the tension between craft values and AI efficiency
- UK/European context (London creative sector, UK games industry, European co-production)
- Cross-sector observations (the game engine artist going to advertising - this is a real phenomenon the audience recognises)
- Honest assessments over cheerleading

**What repels that audience:**
- Generic "AI will transform everything" energy
- US-centric data without UK translation
- Recruiting-speak (talent acquisition, stakeholder alignment, talent pipeline)
- Excessive positivity about AI that doesn't match what they're living

## Market Watch: Structural Threats to Monitor

**Aquent's "Skill" brand** - AI-powered staffing platform launched by Aquent. Combines machine-matching with human recruiting. Currently focused on marketing/creative generalist roles, not production leadership. Watch for whether they move upmarket into senior creative placement.

**Runway Talent Network** - Direct-to-talent marketplace for AI filmmakers. Currently narrow (Runway tool fluency). Does not compete with executive search but signals that AI tool companies want to own parts of the talent market. If Midjourney or Adobe build similar networks, the freelance tier becomes a different market.

**OpenAI Jobs Platform (mid-2026 launch)** - OpenAI confirmed a jobs platform targeting large enterprises and SMBs. AI-matching for AI-skilled talent. Not creative-production-specific but signals further platform competition for mid-market placement. Senior search remains relationship-dependent.

**Implication for FPZ:** Platforms will own transactional placement. The further FPZ moves toward genuine executive search (senior, retained, relationship-driven), the less exposed it is to platform competition.

## Benchmark: What Strong Engagement Looks Like in This Niche

For a 2,000-5,000 follower account in the UK creative/tech recruitment space:
- Good post: 200-500 impressions, 10-20 reactions, 3-5 comments
- Strong post: 1,000-3,000 impressions, 30-60 reactions, 10-20 comments
- Breakout post: 5,000+ impressions, 100+ reactions, 30+ comments

A single contrarian take or specific salary data post can break into the 5,000+ range without paid promotion if it hits a nerve.
