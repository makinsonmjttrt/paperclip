# Day 5 Draft

The salary conversation in creative hiring has a compression problem. Everyone talks about it. AI reduces the hours, so the rates come down.

That is only half the picture.

What I actually see in offer stages is a barbell. One end: senior creative talent with genuine AI fluency commanding material premiums. These are people who can direct AI output, maintain brand standards, and make consequential aesthetic decisions under pressure. Companies pay more for them than they did two years ago, not less.

Other end: mid-level generalist roles under sustained downward pressure. AI handles enough of the mechanical work that companies are consolidating these positions or expecting more output per head.

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs 2025 report identified this divergence at a macro level. McKinsey's data on generative AI confirms it: productivity gains concentrate in senior knowledge workers who integrate AI into high-judgment tasks.

The middle is thinning. The top is paying more. The entry point is narrower.

For hiring managers, this means your compensation benchmarks from 18 months ago are wrong at both ends of the range. For candidates, the premium is in judgment, not execution.

Where are you seeing the biggest shift in your compensation conversations right now?
