## Item 1 — Kering Appoints First Chief Digital, AI & IT Officer

**Date:** 17 March 2026
**Sector:** Luxury / Fashion
**Relevance:** Senior AI executive hire; cross-sector talent move

Kering appointed Pierre Houlès to a newly created Chief Digital, AI & IT Officer role, effective immediately. He joins the Executive Committee and reports to the Group COO. His brief is to accelerate Kering's technology architecture transformation across Gucci, Saint Laurent, Balenciaga and the other Houses.

No luxury sector experience. Kering's decision to hire from outside the fashion industry for an AI leadership role is a signal worth tracking — they needed someone who'd already transformed a complex multi-brand enterprise, not someone who understood fashion.

**Why it matters for FPZ:** Luxury and fashion are quietly building serious AI leadership capability. As that investment flows down into production (campaigns, immersive retail, digital shows), the studios and agencies serving those clients will need talent who bridges AI and high-end creative delivery. This hire is the upstream signal.

## Item 2 — Apple Hires Google VP Lilian Rincon to Lead AI Product Marketing

**Date:** 27 March 2026
**Sector:** Big Tech / AI Product Marketing
**Relevance:** VP-level exec move; AI marketing leadership

Apple hired Lilian Rincon, most recently VP of Product at Google Shopping, as VP of Product Marketing for AI. She will oversee Apple Intelligence and Siri marketing, reporting to SVP Greg Joswiak. The hire comes as Apple prepares a rebuilt, Gemini-powered Siri for later in 2026 after a year of missed AI commitments damaged the brand's reputation.

Rincon spent nine years at Google across Shopping, Assistant, and search products. Before Google, she worked at Microsoft and Skype. Her profile is consumer AI product at scale — the exact gap Apple needs to fill as it tries to close ground on OpenAI and Google in the consumer AI race.

**Why it matters for FPZ:** This is the clearest recent example of AI-specific VP roles being created at the top of the market — roles that didn't exist three years ago. Apple is also a major client of creative production studios for campaigns, product launches, and brand work. A new AI marketing VP reshaping how Apple communicates AI will drive downstream creative production briefs.

## Item 3 — Publicis Acquires Predictive Creative Analytics Platform AdgeAI

**Date:** 12 March 2026
**Sector:** Agency / Adtech / Creative Production
**Relevance:** AI acquisition reshaping creative production workflows

Publicis Groupe acquired Israel-based AdgeAI for a reported $100M (Calcalist), integrating it directly into Publicis Production. AdgeAI uses machine learning to forecast which creative elements — video, image, copy, GIF — are most likely to drive engagement before a campaign goes live. Clients included Google and Underoutfit. Co-founders Eyal and Asaf Ben Shalom remain in post, reporting to Publicis Production CEO Deepti Velury.

The acquisition extends Publicis' growing AI stack: CoreAI (2024), Epsilon (2019), Lotame (2025), Moov AI (talent/workflow, 2025), and now AdgeAI (creative performance). The pattern is clear — they are building a closed-loop system from audience data through creative decision to measurable outcome.

**Why it matters for FPZ:** Predictive creative analytics inside the agency model changes what roles are needed. The senior creative producers and EPs working inside Publicis Production will increasingly be expected to work with AI performance data in their creative decision-making. That's a skill profile shift. Studios and independent agencies who want to compete for that same work will need similar capability — and similar talent.

## Item 4 — CourtAvenue Acquires GTX Solutions to Build Enterprise AI Data Capability

**Date:** 30 March 2026
**Sector:** Independent Agency / Data / AI
**Relevance:** Agency M&A; AI capability acquisition creating new hiring demand

Independent agency CourtAvenue acquired data and marketing technology consultancy GTX Solutions in a cash-and-stock deal. GTX specialises in customer data platforms (Tealium, Adobe, Optimizely) with Fortune 100 clients including Hard Rock and Merck. CourtAvenue plans to grow the GTX team from current size to approximately 30 by end of year, with the combined team building custom AI solutions on top of existing martech stacks.

The deal is about closing the gap between data infrastructure and AI-driven experiences — GTX handles the plumbing, CourtAvenue's experience teams build what sits on top. Chief Data Officer Scott Hamm leads integration.

**Why it matters for FPZ:** Post-acquisition team growth of this kind generates specific senior hiring demand: data-fluent production leaders who can work across AI systems and creative delivery. CourtAvenue operates in exactly the space FPZ serves — experience design meets enterprise tech. Worth monitoring for roles that emerge from the integration.
