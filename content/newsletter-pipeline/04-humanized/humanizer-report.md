## Overall Assessment

The draft was better than most. The argument, structure, and editorial stance were already close to Martyn's voice. Most changes were surgical, not structural. Six specific AI patterns remained in the draft; all were fixed.

## Changes by Section

### Opening (paragraphs 1-3)

**Change 1: "importing capability from a direct competitor" → "poaching from a direct competitor"**
"Importing capability" is consultant-speak. "Poaching" is what Martyn would actually say. Same meaning, no corporate distance.

**Change 2: "write the job description, identify the market, run the process" → "Define the brief, map the market, run the process"**
The original tricolon used the generic corporate vocabulary ("write the job description") that the voice profile explicitly replaces with FPZ-specific language ("brief"). Kept the three-beat structure because it's functional here — describes a known process quickly. But reset the vocabulary to Martyn's.

**Change 3: "Four businesses reached the same conclusion by different routes:" → "Four businesses, same conclusion, different routes:"**
The original is AI-assembled. The revision uses the Triple Declarative structure (Signature Move 10) — fragments functioning as a list. Shorter, punchier, more Martyn.

**Change 4: "It is how you get them faster than the standard process allows" → "It's how you get them before everyone else does"**
"The standard process allows" is slightly formal and passive. "Before everyone else does" is direct, competitive, and positions the reader against a real-world constraint. Also uses the contraction "It's" — Martyn's writing uses contractions; the draft used "It is."

### Kering Section

**Change 5: "from concept through delivery" → "at every stage"**
"From concept through delivery" is a false range (AI pattern 12) — it sounds comprehensive but adds nothing specific. "At every stage" says the same thing without the consultancy-brochure feel.

**Change 6: "The investment is moving downstream. Faster than most production businesses have prepared for." → "That spend will hit production. Most businesses haven't caught up yet."**
"Investment moving downstream" is VC/consulting register, not Martyn's. He would say "spend" and he would not use "downstream" as a metaphor. Rewritten to be direct and in his vocabulary. Also tightened the second sentence — "haven't caught up yet" is more natural than "have prepared for."

### Apple Section

**Change 7: "The talent they require does not exist in any abundance." → "There isn't much talent to fill them."**
"Does not exist in any abundance" is awkward, over-constructed, and sounds like AI filling a word count. The replacement is six words and says the same thing. This is the kind of sentence Martyn would write — short, flat, slightly wry.

### Publicis Section

**FACTUAL FLAG: "fourth major AI acquisition" vs. five companies listed**
The draft says "fourth major AI acquisition" but then lists five: CoreAI, Epsilon, Lotame, Moov AI, and AdgeAI. Either the count is wrong or one of the listed companies does not qualify as a "major AI acquisition." This is a factual inconsistency that needs verification before publishing. Rewritten to remove the specific count claim: "latest in a string of major AI acquisitions by Publicis since 2019, following CoreAI, Epsilon, Lotame, and Moov AI." This is defensible as written regardless of whether AdgeAI is the fourth or fifth.

**Change 8: "They are building a closed loop from audience data through creative decision to measurable outcome." → "They're building a single stack from audience data to creative decision to measurable result."**
"Closed loop" is a technical/architect's metaphor. Martyn would say "single stack" or "one system." Also removed the false range "from...through...to" which over-structures a simple observation. Used contraction "They're."

**Change 9: "It also raises the bar for independent studios competing for the same briefs." → "Independent studios competing for the same briefs are further behind than most of them realise."**
"Raises the bar" is a listed cliche. The rewrite makes the same point but from the perspective of the studios — which is more interesting and more direct. "Further behind than most of them realise" is the kind of candid insider observation that is authentic to Martyn's voice.

### CourtAvenue Section

**Change 10: "Post-acquisition growth of this kind generates specific senior hiring demand at the integration point: data architecture meeting creative delivery." → "The hiring that follows this kind of acquisition is specific: people who understand data architecture and can operate inside a creative agency."**
"Post-acquisition growth of this kind generates senior hiring demand at the integration point" is over-engineered. It sounds like a management consultant wrote it. The revision says the same thing in plain language. "Operate inside a creative agency" is more Martyn than "meeting creative delivery."

**Change 11: "The brief for them will be unusual and the talent pool for them will be thin." → "The brief will be unusual and the talent pool will be thin."**
"For them" repeated twice in the same sentence is a grammatical artifact of AI construction. Removed both — the context is already established.

### What to Watch Section

**Change 12: AI triplet list broken up**
Original: "Data-fluent production leads. Creative executives who can read performance forecasts. AI-capable EPs working inside enterprise martech stacks."
This is a perfect AI Rule of Three — three parallel structures, same grammatical form, exhaustive in intent. Classic AI comprehensiveness pattern. Replaced with a different structure: two contrasts (a production lead who can vs. one who can't; an EP who can vs. one who can't) followed by an observation about the market. This is Martyn's Contrast Close (Signature Move 1) applied at sentence level.

**Change 13: "will have a structural advantage over the ones who start later" → "will hire faster than the ones who wait"**
"Structural advantage" is MBA/corporate speak. The actual advantage is speed of hire — Martyn would say that plainly. "The ones who wait" is more conversational than "the ones who start later."

**Change 14: "Watch the Q3 hiring from Publicis Production and CourtAvenue. That is where this week's news becomes specific." → "Watch the hiring from Publicis Production and CourtAvenue next quarter. That's where this week's news gets specific."**
Minor but consistent: "Q3" feels like a date tag rather than a natural reference; "next quarter" is how Martyn would say it in conversation. Contraction "That's" instead of "That is." "Gets" instead of "becomes" — more natural, less formal.
