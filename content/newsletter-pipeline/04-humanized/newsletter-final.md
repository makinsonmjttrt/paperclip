# AI Leadership Has Stopped Being a Recruitment Problem

*FourPointZero | April 2026*

---

Three of this week's four stories are about roles that didn't exist three years ago. The fourth is Apple reacting to a specific product failure by poaching from a direct competitor. None of them came through a standard talent pipeline.

Most companies treat inaugural AI leadership like any other C-suite vacancy. Define the brief, map the market, run the process. That works when the role has precedent. When there's no internal benchmark and the global talent pool is people who've done something adjacent in a different industry, the brief needs building differently.

Kering hired a car executive. Publicis and CourtAvenue bought companies. Apple took a nine-year Google veteran. Four businesses, same conclusion, different routes: the conventional process is too slow and too blunt for what they actually need. I see this in briefs week to week. The question is no longer what kind of AI leader you need. It's how you get them before everyone else does.

---

## Kering Creates Its First CDAIO Role

[Pierre Houlès joins Kering's Executive Committee](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/17/3257505/0/en/Pierre-Houl%C3%A8s-appointed-Chief-Digital-AI-IT-Officer-at-Kering.html) as the group's first Chief Digital, AI and IT Officer, responsible for technology transformation across Gucci, Saint Laurent, Balenciaga and the other Houses. His background is automotive (Renault Digital) and broadcast (Canal+). No luxury sector experience.

That was deliberate. Kering needed someone who had already transformed a complex multi-brand enterprise at scale. There was no internal benchmark for this role, so the brief was built around the problem, not the precedent. For studios and agencies serving luxury clients, this is the upstream signal. A CDAIO mandate across eight Houses will generate creative briefs that require AI integration at every stage. That spend will hit production. Most businesses haven't caught up yet.

---

## Apple Imports AI Credibility From Google

Apple appointed [Lilian Rincon](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/27/apple-hires-veteran-google-vp-to-lead-ai-product-marketing/) as VP of Product Marketing for AI. She will own how Apple Intelligence and a rebuilt, Gemini-powered Siri are communicated to market, reporting to SVP Greg Joswiak. Nine years at Google across Shopping, Assistant, and search products.

Apple's AI narrative has been damaged by a year of missed product commitments. The hire is a specific fix for a specific problem: Apple needs someone who has successfully communicated consumer AI at scale. Rincon's track record is the brief, not her familiarity with Apple's product culture. The role itself is new. VP of AI Product Marketing didn't exist at Apple 18 months ago. These inaugural VP-level AI appointments are appearing across every major technology company now. There isn't much talent to fill them.

---

## Publicis Acquires AdgeAI for ~$100M

[Publicis acquired Israel-based AdgeAI](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/adgeai-acquisition-publicis-ai-drive/) in March and integrated it directly into Publicis Production. AdgeAI forecasts which creative elements will drive campaign performance before a campaign goes live. Machine learning applied to the creative decision itself.

AdgeAI is the latest in a string of major AI acquisitions by Publicis since 2019, following CoreAI, Epsilon, Lotame, and Moov AI. They're building a single stack from audience data to creative decision to measurable result. The effect is already visible inside Publicis Production: senior creatives and executive producers now work alongside AI performance data as standard. That changes the talent profile for those roles. Independent studios competing for the same briefs are further behind than most of them realise. The gap is widening.

---

## CourtAvenue Buys Data Capability, Plans to Double the Team

Independent agency CourtAvenue [acquired data and martech consultancy GTX Solutions](https://www.adweek.com/media/exclusive-courtavenue-acquires-gtx-solutions/) in a cash-and-stock deal at the end of March. GTX builds on customer data platforms for Fortune 100 clients including Hard Rock and Merck. CourtAvenue builds experience design on top of enterprise tech stacks. The combined team is planned to reach 30 by year-end.

CourtAvenue couldn't hire data-fluent AI practitioners fast enough through standard search. Acquiring a team was faster. The hiring that follows this kind of acquisition is specific: people who understand data architecture and can operate inside a creative agency. Those roles aren't on job boards yet. They will be in Q3. The brief will be unusual and the talent pool will be thin. This is the same problem Publicis solved by acquisition, at independent agency scale.

---

## What to Watch Next Week

The acquisitions and inaugural appointments announced this month are step one. Step two is integration. When you embed a predictive analytics platform inside a production team, or merge a data consultancy into a creative agency, the roles that emerge are harder to brief. A production lead who can work from AI performance data is a different hire from one who can't. So is an EP who's comfortable inside an enterprise martech stack. Most hiring managers aren't writing those briefs yet.

That talent is genuinely rare. The companies that start building the brief now will hire faster than the ones who wait. Watch the hiring from Publicis Production and CourtAvenue next quarter. That's where this week's news gets specific.
