# Item 01: Kering CDAIO — LinkedIn Post
*Source: April 2026 FPZ Newsletter*
*Platform: LinkedIn | Target: 150–250 words | Standalone*

---

Kering created a role in March that had no equivalent anywhere in luxury.

Chief Digital, AI and IT Officer, responsible for technology transformation across all eight Houses. Gucci. Saint Laurent. Balenciaga. The full group. The person appointed came from Renault Digital and Canal+. No luxury sector experience.

That was deliberate.

When there's no internal benchmark for what the role looks like, the brief can't be built from precedent. It has to be built from the problem. Kering needed someone who had already managed digital and AI transformation across a complex, multi-brand business at scale. That profile existed in automotive and broadcast. It didn't exist in luxury.

I see this in briefs week to week. The companies moving fastest on inaugural AI leadership appointments are the ones who've stopped trying to describe the right candidate and started describing the problem that needs solving. The companies still building conventional briefs for roles that have no conventional benchmark are losing weeks before the search starts.

Pierre Houlès joins Kering's Executive Committee. It's a significant appointment. The more interesting signal is the decision-making process behind it.

How are you building the brief for inaugural AI leadership roles when there's no internal precedent?
