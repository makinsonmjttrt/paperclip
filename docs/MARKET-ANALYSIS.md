## Executive Summary

The single most commercially significant finding: no recruitment or executive search firm globally operates with credibility at the intersection of AI leadership and creative production. The competitive vacuum is confirmed across all seven research sources reviewed. This is a category creation opportunity, not a niche market entry.

FourPointZero is positioned to occupy genuinely uncontested territory — but only if it builds the category before competitors recognise the gap. The window will not stay open indefinitely.

## Target Market

### Primary Clients

**Who they are:** Organisations hiring senior AI leadership in creative production — VFX studios, media owners, creative software companies, AI-native platforms, and agency networks.

**The problem they face:** The role they need to fill spans two separate talent markets simultaneously: AI/ML technical depth and creative production credibility. Their internal TA team can access one pool. VFX-specialist recruiters can access the other. Nobody currently bridges both. This is the structural break point where retained executive search becomes commercially necessary.

## Risks

### Market Risks

**The retained search buying habit is unproven at this level in creative production.** The sector defaults to network hiring for senior roles. Most appointments at mid-market VFX studios and agencies happen via SIGGRAPH relationships, internal TA, or direct outreach — not retained search. The structural break point (cross-market role) is real, but FPZ must educate the market that retained search is the right solution, not simply sell it.

*Mitigation:* Container search model (small upfront plus success component) lowers financial risk for first-time retained buyers while demonstrating process rigour. Price the engagement fee at £5,000–£8,000 for first mandates.

**"Not publicly credited" is not the same as "not used."** Most corporate press releases do not credit search firms even when retained search was engaged (confidentiality norms). The market may have more retained search buying behaviour than visible evidence suggests.

*Mitigation:* Conduct a 20–30 person "route-to-hire" study (anonymised) to establish actual channel data. Research first, then use findings in BD.

**AI capability is often built through M&A and internal elevation, not external hiring.** DNEG's Brahma platform (Metaphysic acquisition) and the WPP Elevate28 restructure are examples of AI capability being built structurally rather than through a stream of CAIO-style placements. This limits the addressable mandate flow in the near term.

*Mitigation:* Position for hybrid engagements — talent mapping, advisory, succession planning, and confidential search — not just placement delivery. This is how Artico Search built authority before it had consistent mandate flow.

### Timing Risks

**The competitive vacuum will close.** The Talent Business has the infrastructure to enter this space and would take 2–3 years to build credible production networks. True Search could pivot its AI practice. A well-funded new boutique could appear. First-mover advantage in thought leadership and candidate mapping is the primary defence — it must be built now.

**US market represents the majority of fee potential but is harder to enter.** A single US VP-level AI production placement generates fees comparable to 3–4 UK placements at equivalent seniority. But US-based search firms (Riviera Partners, True Search) already have VC relationships with Runway, ElevenLabs, and Luma AI.

*Mitigation:* Lead with UK market, use UK placements as case studies, and target US opportunities where FPZ's creative production network is most differentiated.

**Inaugural roles are the richest mandates but have the longest sales cycles.** CAIO appointments at major studios are retained search triggers by definition — but they require executive-level trust and often arise from relationship depth built over 12–24 months.

*Mitigation:* Director-level Head of ML and Head of AI VFX roles at mid-tier companies (Foundry, SideFX, DNEG) are more immediately accessible and generate £30,000–£60,000 fees while building the track record required for C-suite mandates.

### Adoption Barriers

**Fee sensitivity at mid-market VFX studios is unproven.** The research establishes retained search norms (30–33% of first-year total comp) but cannot confirm whether a 100-person VFX studio will sign a £30,000–£50,000 retained engagement or default to contingent/internal hiring.

*Mitigation:* The 20 research conversations must include 3–5 conversations with actual hiring decision-makers at target companies — not just AI leader candidates. Ask: "When you last made a senior hire, how did you go about it? What would make you consider retained search?"

**Candidate pool thin at top tier.** Approximately 50–75 Tier 1 candidates globally can immediately fill the most senior roles. This is a feature (justifies retained fees) but also a delivery constraint — search methodology must be capability-based, not title-based.

*Mitigation:* Build the assessment framework for "AI shipped under real production constraints" rather than searching for "Head of AI" title holders. Tier 2 (75–125 globally) becomes accessible with the right evaluation capability.

**FPZ brand is known in creative production, not AI executive search.** There is no retained track record to cite. Clients making first-time retained search decisions need more reassurance than an established firm.

*Mitigation:* Whitby's principle applies: "Your case studies don't have to be from retained searches." Reframe 300+ contingency placements through a retained lens — client's business problem, search approach, commercial impact of the placed candidate. Container model lets clients experience process before committing to full retained.

## Recommendations

### Immediate (Days 1–30)

**1. Start the 20 research conversations now — they are the highest-leverage single activity available.**
The conversations simultaneously build the candidate network, generate proprietary market intelligence, test positioning language, produce the market briefing deliverable, and create the confidence of having spoken to 20 senior people in the niche before the first pitch. Sequence: conversations 1–5 with accessible Director-level contacts (calibration), 6–10 with VP-level at larger companies (intelligence deepening), 11–15 with C-suite and prominent leaders (authority), 16–20 with referral contacts and second-round calls (reinforcement). Use the PIR framework in each: Perspective, Intelligence, Referrals.

**2. Publish one article in VFX Voice or Campaign in the first 30 days.**
Cost: time only. The thought leadership podium is empty. A single credible article on "who leads AI transformation at a VFX studio" establishes a position that compounds. This is not a nice-to-have — it is the primary credibility mechanism in lieu of a retained track record.

**3. Build the Tier 1 candidate map using LinkedIn Recruiter.**
No other recruiter is systematically building this list. First-mover advantage in candidate mapping is significant. Target current holders of: Head of ML at VFX/creative companies; Head of AI at studios or agencies; Director AI VFX or GenAI at software companies; VP AI Production at media groups; CAIO at creative or entertainment companies.

**4. Identify the five highest-priority target client companies and begin bespoke BD outreach.**
Best immediate targets based on accessibility and fee potential: Foundry, SideFX, Maxon (creative SaaS — most accessible for a boutique, genuine AI leadership need), DNEG (VFX — confirmed AI investment, explicit standalone AI leadership role, most compelling use of FPZ's existing network), BBC Studios (media owner — UK-based, AI Creative Lab mandate, accessible to boutique).

### Short-Term (Days 31–60)

**5. Begin the "State of AI Leadership in Creative Production 2026" report.**
Conduct 10 interviews with senior leaders — mix of potential clients and candidates. Publish at the end of week 8. Every interviewee becomes both a candidate relationship and a client touchpoint. Post one excerpt per interview on LinkedIn. This is the Artico Search model applied to creative production: research first, mandates later.

**6. Target the first container mandate from BD conversations.**
Container model (£5,000–£8,000 engagement fee on commencement, full percentage rate, balance on placement, four-week exclusivity) is the right commercial entry. Target a creative SaaS company (Foundry, SideFX, or Maxon) where FPZ's creative production network is most credible.

### Medium-Term (Days 61–90+)

**7. Convert the first container placement to a full retained mandate.**
Use the placement result (proof of delivery), the proprietary candidate map (proof of access), and the market report (proof of domain authority) as the three pillars of the retained pitch.

**8. Develop the proprietary assessment framework before the first shortlist delivery.**
Build the competency framework for "AI leadership in creative production" from real market language gathered in the 20 research conversations. What does "translating creative vision to technical execution" mean at a VFX studio versus a creative technology agency? What competencies separate the best AI leaders from the mediocre ones? This framework is what separates a search firm from a recruiter.

**9. Resolve the solicitor-reviewed Retained Search Agreement before the first serious retained proposal.**
The 12-month replacement guarantee conditional on client onboarding compliance and the self-sourced hire clause (50–75% of fee payable if client fills the role through their own efforts during the engagement) are non-standard clauses that need professional drafting. This blocks signing, not pitching — but it must be ready before the first prospect conversation reaches the proposal stage.

## Open Questions Requiring Primary Research

The published research answers methodology, fee norms, objection handling, and market sizing. It cannot answer six questions that only conversations with real people in the target market will resolve:

1. **How do target companies actually make senior hiring decisions today?** Is it the CEO unilaterally, a CTO with budget authority, an HR function that gatekeeps, or a board threshold? This determines who to pitch to and how.

2. **What is the real compensation structure for AI leadership in creative production?** Not job posting ranges (aspirational). Actual base, bonus, equity packages for sitting Director/VP/C-suite AI leaders.

3. **What would make a mid-market CXO pay £30,000–£50,000 for a retained search?** Speed? Access to a pool they can't reach? The guarantee? The advisory relationship? The answer determines which WIIFM to lead with.

4. **What are the actual career motivators for senior AI leaders in creative production?** This intelligence is what clients can't get from LinkedIn — and it's why they pay retained fees.

5. **Where are the genuine skills gaps that create urgency for external hiring?** If every company can promote their CTO to cover AI, the market is much smaller. The specific competencies that cannot be developed internally define the addressable opportunity.

6. **What is the actual recruiter penetration rate — and why?** The gap closure research estimates 5–15% retained search penetration. But why? Cost, ideology, or simply no specialist firm has offered? The answer shapes the entire go-to-market.
