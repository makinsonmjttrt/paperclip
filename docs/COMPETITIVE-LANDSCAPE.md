## Overview

FourPointZero operates in a niche at the intersection of three converging markets: creative production recruitment, specialist tech staffing, and AI talent acquisition. No single competitor occupies the same position. The competitive risk comes from adjacent players drifting into FPZ's space as AI becomes mainstream in production pipelines.

FPZ's core differentiation is **senior leadership placement in creative technology** — specifically where AI, spatial computing, virtual production, and real-time workflows intersect. This document tracks the competitive landscape across three competitor tiers.

## Tier 1: Direct Competitors

These firms operate in overlapping niches — either immersive tech recruitment, VFX/production executive search, or AI/tech creative staffing.

### Orbital People

**Website:** orbitalpeople.co.uk
**Founded:** 1997
**Offices:** UK (global placement)

**Positioning:** Specialist in XR, metaverse, virtual production, 3D animation, VFX, and projection mapping. Closest direct competitor to FPZ by sector coverage.

**Specialisms:** XR/AR/VR, Unity/Unreal developers, virtual production, VR technologists, product managers, 3D artists, creative directors.

**Strengths:**
- 25+ years in creative technology recruitment — one of the oldest specialist firms in the space
- Covers virtual production, XR, and animation under one roof — directly overlapping with FPZ
- Global multinational client relationships, award-winning creative agency networks
- Product manager and creative director placement suggests some leadership capability

**Weaknesses:**
- AI integration not prominent in visible positioning
- No dedicated AI practice equivalent to CreativAI
- Less visible public presence than major generalist recruiters
- Specific seniority focus (leadership vs. all levels) unclear

**Recent moves:** No significant public announcements as of April 2026.

**Pricing:** Not published.

**Competitive threat to FPZ:** High. Orbital People is the most direct sector competitor — covering XR, virtual production, and creative technology since before FPZ existed.

**FPZ differentiation:** CreativAI practice, explicit AI pipeline focus, and stronger leadership/C-suite positioning.

## Tier 4: Platform Competitors and Structural Threats

### YunoJuno

**Website:** yunojuno.com

**Positioning:** Leading UK freelance management system (FMS) + marketplace. Not a recruiter — a platform.

**Clients:** Google, BBC, IBM, Accenture, Ogilvy, Adam&EveDDB

**Fee model:** 12% employer fee vs. 20% industry average — significant price undercut on contract briefs.

**Strengths:**
- 100,000+ vetted freelance contractors (70,000+ active)
- AI-driven job matching
- 261,000+ contract data points — strong market intelligence
- Profitable (Q1 2025), recognised as Leader in Everest Group 2025 FEMS PEAK Matrix
- Average day rates now £390/day (£49/hr) — premium market signal

**Weaknesses:**
- Freelance/contract only — not executive or permanent placement
- Platform model, not relationship-led
- Creative tech specialism not visible

**Competitive threat to FPZ:** Medium for contract briefs where clients self-serve. Low for senior permanent leadership.

**FPZ differentiation:** Relationship-led, leadership-focused, specialist vetting.

---

### In-house talent acquisition teams

A structural threat as larger studios and agencies build internal TA functions. Key signals to monitor:
- VFX studios (e.g., DNEG, Framestore, ILM) hiring in-house TA leads
- Technology companies (Epic Games, Unity) building internal creative talent teams
- Agencies with TA investment reducing agency dependency

FPZ's counter-position: Senior and niche leadership roles that in-house teams cannot source alone. Retained search model where ongoing relationship creates switching costs.

---

### Generalist tech agencies expanding into AI

Agencies like Hays, Harvey Nash, and Robert Half are building AI recruitment desks. These firms:
- Have scale but lack creative production knowledge
- Are competing for AI title recognition, not expertise
- Represent volume threat rather than quality threat for FPZ's senior mandates

## Market Pricing Context

Understanding fee norms matters for FPZ's commercial positioning.

| Placement type | Typical fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard permanent | 15–20% of first-year salary | Industry norm |
| Senior/specialist permanent | 20–30%+ | Expected for FPZ mandates |
| Contract (platform, e.g. YunoJuno) | 12% | Disruptive — price signal for clients comparing options |
| Contract (agency) | 15–25% on day rate | Standard agency rate |
| Executive retained search | 25–33% (split across search stages) | Premium retained model |

**Market demand signal (2025):** UK job adverts mentioning "machine learning," "LLM," or "gen-AI" grew +57% year-on-year in Q1 2025. VFX UK tax credit is 29.25% with no cost cap — supporting studio hiring investment.

## Key Differentiators for FPZ

1. **Production-native expertise** — consultants understand pipelines, not just job titles
2. **Leadership focus** — C-suite and senior placement only
3. **AI pipeline integration** — not just "AI jobs" but roles where AI has entered the production workflow
4. **CreativAI practice** — dedicated AI recruitment arm with specific positioning
5. **Spatial computing + XR + virtual production combined** — no competitor covers all three under one roof
