# CreativAI Discovery Call Talk Track
*30-minute first meeting guide — Martyn Makinson, FourPointZero*

**Before the call:** Review the company's recent LinkedIn activity and any notes in `bd-pipeline.md`. Know one specific thing they're doing that connects to AI-integrated production.

---

## Opening — 2 minutes

**Goal:** Set the pace. Establish that this is a peer conversation, not a pitch.

> "Thanks for making time. I'll keep this to thirty minutes — I'd rather hear about your situation than fill the air.
>
> I'll give you a quick sixty seconds on CreativAI, then I want to spend most of the time understanding what's actually happening on your side. Does that work?"

*[Wait for confirmation.]*

> "CreativAI is FourPointZero's executive search practice for the AI-creative intersection. We place the senior operators who run AI-integrated production at VFX studios, experiential agencies, and spatial computing companies. We've been building this network since 2019 — the talent exists, but it's not visible to generalist recruiters or sector specialists working one side of the boundary.
>
> That's the sixty seconds. Tell me what's going on with you."

**Why this works:** Handing them the floor immediately signals confidence and genuine curiosity. It prevents you defaulting to a pitch they've already heard from three other recruiters this month.

---

## Discovery — 15 minutes

**Goal:** Understand their current state, what they've tried, where the pain is, and whether timing and budget are real.

Work through these five areas. Not all as direct questions — let the conversation guide the order. Annotated purpose beside each.

---

### Q1 — Current hiring situation
**Purpose:** Understand if there's an active mandate or you're seeding a future relationship.

> "Walk me through where you are with AI leadership at the moment. Are you building something out, replacing someone, or is this more of a 'we know we'll need this eventually' conversation?"

*[Listen for: specific role, seniority level, urgency signals, whether they've started a search.]*

**Probe if vague:**
> "Is this a live brief or more directional at this stage?"

---

### Q2 — What they've tried before
**Purpose:** Identify which alternatives have failed them and why. This tells you where your pitch lands.

> "Have you gone to market for this kind of role before? If so, what happened?"

*[Listen for: generalist recruiter frustration ("they sent completely wrong CVs"), LinkedIn sourcing attempts, long time-to-fill.]*

**Probe if yes:**
> "What made those shortlists not work — was it sector knowledge, AI depth, or something else?"

---

### Q3 — Where the pain is
**Purpose:** Identify whether their priority is speed, quality, or network access. Your positioning shifts depending on their answer.

> "If you think about what would actually make a difference here — is it speed of finding someone, the quality of the shortlist, or access to people who aren't on the open market?"

*[Listen for the primary driver. Most say all three — push for which one is causing the most pressure right now.]*

**Probe:**
> "Of those three, which one is costing you most at the moment?"

---

### Q4 — Decision timeline
**Purpose:** Qualify whether this is a real near-term opportunity or a relationship to develop over twelve months.

> "What does your timeline look like? Is there a point at which a role going unfilled becomes a real problem for you?"

*[Listen for: project start dates, board pressure, departing incumbent, budget cycle.]*

**If vague:**
> "Is this a Q2 thing, a Q3 thing, or something that's more back-of-mind for now?"

---

### Q5 — Budget signals (without asking directly)
**Purpose:** Understand whether the role is at a level where your fee is proportionate. Don't ask the budget question directly.

> "What level are we talking about — is this a director-level mandate, VP, C-suite?"

*[Listen for: seniority signals. Director and above at £150K+ total comp is your target. Below that, the economics don't work for either party.]*

**If seniority is right, follow with:**
> "And is this a retained search or have you been exploring contingency options?"

*[Retained = serious mandate. Contingency = still exploring. Both are fine at this stage, but useful to know.]*

---

## Positioning — 8 minutes

**Goal:** Map what you heard directly to CreativAI's value. No generic pitch. Reference their specific pain.

**Delivery framework:** Lead with what you heard, not with what you do.

> "Based on what you've described — [reference their specific pain: e.g., 'you've had shortlists that looked credible on paper but missed the production delivery piece'] — this is exactly the problem we were set up to solve."

Then select 1-2 proof points from the list below that match their situation:

---

**Proof point A — Network depth (use when: they've struggled to find candidates at all)**

> "The people we're placing aren't on job boards and they're not responding to InMail. They're senior operators in live roles at ILM, DNEG, Framestore, Adobe, the Foundry. We've been building those relationships since 2019. That network took six years. An AI recruiter who's pivoted to cover creative production can't replicate it."

---

**Proof point B — Both-sides assessment (use when: they've had shortlists that looked right but didn't hold up)**

> "We work both sides — client-side and candidate-side. So when we shortlist, we already know which candidates would move, what they're worth, and whether their AI delivery experience is real or theoretical. We find that out before the first interview, not in the third."

---

**Proof point C — Not an AI recruiter (use when: they mention they've also talked to Digitalent, Xcede, or similar)**

> "There's a distinction worth making. AI tech recruiters — Digitalent, DeepRec — place ML engineers and AI technical leaders in technology companies. The people we place are production leaders: people who've run pipelines, managed crews, delivered under production pressure, and understand where AI enters that workflow. They're different candidates. Most AI recruiters have never worked a VFX or experiential brief in their lives."

---

**Proof point D — Sector embeddedness (use when: they care about intelligence, not just filling roles)**

> "We're APSCO, VRARA, and AIXR members. We publish a weekly industry newsletter with active readership in this space. We see career moves and studio announcements before they reach the public domain. That matters when the candidate you want is currently three months from being available but not publicly looking yet."

---

**After the proof point(s):**

> "Does any of that connect with what you're dealing with?"

*[Pause. Let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]*

---

## Close — 5 minutes

**Goal:** Agree a specific next step. Leave with either a live brief, a warm relationship, or permission to send the one-pager.

---

### If they're interested

> "The obvious next step would be for me to get a brief from you — either now if you want to talk through the role, or I can send over a short brief template you can fill in on your end. From there I can tell you quickly how deep our coverage is and give you a realistic sense of timeline.
>
> Which works better for you?"

*[Commit to a specific action before ending the call.]*

---

### If they're interested but not ready

> "Understood — timing isn't always right. The most useful thing I can do right now is send you the CreativAI one-pager. It's one page — gives you the clearest picture of what we do and where we sit in the market. That way you've got it when the right moment comes.
>
> Happy for me to do that?"

*[Always leave with the one-pager sent or confirmed. That keeps the relationship warm without asking for anything.]*

---

### If it's clearly not the right time

> "That's useful to know. I'm not going anywhere — I'll be building the candidate map in your space regardless. If anything worth sharing comes up on the AI leadership side, I'll flag it. No agenda beyond that.
>
> Is it worth staying connected?"

*[Mark SOFT in the BD tracker. Set a 6-week re-engagement reminder.]*
