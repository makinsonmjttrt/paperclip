### Friday 24 April — D+7: AI Pipeline TD (Role Education)

**Theme:** The hardest role to hire, least understood
**~330 words | Ends with soft offer | Education pillar**
**Post at 9am BST**

*Source: `workspace/creativai-launch-dplus7-post.md` — copy from that file.*

---

There's a role coming up in creative production briefs that most people outside the industry have never heard of. AI Pipeline Technical Director. It's one of the hardest to fill and, honestly, one of the least understood, even by the studios trying to hire one.

What does the role actually do?

An AI Pipeline TD sits between production and AI tooling. Their job is to make AI work inside a real production pipeline. Not in a demo or a prototype. Under actual delivery pressure, with a team who need to hit a brief, not run experiments.

That means knowing where AI tools break down in production conditions, where they cause downstream problems for compositing, delivery, or brand compliance, and how to bring them in without wrecking what already works.

It's not purely an engineering role. The AI Pipeline TD needs enough creative instinct to understand what the team is trying to achieve and enough technical grounding to know whether the tool will hold up when it matters. Most candidates are strong on one side. Very few have both.

So why is it so hard to hire?

The role is genuinely new. There's no established path that produces candidates for it. People come from VFX, post-production supervision, pipeline engineering, machine learning. Their CVs look nothing alike, and standard screening misses most of them.

Studios often write the brief before deciding what the role actually needs to do. They know they need to do something with AI in production. Whether they need someone to build the tooling, run it, or both, that's often still being worked out. That ambiguity makes the brief hard to write and hard to match.

The strongest people aren't looking. They're employed, doing adjacent work that's not quite this role but close enough to keep them interested. Finding them means knowing who they are, not waiting for them to apply.

This is what CreativAI was built for. Six years of relationships with the people on both sides of this brief.

If you're trying to hire an AI Pipeline TD, or still figuring out what the brief should say, happy to share what we're seeing.

### What to measure (the 3 that matter for FPZ)

Ignore raw impressions as a success metric. These three tell you whether the post is working for the business:

1. **Profile views generated** — the clearest signal that someone was curious enough to investigate you. Check your profile analytics 24 hours after posting and note the spike. This is the conversion bridge between content and enquiry.

2. **Comments from relevant accounts** — a single comment from a Head of Production or Creative Director at a target studio is worth more than 50 reactions from people outside your market. Note who commented, not just how many.

3. **Connection requests received** — new people adding you after a post are entering the pipeline. Note any that match your ICP (studio heads, AI creative leads, senior practitioners).

Reactions and impressions go in the table for pattern-spotting over time. They are not the primary measure of success.

---

### When to check

**24 hours after posting.** LinkedIn distributes most organic reach within the first 18–24 hours. Check once at that point and fill in the log. Checking sooner inflates anxiety, checking later misses the spike.

---

### What good looks like

These are directional benchmarks for an account in the 500–1,500 follower range. Not industry data — calibrated for a boutique specialist firm posting thought leadership, not a media brand.

| Metric | Decent | Strong |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Impressions | 200–500 | 500+ |
| Reactions | 5–15 | 15+ |
| Comments | 1–3 | 3+ or 1 from ICP |
| Follows gained | 1–3 | 4+ |
| Profile views spike | +5–15 vs daily baseline | +15+ |

A post hitting "decent" on all five is a good post. A post hitting "strong" on profile views or one quality comment from a client prospect has done its job regardless of other numbers.

---

### If a post underperforms

Two things to try on the next post:

1. **Rewrite the hook.** The first line determines whether LinkedIn shows the post at all and whether anyone clicks "see more." If impressions are low, the algorithm did not amplify it. If impressions were fine but engagement was low, the hook did not earn the click. Either way, the next post should open with a more direct observation, stronger opinion, or concrete fact — not a question.

2. **End with a prompt.** Posts that invite a specific response ("What are you seeing in your studio?" / "Curious if others are navigating this.") outperform posts that close neutrally. Add one genuine question on the next post and see if comments increase.
