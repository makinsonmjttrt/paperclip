# Candidate Talent Pool: AI Creative Tech Executive Roles
**FourPointZero | CreativAI Practice | April 2026**

---

## Purpose

This document profiles the candidate supply side for senior AI creative tech roles FPZ places through the CreativAI practice. It feeds BD conversations and practice positioning. All market availability assessments use directional language only — no invented statistics.

---

## Four Role Archetypes

---

### Archetype 1: Head of AI Production

**What this person does**

Senior operational and strategic leadership over AI-integrated production workflows. They own how AI tools enter the pipeline at studio or agency level, govern quality and output standards, and bridge creative vision with technical implementation. They often hold P&L accountability or direct pipeline ownership.

Active title variants in the UK market: Head of AI Production, AI Production Director, Head of AI Creative Lab, VP of AI Production, AI Pre-Production Supervisor. At larger organisations, Global Head of AI Workflows.

**What defines the ideal candidate**

- Proven delivery experience across full production pipelines — not just familiarity with tools, but shipped productions
- Ability to evaluate, select, and integrate generative AI tools into existing workflows (ComfyUI, Runway, Pika, Sora, Adobe Firefly, custom model fine-tuning)
- Cross-functional authority: can direct technical engineers and creative directors simultaneously
- Budget and vendor management experience
- Risk awareness around AI output quality, IP, and usage rights

**Where they typically come from**

Three dominant career routes:

1. Senior producers or EPs at major VFX houses (DNEG, Framestore, MPC, ILM) who spent the last two to three years building internal AI workflows before the market caught up with the title
2. Creative technologists at advertising and experience agencies who grew from "innovation lab" roles into full production ownership
3. Heads of Production at XR and experiential studios who moved into AI integration as projects shifted from game engine pipelines to hybrid AI/real-time workflows

**Market availability**

This is the most constrained archetype in the UK market. The intersection of production delivery experience and genuine AI tool fluency narrows the pool considerably. Big tech companies — Meta in particular — have been pulling experienced production operators out of the studio ecosystem for AI training work. The 2023-2024 VFX industry contraction (DNEG Montreal, Framestore Vancouver) displaced some senior people who moved quickly into the available roles; replenishment is slow.

**LinkedIn titles to search**

Head of AI Production, AI Production Lead, Head of AI Creative Lab, VP Production (AI), AI Workflow Lead, Head of Generative AI Production, Senior Creative Technologist (AI)

### Archetype 2: AI Pipeline Technical Director

**What this person does**

Deeply technical role responsible for designing, building, and maintaining the AI components of a studio's production pipeline. Distinct from a traditional Pipeline TD: the work involves integrating ML models, managing inference infrastructure, building custom AI tooling, and connecting disparate AI services into coherent artist-facing workflows.

Active title variants: AI Pipeline TD, AI Pipeline & Tools Engineer, Gen AI Workflow Designer, ML Pipeline Engineer, R&D Pipeline Engineer (AI), Senior ML Software Developer.

**What defines the ideal candidate**

Technical foundation (non-negotiable):
- Python fluency; C++ familiarity common at senior level
- ML frameworks: PyTorch is the dominant studio standard
- Diffusion model architecture knowledge (Stable Diffusion variants, ControlNet, LoRA fine-tuning)
- Cloud infrastructure: AWS or GCP; containerisation (Docker, Kubernetes)
- Deep pipeline tool familiarity: Houdini, Nuke, Maya; USD (Universal Scene Description) increasingly expected
- Version control and CI/CD practices applied to ML model deployment

Creative-technical bridge (differentiating):
- Understands how artists actually work and what friction they experience
- Translates artistic requirements into technical specifications
- Communicates pipeline constraints in language non-technical creative leads understand

ScreenSkills confirms Pipeline TD is not an entry-level path — it requires demonstrated VFX pipeline experience, not just engineering credentials.

**Where they typically come from**

Four clear entry routes:

1. Traditional VFX Pipeline TDs who self-trained in ML and began integrating AI tooling into existing pipelines — the most common transition path at senior level
2. Games engine and real-time pipeline engineers (Unreal, Unity toolchains) who moved into production studio contexts
3. R&D programmers from major VFX studios (ILM, Pixar, DNEG R&D teams) who have been working on AI tooling before commercial generative AI existed
4. CS or ML postgraduates who entered studios through junior TD roles and built up seniority

**Market availability**

Arguably the single most scarce profile in the UK creative tech market. The candidate needs simultaneous competence as an ML engineer, deep fluency in VFX and production software stacks, and genuine pipeline delivery experience. Big tech competition — Meta's documented strategy of hiring VFX technical talent for AI training — has pulled from this pool most acutely. Entry-to-mid level candidates are more available as ML programmes produce graduates; senior engineers with production-grade creative application experience are a different matter.

**LinkedIn titles to search**

Pipeline TD, Pipeline Technical Director, AI Pipeline Engineer, ML Engineer (VFX), Gen AI Workflow Designer, R&D Software Engineer (VFX), Pipeline Tools Developer, ML Infrastructure Engineer, Generative AI Engineer

### Archetype 3: Generative AI Creative Director

**What this person does**

Creative authority on AI-generated or AI-assisted output. Unlike a traditional CD, this person has a working technical vocabulary — they can direct an AI system, not just direct human artists. They understand model behaviour, prompt engineering at a sophisticated level, fine-tuning concepts, and the aesthetic language of different generative systems.

Active title variants in the UK market: Generative AI Creative Director, AI Creative Director, Head of AI Creative, AI Creative Lead (Art Director), Creative AI Lead. Jellyfish ran an "AI Creative Lead (Art Director)" posting in London — this is a live active title category.

**What defines the ideal candidate**

Creative foundation:
- Strong art direction or visual design background with a portfolio that demonstrates taste, not just tool use
- Experience directing human creative teams and translating briefs
- Knowledge of visual production formats (film, advertising, experiential, real-time)

AI-specific skills:
- Deep prompt engineering fluency across multiple model families (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion variants, Runway, Sora, Adobe Firefly)
- Image-to-image, video generation, and inpainting workflows
- Ability to fine-tune models on brand or project-specific training data
- Understanding of how to maintain brand and creative consistency across AI-generated output
- Comfortable with ComfyUI or similar node-based workflow tools

**Where they typically come from**

This archetype is newer and less likely to have a linear career path:

1. Senior art directors or CDs at advertising, design, or experiential agencies who actively adopted AI tools from 2022 onwards and built visible portfolios
2. Motion designers and 3D artists who developed generative practices and grew into creative leadership
3. Independent AI artists and practitioners with public reputations in the creative AI community (conference speakers, NeurIPS Creativity Workshop participants, gallery exhibitors) transitioning into commercial roles
4. Game art directors who brought procedural and real-time creative direction skills into AI-adjacent work

**Market availability**

More candidates exist here than in the technical archetypes, but genuine creative authority combined with AI fluency is still uncommon. Many people in this space hold the title without the portfolio to back it. The creative AI community in the UK is relatively tight — Luba Elliott's Creative AI London meetup (running since 2016) represents the networking hub where genuine practitioners congregate. The challenge is distinguishing real creative AI authority from surface-level adoption (see AI-native vs AI-curious signals below).

**LinkedIn titles to search**

Generative AI Creative Director, AI Creative Director, Head of AI Creative, Creative AI Lead, AI Art Director, AI Creative Lead, Head of Generative AI, Creative Director (Generative AI)

Note: add `NOT ("marketing" OR "social media" OR "content creation")` to exclude the large volume of marketing-side "AI Creative Director" roles that bear no relation to production.

### Archetype 4: AI/ML Engineer for Creative Applications

**What this person does**

Engineering role that sits closer to creative production than a standard ML position. Responsible for building and deploying AI systems that artists and creatives directly use — custom image generation pipelines, video synthesis tools, real-time AI processing for live events, or fine-tuned models for specific production tasks.

Active title variants: AI/ML Engineer (Creative Applications), Applied ML Engineer, Generative AI Engineer, AI Software Engineer (VFX/Creative), Technical AI Artist, Principal Tech Artist (AI). Meta has publicly posted a "Principal Tech Artist" role specifically for AI work.

**What defines the ideal candidate**

Technical core:
- Python; comfort with lower-level languages (C++, CUDA) for performance-sensitive work
- PyTorch or JAX for model development and fine-tuning
- Diffusion models, GANs, video generation architectures
- Real-time inference optimisation (running models at interactive frame rates for live or interactive contexts)
- REST APIs, model serving infrastructure, cloud deployment
- Familiarity with OpenUSD, game engine pipeline development (Unreal/Unity plugins)

Creative interface:
- Can work directly with artists to understand requirements
- Has aesthetic sensibility — understands what "good" output looks like in a production context
- Often has a portfolio of creative AI work in addition to technical credentials

**Where they typically come from**

1. ML engineers from big tech seeking more creatively stimulating applications who are drawn to studio/agency culture
2. Technical artists from games who transitioned from procedural tools into ML-based approaches
3. PhD researchers in computer vision or generative models who moved into industry via studios or AI-first production companies (Wonder Dynamics/Autodesk Flow Studio is an archetypal example)
4. Self-taught engineers with strong open-source contributions to the Stable Diffusion ecosystem, ComfyUI, or Hugging Face who built their way into recognition

**Market availability**

The broadest pool of the four archetypes, but still characterised by significant competition from non-creative-sector employers. ML engineers with creative sector experience command a premium. Senior engineers with production-grade creative application experience — as distinct from entry-to-mid level candidates from ML programmes — are a different and smaller pool.

**LinkedIn titles to search**

AI/ML Engineer, Generative AI Engineer, Applied ML Engineer, Technical AI Artist, ML Software Developer, AI Software Engineer, Generative AI Developer, Principal Tech Artist

## LinkedIn Search Strategy

### Core approach

Standard title search misses many strong candidates because people in this space often hold legacy titles while doing genuinely AI-forward work. Layer title search with skills-based search and employer-based search.

### Boolean search strings by archetype

**Head of AI Production:**
```
("Head of AI" OR "AI Production" OR "AI Creative Lab" OR "generative AI" OR "AI workflow") AND ("production" OR "VFX" OR "director" OR "lead") AND ("studio" OR "agency" OR "experiential")
```

**AI Pipeline TD:**
```
("pipeline" OR "Pipeline TD" OR "Pipeline Technical Director") AND ("AI" OR "machine learning" OR "ML" OR "generative") AND ("VFX" OR "Houdini" OR "Nuke" OR "Unreal")
```

**Generative AI Creative Director:**
```
("generative AI" OR "AI creative" OR "creative AI") AND ("director" OR "lead" OR "head") NOT ("marketing" OR "social media" OR "content creation")
```

**AI/ML Engineer Creative:**
```
("ML engineer" OR "AI engineer" OR "generative AI engineer" OR "applied ML") AND ("VFX" OR "creative" OR "real-time" OR "experiential" OR "games" OR "studio")
```

### Key filters

- **Geography:** UK first (London, Manchester, Bristol, Edinburgh — the main VFX clusters). Expand to global for passive or relocatable candidates.
- **Employer filters:** Search by company — DNEG, Framestore, ILM, MPC, Weta Digital, Passion Pictures, Electric Theatre Collective, Nexus Studios, MediaMonks, Imagination, Jack Morton, Holovis, Preloaded, Marshmallow Laser Feast, Brahma AI, Autodesk Flow Studio
- **Skills filters (LinkedIn Recruiter):** Python, PyTorch, Stable Diffusion, ComfyUI, Houdini, Unreal Engine, USD

### Alternative sourcing beyond LinkedIn

- **GitHub:** Search active contributors to Stable Diffusion, ComfyUI, and InvokeAI repos — active contributors are often practitioners first
- **Hugging Face:** Model authors and Space creators with VFX or production context in their profiles
- **The Pipeline Conference** speaker archive (thepipelineconference.com)
- **NeurIPS Creativity and Design Workshop** papers and participant lists
- **SIGGRAPH Production Sessions** presenter lists

## AI-Native vs AI-Curious: Key Signals

The challenge: generative AI became mainstream enough in 2022-2023 that adding "AI" keywords became standard CV hygiene rather than a meaningful signal. A candidate who attended one workshop and lists Midjourney as a skill is not the same as someone who has been building AI workflows since 2020.

### AI-native signals (genuine depth)

**Portfolio evidence:**
- Work samples that could only have been produced with deep model knowledge — consistent style across generated assets, evidence of fine-tuned models, complex outputs integrating AI and traditional pipeline elements
- GitHub activity in generative AI repos predating the mainstream (pre-2023 activity is a meaningful signal)
- Public writing, talks, or tutorials demonstrating conceptual understanding rather than tool familiarity

**Career trajectory:**
- Involvement with creative AI community before it became commercially fashionable — NeurIPS Creativity Workshop, Creative AI London meetups, SIGGRAPH AI papers
- Roles at AI-first companies that were building with generative AI before it was mainstream: Metaphysic (now Brahma/DNEG), Wonder Dynamics (now Autodesk Flow Studio), Stability AI, Runway
- Evidence of building custom pipelines rather than using off-the-shelf tools

**Vocabulary:**
- AI-native candidates discuss model behaviour, fine-tuning, inference speed, latency, training data, LoRA weights, ControlNet conditioning
- AI-curious candidates discuss prompting, using Midjourney, ChatGPT for ideation

### AI-curious signals (surface adoption)

- AI skills added in a cluster in 2022-2023 with no supporting portfolio evidence
- "AI" appears only in the skills section, not woven through job descriptions with context
- No community participation: no conference talks, no open-source contributions, no public writing
- Tools listed are exclusively consumer-facing (ChatGPT, Midjourney, Canva AI) rather than pipeline tools
- Job descriptions describe using AI to speed up existing work rather than fundamentally changing the nature of the work

### The brief test (for first-round screening)

Ask candidates to describe a production problem they solved with AI that they could not have solved any other way. AI-native candidates have specific stories with technical detail. AI-curious candidates describe efficiency gains or inspiration workflows.

## Market Context

**Supply constraint is structural, not cyclical.** The candidate pool for all four archetypes is thin because the roles are new. You cannot have ten years of experience as a Generative AI Creative Director because the tools have not existed for ten years. The most credible candidates are those who came from adjacent disciplines and moved early.

**Big tech is a direct competitor for the same pool.** Meta's documented strategy of hiring VFX technical talent for AI training work — alongside Google, Adobe, and Autodesk building internal AI creative teams — means FPZ's studio and agency clients are competing against technology company salaries and brand cachet. This creates urgency (act before big tech absorbs the available pool) and a genuine placement lever (many creative practitioners prefer studio culture over tech company culture, and that preference can be identified and matched).

**The 2023-2024 VFX industry contraction created availability in the market.** DNEG's Montreal cuts and Framestore's Vancouver closure displaced experienced pipeline engineers and production leaders. Some were absorbed quickly; others remain available or working freelance and open to the right senior opportunity. This cohort is worth actively mapping.
