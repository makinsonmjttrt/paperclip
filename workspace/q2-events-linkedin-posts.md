## Post 1: NAB Show Final Day
**Date:** 22 April 2026
**Type:** Long-form
**Hook angle:** AI production tools vs. the people who run them

---

Every vendor at NAB is showing you an AI tool for production. The question studios are quietly asking: who actually runs it?

NAB 2026 is packed with AI workflow demos. Better colour grading. Faster transcription. Generative B-roll. Real-time compositing that used to take a week.

Studios are buying these tools. What they are not buying is the person who decides which tools enter the pipeline, what quality bar they are held to, and when a human needs to step in.

That is a leadership hire. And in creative production, it is one of the harder briefs on the market right now.

The role sits somewhere between technical director, head of post, and executive producer. Sometimes all three wrapped into one brief. Most studios are trying to retrofit it onto someone already stretched thin.

We are working on that exact hire across post-production and virtual production studios. The problem is never finding someone who has used AI tools. It is finding someone who has led teams through integration and kept delivery on schedule.

If you are scaling AI into your production workflow and working out who should own it internally, worth a conversation.

## Post 3: Post-FMX
**Date:** 9 or 10 May 2026
**Type:** Long-form
**Hook angle:** One specific insight from FMX conversations

---

Three conversations at FMX. Three studios. Same sentence each time.

"We know what we want. We just can't find anyone who has actually done it."

The titles they used were all slightly different. The brief was the same: someone senior enough to lead an AI-integrated production pipeline. Understands the craft well enough to set quality standards. Knows the technology well enough to decide which tools actually belong in the workflow.

Came up across real-time content production, VFX pipeline integration, animation studios retooling for AI-generated content.

The studios making real progress have all done the same thing: hired for this leadership role before the tools were mature. Before the workflow was decided. The human infrastructure came first.

Studios still in early stages are mostly waiting to see how the tools settle before they work out who should lead them.

The problem with that order: the person leading AI integration needs to shape how the tools get adopted, not inherit a workflow that was already locked before they arrived. That is a fundamentally harder brief. We see it regularly.

## Post 4: Post-MPTS
**Date:** 15 May 2026
**Type:** Long-form
**Hook angle:** VP talent gap is structural

---

MPTS confirmed what UK studio clients have been telling me for six months.

The virtual production talent gap is structural. Not a short-term shortage that resolves as the market adjusts. The number of VP stages opening across the UK has outpaced the pool of experienced supervisors who know how to run them.

VP stage experience does not transfer from traditional production in any straightforward way. The on-set dynamics are different. The pipeline is different. The mental model for running a production where creative direction and real-time rendering have to work together in the same moment takes time to build, and there is no shortcut.

The supervisors who have it are employed, mostly on multi-year projects. The training pipeline is catching up, but slowly.

Studio leads at MPTS said the same thing two ways: waiting months to fill the role, or hiring below experience level and absorbing the overhead.

A VP stage sitting idle while a production waits is an expensive problem. Most studios I spoke to do not have a clear plan for closing the gap over the next two or three years.

If you are building out VP capability and the talent market is thinner than you expected, I am talking to studios in exactly the same position.

## Post 6: Post-AI Summit London
**Date:** 12 June 2026
**Type:** Long-form
**Hook angle:** One concrete observation from the room

---

Two days at AI Summit London. 4,500 senior technology and business leaders from media, entertainment, and enterprise.

One thing I kept coming back to.

The gap between where companies think they are on AI adoption and where they actually are is wider in creative production than in most other sectors I heard discussed.

In most industries, AI adoption is largely about process automation and efficiency gains. You can measure output and benchmark it.

In creative production, the quality standard is not fixed. Output gets judged against creative intent, not a specification. And the person responsible for making that call is often absent from the AI integration plan entirely. Who decides when something is good enough and what happens when it is not.

The companies making real progress hired a creative leader with technology fluency before they needed one. Before the tools were even chosen.

The ones still in pilot mode are mostly working out the technology strategy first. The leadership question comes after.

It is the wrong order, and it kept coming up.

## Post 7: Cannes Lions AI Craft
**Date:** 23 June 2026
**Type:** Long-form
**Hook angle:** Who leads the human side of AI-generated award work

---

Cannes Lions added AI Craft as a subcategory this year. Good call. It also surfaces a question production studios have not fully answered.

When AI-generated creative work wins awards, who owns the quality decision?

Practically: who reviews the output? Who decides it is good enough to put in front of a client or a jury? Who is accountable when it falls short?

In traditional production, that sits with a creative director or executive producer. Someone with a track record the studio and client trust. Their judgement is the filter.

In AI-integrated production, that role is being built from scratch at most studios. A creative director who has learned to direct generative tools. A technical director who has developed a genuine creative sensibility. At some studios, a newly created role sitting between the two with no established title yet.

The studios winning at Cannes with AI Craft work have figured out who that person is. They have built the accountability structure around AI-generated output before they needed it.

Studios still in early stages are mostly focused on the toolchain. The leadership question comes later, and so does the quality consistency.

That is the brief we are fielding right now. Creative leadership in a studio where AI is in the workflow.

## Post 8: Post-Annecy MIFA
**Date:** 27 June 2026
**Type:** Long-form
**Hook angle:** The animation pipeline role with no consistent title

---

MIFA last week. Four days of business conversations with animation studios from across Europe.

The AI conversation in animation has moved on from "are we using these tools."

The question coming up in studio meetings:

Who is the senior person deciding which AI tools enter the pipeline, how they get integrated, and what quality standards apply?

At some studios, a technical director has taken that on. At others, it sits with a head of production managing technology oversight alongside everything else. I talked to one studio where the role had been specifically created for this purpose, under a title that does not match what any other studio calls it.

No consistent title exists yet. That creates two real problems.

Studios that need to hire for it have no clean search term. Candidates already doing the role are often underpaid because there is no market rate for a job that did not have a name twelve months ago.

This person is what makes AI integration actually work in an animation studio. A live hiring need at the studios I spoke to at MIFA, not a future-state aspiration.

We are mapping this talent pool now across European and international animation studios. If you are building this role, or you are already doing it somewhere, I would like to understand what the brief looks like.
