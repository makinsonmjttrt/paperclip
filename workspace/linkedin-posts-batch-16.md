## Post 1 — Two Weeks In: What CreativAI Has Actually Been

**Pillar: Brand / Reflection | Audience: Both | Schedule: Tue 29 Apr**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Observation-Pattern-Reflection-CTA**

---

Two weeks running CreativAI, and the pattern is not what I built for.

I assumed most conversations would be searches. A brief, a candidate pool, a shortlist.

Those conversations are happening.

What I didn't expect was how many would start before the brief exists.

Studios reaching out while they're still working out what the function should own. Founders who've decided they want senior AI production leadership but haven't finalised what year one looks like. Candidates asking whether a studio has genuinely thought through the mandate before they'd agree to a conversation.

The search is a smaller part of this than I thought.

The harder question — what does this role actually need to deliver, and is the organisation ready for someone to do that — arrives first.

That's not specific to AI roles. But it's more visible here.

Senior AI production talent have often been in under-resourced roles before. They ask structural questions early because they know what happens when the answers aren't clear.

Two weeks in: brief quality is as important as candidate quality.

Probably more so.

If you're working through what a senior AI production function should look like, that's the conversation I'm having this week.

P.S. The first two weeks were quieter on volume than I expected. Sharper on quality.

## Post 2 — The Gap Between AI Hype and Actual Hiring Demand

**Pillar: Market Intelligence | Client-facing | Audience: Studio leaders | Schedule: Wed 30 Apr**
**Hook type: Contrarian/Myth-Bust | Structure: Contrarian Rant → What the briefs actually say → Practical implication → CTA**

---

The narrative around AI in creative production has been consistent for three years.

Roles will disappear. Headcount will shrink. Studios will do more with fewer people.

That's not what I'm seeing in the briefs.

Studios running AI-integrated production pipelines are not cutting senior headcount. They're restructuring it.

The question coming through isn't "how do we replace this function with AI?" It's "who can lead a team that uses AI throughout the pipeline?"

That's a different problem. It requires a different kind of hire.

The director-level candidates studios need right now have run live delivery, managed crews, and navigated the pressure of a commercial brief with a hard deadline. AI fluency is one dimension of that profile. Production experience is the other.

Generalist AI talent with no production background can't do what these roles require. Senior production executives who've never worked with AI tools are equally limited.

The overlap between those two populations is small. It took years to build before this demand existed.

The hype said AI would reduce the need for this kind of hire.

The actual market is telling a different story.

P.S. If your brief has been producing thin shortlists, the population you're describing is smaller than the hype suggests.

## Post 3 — The VFX Role Changing Fastest Right Now

**Pillar: Market Intelligence | Technical/Specific | Audience: Studio leaders, VFX Supervisors, Heads of Production | Schedule: Thu 1 May**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Identify-the-shift → Why it's happening → Practical implication → Authority close**

---

The role under the most structural pressure in VFX right now is not what most people name.

Not the concept artist. Not the compositor. Not the VFX supervisor.

It's the Pipeline Technical Director.

Three years ago, this person owned the tools and scripts that held production together. The bridge between shot work and infrastructure. Deep Houdini, Python, familiar with Nuke and the broader DCC ecosystem.

That profile still exists. But the brief has shifted.

Studios building AI-integrated pipelines now need Pipeline TDs who understand where model inference fits in a shot workflow. Who can work alongside ML engineers without being one. Who understand what a generative step does to a render farm, a review process, a delivery schedule.

That's a different technical vocabulary layered on top of an existing one.

The candidates who have both are rare. They were mostly doing R&D work two years ago, when studios were in exploration mode.

They're mid-delivery now.

Finding them requires knowing where that R&D happened.

P.S. If you're building a pipeline team for an AI-integrated show, the brief needs to be more specific than "Python, Houdini, AI." The candidate that fits it is specific too.

## Post 4 — Why the Best Creative Technologists Are Still Found Through Networks

**Pillar: Brand / Authority | Positioning | Audience: Both | Schedule: Fri 2 May**
**Hook type: Contrarian/Myth-Bust | Structure: Challenge the assumption → Why it breaks in this market → What actually works → Authority close**

---

The assumption built into most hiring tools is that visibility equals access.

Put the role out. Reach the right people. Fill the pipeline.

In most of the market, that logic holds.

In specialist creative production, it breaks down at the point that matters.

The senior AI creative technologists in active demand right now are not checking job boards. They're mid-project. Some are fielding conversations from studios they already know. The few who'd consider a move aren't broadcasting that.

They're accessible through relationships, not algorithms.

The studios making the fastest hires in AI production leadership have one thing in common. They have a line into that population before the role exists, not after.

That line comes from years of presence. Events, placements, relationships built before this intersection of AI and creative production became commercially obvious.

The platforms that promise access to passive candidates are indexing public profiles. They're not in the room where that conversation happens.

FourPointZero has been in that room since 2019.

If you're building a brief for a senior AI creative role, the question worth asking first is where your shortlist is actually going to come from.

P.S. Reach without relationships is noise. Especially in a candidate pool this small.
