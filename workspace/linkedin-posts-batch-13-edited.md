**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration → Problem-Agitate-Solution structure**

---

Generalist recruitment agencies will tell you they can hire for your AI creative roles.

Some of them believe it.

The gap isn't effort. It's that they don't know the space.

When a generalist recruiter searches for an "AI Creative Director," they're matching keywords from other briefs. They don't know what the role actually needs to deliver. They don't know which studios are genuinely building AI production capability and which are running experiments. They don't know the real candidates, because that community doesn't surface to anyone outside the space.

So you get CVs that look right on paper. Candidates who arrive without understanding your workflow. Or people from adjacent disciplines who did one AI project and repositioned themselves as specialists.

The brief sits open for months. Interview cycles that don't convert.

You can't find these candidates with a keyword search. We've spent six years mapping senior talent at the intersection of creative production and emerging technology. When a brief comes in, the people we contact aren't strangers to us. Or to the work.

That changes how fast it moves. And how relevant the shortlist is.

What's the longest a specialist creative tech role has stayed open in your studio?

## Post 4 — The Hire That Actually Changed Things (From This Week's Conversations)

**Pillar: Market Intelligence | Authentic Insight | Audience: Both | Schedule: Fri 2 May**
**Hook type: Time-Stamped Origin Story → Personal Reflection CTA**

---

Something came up in three separate conversations this week that I wasn't expecting.

All three were with studios that have made multiple AI-related hires over the last 18 months.

All three said essentially the same thing.

The hire that changed things most wasn't the AI specialist.

It was the senior production leader they brought in later. The one who understood delivery. Who could hold the creative quality bar while the team worked out where the new tools fitted. Who could translate between the people running experiments and the clients who still needed the work to land.

The AI expertise created the opportunity. What made it usable was having someone who understood delivery, quality, and what clients would actually accept.

Studios building the fastest aren't necessarily the ones with the best AI hires. They're the ones who got the operational layer right around those hires.

I don't know if this pattern holds broadly. Three conversations isn't a trend. But I've heard it clearly enough now that it's worth saying out loud.

If you've seen something similar, I'd genuinely like to hear it.

What was the hire that actually changed how your AI work delivered?

P.S. It's also changing how I think about what role to prioritise when studios ask what to hire next. The answer isn't always another AI specialist.
