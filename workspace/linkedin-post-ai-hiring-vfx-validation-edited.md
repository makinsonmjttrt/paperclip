The most in-demand VFX hire of 2026 is already employed.

They're not on LinkedIn. They haven't updated their CV in three years. They're mid-show, probably fixing something that shouldn't have broken, and they're getting calls from two other studios and a tech company every month.

This is the profile studios keep asking me about: a senior technical artist or pipeline lead who has absorbed AI tools into their daily workflow without being told to. ComfyUI or Fooocus for concept exploration. AI-assisted roto and cleanup. Real-time AI inpainting in compositing.

The problem isn't finding them. The problem is competing for them.

Tech companies spotted this profile two years ago. They offer remote-first, equity, and an AI research budget. Most VFX studios are still offering the same package they offered in 2019.

The studios adapting right now are doing two things differently.

First, they're promoting from within. Taking the TD who's been quietly building AI tooling on personal projects and giving them a formal mandate, a title uplift, and budget to match.

Second, they're being honest about what the role actually is. Not "AI Supervisor" with a vague remit. A specific brief: own this part of the pipeline, build the tooling, train the team.

Both approaches move faster than waiting for the perfect external candidate to appear.

Are you building AI capability from within, or still holding out for the unicorn hire?

P.S. If you've got a senior technical person already experimenting with AI workflows and no formal structure around them, that conversation is worth having before someone else has it.
