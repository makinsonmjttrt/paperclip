## Format 2: Story version

### Context

A major UK visual effects studio had made a significant investment in AI production tooling across its pipeline. The technical infrastructure was in place. What was missing was someone to lead it: a Head of AI Production who could bridge the AI/ML engineering work and the creative floor, set the direction for how AI tools were adopted and evolved, and give the rest of the production team a single point of accountability for AI decisions.

This was not a new category of hire for visual effects. But the way this studio wanted to approach it — integrating AI judgment into the leadership structure rather than treating it as a separate technical function — was unusual enough that previous searches had struggled.

### The challenge

The role had been open for four months before the studio engaged FourPointZero. In that time, they had briefed two generalist recruiters and spent significant internal resource on direct search.

The shortlists they had received shared the same flaw: strong AI/ML credentials, limited production context. Candidates who had worked in research environments or at technology companies, but had never managed an AI pipeline under actual VFX delivery conditions. People who understood the tools but hadn't developed judgment about when to use them.

The unspoken requirement, the one that hadn't made it clearly into the brief, was that this person needed to have experienced the specific pressure of AI integration in a production environment that doesn't tolerate failure. A candidate who had shipped generative AI features for a consumer app and a candidate who had run an AI workflow inside a complex VFX delivery pipeline are doing different jobs. The brief needed to reflect that.

### The FPZ approach

We started by challenging the brief. The existing job specification had been built around the technical depth that proved AI capability: ML experience, model familiarity, team management. What it didn't capture was the production credibility requirement. We added evaluation criteria around delivery track record in production contexts: AI shipped under deadline pressure, experience managing the failure modes of generative tools on complex creative pipelines, ability to assess AI output against client-driven creative standards.

This changed the candidate pool. The profiles we were looking for weren't responding to conventional search because they weren't looking in the same places as conventional candidates. They were passive, employed at studios and technology companies that were actively using their skills, and receiving outreach regularly. What made them respond was outreach that demonstrated understanding of their specific experience, not a generic description of the role.

We reached the placed candidate through a direct approach that referenced work they had done publicly on AI pipeline integration. The approach landed because it was specific: we knew what they had built, understood the constraints they had been working within, and could articulate clearly why this role was different from others they were likely receiving.

The placed candidate was not on any recruiter shortlist. They were not visible through the channels the studio had been using.

### The result

Shortlist delivered in three weeks. The studio interviewed all four shortlisted candidates. They made an offer to their first choice. The placed candidate is still in post 18 months later.
