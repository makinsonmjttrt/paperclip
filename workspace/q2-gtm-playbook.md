## 1. Target client segments: Q2 (three, prioritised)

**Priority 1: VFX studios with confirmed AI mandates**

DNEG, Framestore, Weta FX. These organisations have active AI programmes at board level, confirmed hiring signals, and the budget to commission retained search. They also have the most complex candidate requirements — AI/ML depth plus production credibility — which is precisely the brief FPZ is built to fill. This is the highest-fee, highest-alignment segment.

**Priority 2: Creative software platforms**

Foundry, SideFX, Maxon, Stability AI. All four are shipping AI into their products. All four need leaders who understand both the technology and the creative production community they serve. This segment is more transactional at CEO level. The right entry point is often CTO or CPO. The network relationships are strong and the sequences are ready to go.

**Priority 3: Broadcasters and media owners with AI mandates**

BBC Studios. One target in Q2. The union and editorial context makes the brief distinctive and the candidate pool rare, which is where FPZ adds disproportionate value. Do not spread further into this segment in Q2. One relationship well-developed outperforms three cold ones half-built.

## 2. Channel mix

**LinkedIn direct outreach carries Q2.** The sequences are written, the contacts are identified, the timing signals are live. Everything else in Q2 supports this channel. It does not compete with it.

**Events for depth, not breadth.** FMX in May is the only event in Q2 where relationship depth is available at scale. MPTS is useful for UK client visibility at low cost. AI Summit London is positioning, not pipeline. Annecy MIFA is early-stage relationship building with animation studios. None of these replace direct outreach. They accelerate conversations already started.

**Newsletter and content as signal amplifiers.** The weekly newsletter maintains presence with the broader network. It does not generate qualified pipeline in Q2. Do not allocate BD time to content creation at the expense of direct outreach.

Rough channel weighting for Martyn's BD hours in Q2:
- Direct LinkedIn outreach and follow-up: 60%
- Event preparation and attendance (FMX, MPTS): 30%
- Content review and newsletter: 10%

## 5. Biggest risk and mitigation

**Risk: Batch 1 pipeline sits unsent while the market signals age.**

The outreach sequences were built on live, named signals. The Griptape acquisition and the Cinema 4D MWC announcement are time-sensitive hooks. Every week they sit unsent, the "I noticed..." opener becomes less fresh. The sequences lose their most distinctive quality: specificity.

A secondary version of this risk: Martyn approves in principle but execution slips week by week as client delivery and candidate work take priority. Three weeks of delay turns a strong sequence into a generic one.

**Mitigation:** Treat Batch 1 send as a hard deadline, not a task. The sequences are ready. The checklist items are clear (DNEG warm intro check; LinkedIn connection check). Block two hours this week to complete both and send. Nothing in the Q2 playbook can move until Batch 1 is in market.
