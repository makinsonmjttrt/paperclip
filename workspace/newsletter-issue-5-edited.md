## Introduction

Issue 4 covered the signals that tell you an organisation has crossed from experimental to infrastructure hiring: role seniority up, remit now includes building a team, internal alignment happening before the search starts.

Flip the lens. What does that same market look like from the supply side?

The pattern, consistently across recent searches for AI creative leadership: the shortlist is short. Not because the brief is wrong. Because the pool of candidates with real production delivery experience is smaller than the demand, and most of them are not available.

---

## The pool is smaller than it looks

There are many people with AI on their CV. Fewer have AI production delivery on their CV. Fewer still have led a creative team through that process, built the workflows, and shipped something at scale.

The people with genuine experience, not AI enthusiasm and not SaaS product ownership, have typically been in role for 12 to 24 months. They moved early. They are building something. They are not on job boards and they are not responding to LinkedIn messages.

Candidates available at this level tend to be available for a reason. That reason is worth understanding before you advance them to first interview.

---

## Reading the available market

When a candidate at this level is actively on the market, ask yourself why. In a healthy talent market, senior people move on their own terms, toward something better. Active availability at this level sometimes means exactly that. Sometimes it means something else.

The searches consistently finding the strongest shortlists are not starting with the available market. They are accessing candidates who were not available to everyone else, because the relationship existed before the search started.

---

## The network is the asset

FourPointZero has been mapping this part of the market since before AI creative leadership existed as a formal job category. Those conversations, with the people now building and leading AI production workflows, began years before the roles existed.

A search that starts from scratch, using conventional sourcing, takes months to reach the same candidates. By that point, the best ones are often no longer available.

---

## Practical takeaway

If you are six months from needing an AI creative leader, start the conversation now. Not to place someone. To understand what is available, who is genuinely open to a move, and what that move would need to look like.

Market mapping costs nothing and creates a baseline for when urgency arrives. The organisations acting on that conversation ahead of time are the ones with the shortest shortlists.
