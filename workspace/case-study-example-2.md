## Format 2: Story version

### Context

A major streaming platform's UK production division had commissioned a permanent virtual production stage. The infrastructure was operational: the LED volume was built, the real-time render pipeline was configured, the facility was ready to shoot. What they lacked was someone to lead it as a production function rather than a technology experiment.

The role required fluency in two separate worlds. On one side: real-time production technology, Unreal Engine pipeline management, and the specific demands of shooting for an LED volume. On the other: the production rigour of a major broadcaster, scheduling complexity, union considerations, and the creative partnership with directors and cinematographers who were encountering the technology for the first time.

The platform had invested substantially in the facility. The right Head of Virtual Production would determine whether that investment became a genuine production capability or an expensive, underused asset.

### The challenge

The role had been open for three months before the client engaged FourPointZero. Two internal attempts at filling it had produced shortlists with the same structural gap: candidates who were strong on one side of the brief and not the other.

From the technology community, they had seen strong real-time rendering candidates with Unreal Engine depth and LED-wall operational experience. Most had come from games or broadcast tech backgrounds, but had limited experience running a production floor with union crews, managing delivery schedules to a streaming platform's quality standards, or working as a genuine creative partner to senior directors.

From the traditional production community, they had seen experienced production managers and associate producers who understood how to run a complex shoot. Most had no direct virtual production experience, and the transition learning curve was more than the role could absorb in its first six months.

The unspoken requirement was judgment: the ability to assess what the technology could deliver on a specific shooting day, manage expectations across engineering and creative departments simultaneously, and earn credibility on both sides. That profile didn't map cleanly to either talent pool, which is why two searches had stalled at the same point.

### The FPZ approach

We started with a candidate map rather than the job specification. The pool of people who had genuinely led virtual production as a production function, not just operated or supported it, was small and growing quickly. We identified three distinct groups: people who had run virtual production at major US studio facilities and were open to UK relocation; UK-based cinematographers and production supervisors who had retrained through significant VP productions; and a smaller group who had made a deliberate move from the games industry into broadcast and streaming production.

The right profile came from the third group. The placed candidate had led real-time production workflow at a UK games studio before transitioning to virtual production on a series of high-profile streaming productions, taking on increasing production responsibility alongside the technical role over a period of several years.

They were not on any recruiter's shortlist. They were not visible through the channels the platform had already used. FPZ's approach referenced their specific productions and the particular combination of real-time depth and production management experience they had built. That specificity opened the conversation.

### The result

Shortlist delivered in four weeks. The client interviewed three of the four shortlisted candidates. They made an offer to their first choice. The placed candidate is now building the virtual production function from within, with a second hire already underway.
