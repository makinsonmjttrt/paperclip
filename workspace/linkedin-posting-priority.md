## Week 1 (Apr 17–24): Launch Week

**Cadence:** Daily. Every post matters. Do not go quiet between launch and D+7.

**Theme:** Announce, reflect, educate. Each post should feel like a different facet of the same story — not repetition of the announcement.

### Posting protocol for launch week

- Post between 9–10am BST each day.
- Set a 60-minute reminder after posting. Reply to every comment that appears before the hour is up — even briefly. This is the window when LinkedIn's algorithm decides how far to distribute the post.
- Do not use hashtags. The FPZ audience responds to substance. Hashtags on thought leadership posts in this sector add noise, not reach.
- Tag 2–3 relevant people in your own first comment (not in the post body) within 30 minutes of publishing. See tagging guide below.

### Who to tag — launch week

Tag in your first comment, posted within 30 minutes of publishing. 2–3 people maximum per post. Only tag people you have spoken to or who know FPZ — cold tagging on a launch announcement reads as spam.

**D+0 Launch (Apr 17):**
- 1–2 people you have placed recently who are now in AI-adjacent production roles — natural social proof, they are likely to re-engage.
- 1 industry peer who has publicly supported FPZ before.

**D+5 Early Signal (Apr 22) — ends with a question:**
- 1–2 studio directors or production leads from your BD pipeline (see `workspace/bd-precall-briefs.md` for names). The question at the end gives them a reason to respond.
- Only tag people you have DM'd in the last 30 days.

**D+7 AI Pipeline TD (Apr 24):**
- 1 technical director or VFX pipeline lead from your network — this post is their world.
- 1 head of production at a studio you know is building AI pipeline capacity.

### Boost recommendation

**One post only. The D+0 launch post. Budget: £50–100 over 3–5 days.**

- Objective: Awareness (not lead gen — this is a brand introduction, not a form push).
- Audience targeting: UK + US, job titles Head of Production / Creative Director / Technical Supervisor / VFX Supervisor / Head of Technology, industry Film, TV, Advertising, Experiential.
- Run dates: Apr 17–21 only.
- Stop if engagement rate drops below 0.5%.

Do not boost the D+1, D+3, or narrative posts — paid reach on first-person reflection reads as artificial. Let thought leadership posts run on organic reach.

### Newsletter issue 7 and LinkedIn (approx. Apr 21)

Newsletter issue 7 sends around Apr 21. Use that timing window deliberately:

- The newsletter reaches subscribers directly. LinkedIn reaches the wider network. They serve different functions — do not cross-post newsletter content verbatim to LinkedIn.
- After the newsletter lands, check LinkedIn comments on your D+5 post (Apr 22). Newsletter readers who are also LinkedIn connections may show up in comments that week — the content themes align, so engagement often clusters.
- If someone replies to the newsletter with a substantive point, consider using it (anonymised if necessary) as a comment on your next LinkedIn post to seed discussion.

## Connection Request Surge Protocol

If D+0 or launch week drives a spike in connection requests:

**Accept vs. ignore:**
- Accept: studio leaders, heads of production, creative directors, pipeline supervisors, AI/tech leads at agencies or studios — anyone who matches your ICP
- Accept: anyone who comments on your launch post (they are already engaged)
- Ignore for now: recruiters at other firms, vendors, people with no apparent connection to creative production
- Ignore for now: accounts with no profile picture, generic job titles, or obvious sales automation accounts

**After accepting — what to do within 48 hours:**
- Do NOT send a pitch. Send a short human message acknowledging the connection. One or two sentences.
- Example: *"Good to connect — saw you engaged with the CreativAI post. Happy to share more context on what we're building whenever useful."*
- If they match your BD ICP (studio head, creative director at a named target account): flag in `workspace/bd-response-tracker.md` and begin BD Touch 1 sequence when appropriate (not on day of acceptance).

**Volume threshold — when to triage:**
- Under 10 requests: process same day.
- 10–30 requests: batch-process in one 15-minute window. Accept ICP matches first.
- Over 30 requests: work through the queue over 2 days. Prioritise anyone who commented or messaged.

## Newsletter Issue 7 — LinkedIn Announcement

**Send date:** ~Apr 21 (Tue). Do not delay past Apr 22.

**How to announce on the main feed:**
Do not cross-post newsletter content. The newsletter goes to subscribers — the LinkedIn post reaches people who have not subscribed yet. They serve different audiences.

Instead, post a short standalone teaser:
- 2–3 lines maximum. No "this week's newsletter" framing — that works for media brands, not for a specialist recruiter with 500–1,500 followers.
- Better framing: mention the core insight from Issue 7 as a standalone point, then close with something like: *"That's in this week's newsletter for subscribers — link in bio if you want it."*
- This functions as a subscriber acquisition touchpoint, not a content recap.

**Timing:** Post the newsletter teaser as a LinkedIn update at 9–10am on the send day or morning after. Not as a new primary post — use a short status update format (under 150 characters is fine). The primary posts for those days are on the existing schedule.

**Do not:** announce newsletter open rates, subscriber counts, or growth milestones publicly. That is internal data, not audience value.

## How to Respond to Comments (Engagement Protocol)

LinkedIn's algorithm measures early engagement velocity. Responding within the first hour pushes the post to more feeds.

**The protocol:**

1. Post at 9–10am BST.
2. Set a 60-minute reminder (launch week) or 4-hour reminder (consolidation/build weeks).
3. When you check back, reply to every comment — even briefly. Do not just react with a like.
4. Ask a follow-up question in your reply where it fits naturally.

**What to write:**

- If someone agrees: add one concrete point they did not mention. ("Yes — and the gap gets wider when the brief is written by someone who has only ever done one side of it.")
- If someone shares an experience: acknowledge it and ask a follow-up. ("That matches what I'm seeing. Is it the brief stage or the shortlist stage where you're hitting the same wall?")
- If someone challenges the post: engage directly. A respectful back-and-forth is the best organic reach multiplier available. Do not delete or ignore.

**Priority order:**

1. Studio directors, heads of production, creative directors — reply to these first.
2. Anyone from your BD contact list who shows up in comments — reply quickly, then DM the same day.
3. Other recruiters or agency owners — engage professionally.

**Engagement on others' posts (Weeks 3–4):**

From May 1 onwards, spend 10–15 minutes per week leaving substantive comments on 3–4 posts from studio heads, creative directors, or AI production leads in your network. Not "great post" reactions — observations that add to the conversation. This extends your reach beyond your own follower base without requiring new content. One thoughtful comment on a well-trafficked post can surface your profile to people you have not yet reached.

## Performance — What to Track

Check 24 hours after each post. Five minutes maximum.

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Profile views spike | Someone was curious enough to investigate. This is the conversion signal. |
| Comments from studio contacts | Quality beats quantity. One comment from a relevant studio contact outweighs 50 reactions from outside your market. |
| Connection requests | New people entering the pipeline — note if they match ICP (studio heads, AI production leads). |

**Directional benchmarks (500–1,500 follower account, specialist thought leadership):**

| Metric | Decent | Strong |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Impressions | 200–500 | 500+ |
| Reactions | 5–15 | 15+ |
| Comments | 1–3 | 3+ or 1 from ICP |
| Follows gained | 1–3 | 4+ |
| Profile views spike | +5–15 vs daily baseline | +15+ |

**D+0 launch post specific benchmark:** If it reaches 500+ impressions organically by end of day, the algorithm is distributing it. If under 200 by noon, the hook may be truncated — confirm the first two sentences are visible without clicking "see more."

**If a post underperforms:**

1. Rewrite the hook on the next post. Low impressions mean the algorithm did not amplify it. Low engagement despite good impressions means the hook did not earn the click.
2. End with a prompt. Posts that invite a specific response consistently outperform posts that close neutrally.
