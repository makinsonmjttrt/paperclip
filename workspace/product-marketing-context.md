## Product Overview

**One-liner:** The only executive search practice built from inside creative production, for the leaders navigating AI transformation of how creative work gets made.

**What it does:** FourPointZero places senior leadership talent at studios and agencies working at the intersection of creative production and emerging technology — spatial computing, real-time, experiential, and AI-integrated workflows. The CreativAI practice focuses specifically on AI-sector executive search: placing the leaders responsible for integrating AI into production pipelines at VFX studios, media owners, creative software companies, and AI-native platforms.

**Product category:** Specialist executive search and senior recruitment

**Product type:** Service — retained and contingency placement, with a growing retained search model for senior and C-suite mandates

**Business model:** Retained executive search (25–33% of first-year base salary, staged in thirds). Container search model for first-time retained clients (small engagement fee plus success rate). Contingency for select mandates. Minimum fee floor: £24,000. Anchor rate: 30%. Fee ranges: UK Director/VP level £27,500–£90,000; US Director/VP level $45,000–$165,000 per placement.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Creative production companies need to hire senior AI leaders who understand both the AI/ML technical depth AND creative production workflows, pipelines, and culture. This cross-market role exists at an intersection that internal TA teams and specialist recruiters on either side cannot bridge. The result: roles sit open for months, or are filled with candidates who are strong on one dimension only.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- **Generalist recruiters** don't understand production culture, toolchains, or what "shipping AI under real constraints" means — they source on title, not capability
- **Pure AI/tech recruiters** (True Search, Harnham, DeepRec.ai) have no creative production network, can't assess production credibility, and don't understand studio client culture
- **VFX specialist recruiters** (Orbital People, Talent Outpost, TechNET Immersive) have the production depth but no AI leadership capability — they're building it reactively, not proactively
- **Internal TA** lacks the network depth in a candidate pool of 50–75 Tier 1 individuals globally
- **Job boards and LinkedIn** generate noise, not the right passive talent at senior level — personalised outreach to this pool yields 20–30% response rates vs. ~7% for generic InMail

**What it costs them:**
- Roles sitting open 3–6+ months
- Projects delayed or relaunched with inadequate AI leadership
- Existing team stretched covering the gap
- Missed competitive window as AI capability becomes table stakes in production

**Emotional tension:**
- Frustration at receiving irrelevant CVs from recruiters who clearly don't understand the brief
- Fear of making a wrong senior hire in a small, reputation-driven industry
- Anxiety about falling behind competitors who are further ahead on AI integration
- For candidates: wariness about AI ethics, IP provenance, and reputational risk — they won't respond to approaches that don't show understanding

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

1. **Network depth:** Six years of creative production placement history. FPZ knows the Tier 1 AI-in-production candidates at ILM, DNEG, Framestore, Adobe, and Foundry. No AI search firm has this. No VFX recruiter has the AI leadership side.

2. **Production fluency:** Ability to assess candidates on "AI shipped under real production constraints" — ComfyUI, Unreal Engine, Nuke, Maya, Houdini workflows under deadline pressure. No creative agency search firm has this evaluative capability.

3. **Proprietary candidate map:** The 50–75 Tier 1 candidates globally who can immediately fill the most senior AI-in-production roles are being mapped by FPZ. No other recruiter is doing this systematically. First-mover advantage compounds.

4. **Category creator advantage:** FPZ is not entering an existing category — it is creating one. The ability to define what "AI leadership in creative production" means is a durable competitive advantage.

5. **Informed observer positioning:** Not AI evangelism, not production nostalgia. The perspective of someone who understands why AI matters for creative production — and what it actually changes in how work gets made.

**How we do it differently:** We only recruit at this intersection. Every conversation, every event, every placement deepens the map of who's who in creative production leadership. We don't dip in and out — we live here. And we approach candidates with enough sector context that they respond.

**Why customers choose us:** They've either been burned by generalist recruiters who don't understand the brief, or they've reached the limits of their own network for a role that crosses two talent pools simultaneously. We offer the only solution that's genuinely qualified on both sides.

## Switching Dynamics (JTBD Four Forces)

**Push — what drives clients away from their current approach:**
- Generalist recruiters sending irrelevant CVs from people who've never worked in a production environment
- Roles sitting open for 4–6 months with no quality pipeline
- Internal TA exhausting their referral network without finding the right candidate
- Prior bad hires made through non-specialist routes — expensive mistakes at senior level

**Pull — what attracts clients to FPZ:**
- A recruiter who uses the right vocabulary without explanation
- Pre-vetted shortlists from a network built over six years in the sector
- Access to passive candidates who aren't visible on LinkedIn or job boards
- Confidence that the firm can evaluate technical depth in a production context

**Habit — what keeps them stuck:**
- Existing recruiter relationships built over years ("we always use X")
- Internal HR processes that default to job board posting
- Budget holders who haven't tried retained search and are uncomfortable with the fee structure
- "Our CEO just calls people they know from SIGGRAPH"

**Anxiety — what worries them about switching:**
- "Is this boutique firm large enough to deliver at the level we need?"
- "Will they have enough candidates in this niche?"
- "Is the retained fee justified before they've proven it to us?"
- "What if the placed candidate leaves quickly?" (addressed by retention guarantee on retained mandates)

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Professional but approachable. Knowledgeable without being preachy. Direct. Confident without overselling.

**Style:** Industry-insider perspective, not recruiter pitch. Short sentences as default. Vary length for rhythm. Active voice. Concrete over abstract. UK English throughout.

**Personality — five brand traits:**
1. **Straight-talking** — Direct, no fluff. Say what we mean.
2. **Embedded** — We are genuinely part of this industry, not observers from outside it.
3. **Precise** — We deal in specifics. Names, roles, context — not generalities.
4. **Curious** — Interested in where the industry is going, not just where it has been.
5. **Unhurried** — Confident enough not to oversell. The work speaks.

**Tone by channel:**

| Channel | Tone | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| LinkedIn posts | Informed, conversational, direct | First-person, one idea per post |
| Newsletter | Curated intelligence, editorial voice | Neutral observer — no promotion |
| Outreach messages | Warm, specific, respectful | Always reference the specific person's work |
| Website copy | Confident, minimal | Let positioning breathe |
| Case studies | Evidence-led, specific | Outcomes over process — numbers where available |

**On-brand example:**
> "The shift to real-time production is accelerating faster than most studios are hiring for. We know who's ahead of it."

**Off-brand example:**
