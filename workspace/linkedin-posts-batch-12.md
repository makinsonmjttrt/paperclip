## Post 1 — What the First Week Taught Us

**Pillar: Brand / Reflection | Audience: Both | Schedule: Mon 21 Apr or Tue 22 Apr**

---

We launched CreativAI last Friday. Here's what the first week actually taught us.

I expected the inbound to be about AI tools. Which platforms. Which workflows. Whether someone knew ComfyUI or had done anything with Sora.

That wasn't what came up.

The questions we got most often were structural. Not who. What.

What should this role actually own? Where does it sit in the org? How do we give it what it needs to deliver?

Studios that have already made an AI hire are asking those questions because the first one didn't work the way they expected. Not because the person was wrong. Because the structure around them wasn't right.

Studios preparing to hire are asking them because they've watched peers get it wrong and want to avoid the same outcome.

The demand for specialist support in AI creative hiring isn't new. What's new is the specificity of what studios need help thinking through.

We built CreativAI for that conversation. Week one confirmed it's the right one to be having.

## Post 2 — The AI Supervisor Role

**Pillar: Market Intelligence | Role Spotlight | Audience: Both (Studio Directors, Heads of Production; senior candidates) | Schedule: Wed 23 Apr**

---

The role of AI Supervisor has existed in visual effects pipelines for a while.

It's now appearing in briefs from experiential studios, content houses, and creative agencies that have never hired for it before.

Most of them don't quite know what they're asking for.

The VFX version sits between creative direction and technical delivery. They know enough about machine learning to validate outputs, enough about production to manage timelines, and enough about the brief to tell when the result is wrong.

That's a specific intersection. It doesn't come from a tools list.

Studios outside VFX are discovering this role exists because their projects now require it. Generated visual assets, AI-assisted animation, real-time pipelines with AI tools running mid-production. Someone has to own the quality and delivery of that layer.

The candidates who can do this well usually came up through one of two routes.

Deep VFX pipeline experience where AI entered the workflow. Or generative media art practice at serious production scale.

They're not on job boards.

They're working on projects they find interesting. You have to know where to find them.

P.S. CreativAI was built to map exactly this candidate pool. That's what we've been doing since Friday.

## Post 3 — The Studios Asking the Smartest Questions

**Pillar: Market Intelligence | Industry Observation | Audience: Client-facing | Schedule: Thu 24 Apr**

---

The studios asking the smartest AI hiring questions right now aren't the ones with the biggest AI strategies.

They're the ones who made a hire already and are preparing to make another.

The first time, they wrote a brief that described the tools they wanted someone to know. The role sat open for months. When they eventually hired, the person was good. The surrounding structure wasn't.

The second time, they call differently.

Not "we need an AI Creative Director." But: what does this function actually need to own? Who does it report to, and does that person understand what they're enabling? What does year one delivery actually look like?

Those questions change the brief. They change the search.

The studios moving fastest in AI creative production aren't faster because they started earlier. They're faster because they stopped describing what they want and started designing what they need.

That's the conversation that actually produces good hires.

What's the question you're working through before writing the brief?

## Post 4 — How to Position for AI-Hybrid Creative Roles

**Pillar: Talent Intelligence | Candidate Advice | Audience: Candidate-facing (senior EPs, Creative Directors, Heads of Production, Technical Producers) | Schedule: Fri 25 Apr**

---

If you're a senior creative professional trying to position yourself for AI-hybrid roles in 2026, the CV is probably not the problem.

The positioning conversation is.

Most experienced creatives undersell their AI relevance because they frame it as a tools question. Whether they've used a specific platform, whether they're "good at prompting." That's not what studios are hiring for.

Here's what actually lands in conversations with hiring decision-makers:

**Workflow ownership, not tool familiarity.** Did you build a process that changed how a team delivers something? That's the evidence. The tools are incidental.

**Delivery at scale.** Using AI on a personal project is different from using it on a project with a real client, a real deadline, and other people depending on your output.

**The ability to explain what didn't work.** Senior leaders trust people who understand failure modes. Candidates who can articulate where AI fell short, and what they did about it, read as more credible than those who only describe the wins.

The transition from "creative who uses AI" to "AI creative production leader" is a positioning shift, not a skills gap.
