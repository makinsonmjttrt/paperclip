## What These Conversations Are For

Every conversation does five things simultaneously:
1. Builds the candidate network (people, referrals, warm introductions)
2. Generates proprietary market intelligence nobody else has published
3. Tests FPZ positioning language in real conversations before pitching
4. Produces the market briefing deliverable (State of AI Leadership 2026)
5. Creates the confidence of having spoken to 20 senior people before the first retained pitch

This is not a candidate outreach exercise. It is a market research exercise that builds the business.

## The PIR Framework — What to Cover in Every Call

Every conversation follows the same three-part structure. Total call time: 20 minutes.

### P — Perspective (8 minutes)
Their view of the market. This is why they'll take the call — everyone wants to share their perspective.

Core questions (pick 2–3, do not run through all of them):
- "From where you sit, what does senior AI leadership in [their sector] actually require today — technically and operationally?"
- "How is your company thinking about the AI leadership hire? Is it a new role or an evolution of an existing one?"
- "What's the hardest part of making this kind of hire? Is it finding the right profile, or something else?"
- "When you look at the people doing this well — what separates the best from the rest?"

### I — Intelligence (8 minutes)
Specific market data you can't find from a desk. This is the proprietary layer.

Core questions (pick 2–3):
- "How did the last senior hire in this space actually happen — was it a search firm, internal promotion, network referral?"
- "What compensation package is realistic for a genuine Head of AI in a creative production environment? Base, bonus, equity?"
- "Would you consider a retained search firm for a role like this — and what would make that an easy yes?"
- "What do you think the right background actually looks like? ML research-to-industry, or pipeline engineering first?"
- "Union and IP sensitivity aside, how openly are companies talking about their AI hiring plans?"

### R — Referrals (4 minutes)
The compounding mechanism. One conversation becomes three.

Always end with:
- "Who else do you think I should be speaking to — either at the leadership level in this space, or on the client side who's actively thinking about this hire?"
- "Is there anyone you'd be comfortable making an introduction to? Even a one-line email would be brilliant."

## Multi-Channel Sequence (Per Contact)

| Day | Action |
|-----|--------|
| Day 1 | Send LinkedIn connection request (no message yet) OR send email if address known |
| Day 2 | If connected: engage with 1 piece of their recent LinkedIn content (comment, not just like) |
| Day 3–5 | Send primary outreach message (InMail or email) |
| Day 7 | If no response: single short follow-up — "Just checking this landed — happy to be brief" |
| Day 12 | Phone follow-up if direct number available |
| Day 14 | If still no response: pause this contact, move to next. Revisit in 30 days |

Response rate benchmarks (MARKET-ANALYSIS.md sourced):
- LinkedIn InMail cold C-suite: ~7%
- Personalised message referencing specific work: 20–30%
- Multi-channel + hyper-personalised: 34.5%

## Six Open Questions This Research Must Answer

From `docs/MARKET-ANALYSIS.md` — these are the questions desk research cannot resolve. Every conversation should advance at least one of them.

1. How do target companies actually make senior hiring decisions today? (CEO unilateral, CTO with budget, HR gatekeep, board threshold?)
2. What is the real compensation structure for AI leadership in creative production? (Not job-posting ranges — actual packages)
3. What would make a mid-market CXO pay £30–50k for a retained search? (Speed? Access? Guarantee? Advisory relationship?)
4. What are the actual career motivators for senior AI leaders in creative production?
5. Where are the genuine skills gaps that create urgency for external hiring?
6. What is the actual recruiter penetration rate — and why?
