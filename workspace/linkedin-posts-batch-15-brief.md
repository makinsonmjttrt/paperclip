## Post P1 — Candidate-facing | Thu 14 May

**Working title:** What a strong AI production candidate profile actually looks like in 2026

**Target audience:** Senior AI creative production professionals — Heads of AI Production, Technical Directors, Creative Technologists, VPs of Production with AI scope

**Pillar:** Talent intelligence / Candidate advice

**Angle:**
Not a skills checklist. This post is observational — what do candidates who land the right AI production roles actually do differently in how they position themselves?

The insight to build around: strong candidates in this space don't lead with their tool proficiency. They lead with how they make decisions under constraint — what they prioritised, what they cut, what they would do differently. Studios hiring at this level are not testing for fluency with a specific platform. They are trying to work out whether this person has built real production capability or has supervised workflows built by someone else.

Secondary angle: the "AI experience" question. Candidates who answer this well describe a body of work with honest complexity — what the tooling couldn't do, what the brief required them to work around, where the process failed and why. Candidates who answer it poorly give a list of platforms and a clean success narrative.

**Structure guidance:** Observation from placement work → the contrast (what strong profiles do vs weak) → the underlying reason (why studios care about this) → actionable close (what candidates can do with this).

**Word count target:** 180–220 words
**Schedule slot:** Thu 14 May (confirmed in May calendar)

## Post P2 — Thought leadership | Flex (May Week 2–3, Martyn to schedule)

**Working title:** The org design question that determines whether an AI production hire succeeds

**Target audience:** Studio leaders, Heads of Production, COOs, Founders — client-facing

**Pillar:** Market intelligence / Strategic insight

**Angle:**
Post-FMX, post-MPTS, studios are returning from events with AI production conversations front of mind. The pattern that emerges from inbound briefs: most studios frame the problem as "who should we hire" before they have resolved "what should this function own."

The insight: an AI production leadership hire into an undefined mandate is high-risk for both the studio and the candidate. The role becomes what the person can defend rather than what the business actually needs. Year one gets spent lobbying for scope rather than delivering against it.

This is not criticism of studios. It is the shape of a genuinely new organisational challenge — AI production doesn't map neatly onto existing org structures, so studios are working it out as they go. The thoughtful ones are treating the hire as an org design decision, not just a headcount decision.

Angle for Martyn: this should be positioned as an observation from working with studios on both sides of the hire, not prescriptive advice. The voice is "here is what I keep seeing" rather than "here is what you should do."

**Structure guidance:** Pattern observation → why it happens → what it costs (candidate and studio) → what the alternative looks like (brief clarity before search starts) → close that invites conversation.

**Word count target:** 190–240 words
**Schedule slot:** Flex — suitable for Week 2 or Week 3, not BD-flagged
