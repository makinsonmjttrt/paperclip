## Competitive Landscape: Current State

### Threat level: Low

No new specialist firm has launched in FPZ's lane since January 2026. Senior AI leadership search at the intersection of creative production, VFX, spatial computing, and virtual production remains uncontested by any specialist recruiter.

### Existing firms: what they have actually done

**Aquent** is the most active mover but not in FPZ's lane. They launched **Skill** (January 2026), a machine-matching staffing platform sibling, and **RoboHead Spark** (February 2026), an AI layer for creative project management. These are tooling plays aimed at all sectors. No VFX or virtual production vertical evident.

**Artisan Talent** is producing thought leadership on AI-era creative hiring (AI Product Officer, AI Ethicist as emerging roles) but is focused on broader marketing and agency creative, not production-side VFX or spatial computing executive search.

**24 Seven Talent** has a generic AI practice and won staffing awards in February 2026. No creative tech production angle.

**Searchlight Global** is publishing on AI's effect on entertainment recruitment and calling 2026 pivotal for immersive experience demand. They are incumbents in broadcast and media executive search. They remain the one firm with the sector relationships to credibly enter FPZ's lane if they chose to. No evidence they have moved there yet.

**Xcede, Digitalent:** No verified announcements in this window.

### Structural events that matter to FPZ

**Pixomondo closed** (March 2026): Sony wound down Pixomondo across six cities, consolidating VFX work at Sony Pictures Imageworks. Approximately 500 people affected. This has displaced a cohort of senior VFX and virtual production talent into the active market, which is a candidate-side opportunity.

**Meta is hiring VFX artists** on short-term contracts (roughly three months) via third-party recruiters to evaluate AI-generated visuals and fine-tune models. This is contract AI training labour, not permanent leadership placement. It is pulling some VFX talent out of the permanent hire pool at the mid-level.

**Promise / The Generation Company** launched with Google and Peter Chernin backing, creating an AI-generated VFX advisory division under Nem Perez. This is the type of embedded AI-creative leadership structure FPZ should be placing into. Not a competitor, but a signal of the market FPZ is serving.

### What to watch

Searchlight Global. No action taken yet, but they have the relationships to be a credible entrant. Monitor quarterly.

### Recommended response posture

Hold course. The competitive silence observed in the D+7 brief below remains accurate. No scenario has changed. The Pixomondo displacement creates a near-term BD angle: studios consolidating headcount are the clients most likely to need specialist external search for the senior AI-production roles they cannot fill internally. The Meta contract-hire pattern is noise at FPZ's seniority level.

# CreativAI D+7 Competitive Response Brief

**Type:** Internal strategy brief — not for distribution

## What Competitors Will Do

### Scenario 1 — Counter-positioning (most likely source: Digitalent, Searchlight)

One or both will update their LinkedIn presence or website copy to signal adjacency to the AI-creative space. Digitalent might add "creative AI teams" language. Searchlight might add "AI production leadership" to their sectors page.

**FPZ stance:** Hold the specific claim, don't respond to the general one. The moment a competitor moves toward this space with vague language, sharpen the message further — name the exact sectors, exact seniority band, exact candidate profile. Specificity defeats adjacency. If a prospect raises a competitor by name, use the objection responses already built into the positioning brief. Don't acknowledge the competitor publicly.

---

### Scenario 2 — Silence (most likely: Xcede, DeepRec.ai, Talent Outpost)

Most competitors will not respond at all. Their clients are not FPZ clients. Their candidates are not FPZ candidates. The launch will register as noise in a different sector.

**FPZ stance:** Use the silence. In sales conversations, the fact that no established AI recruiter and no established media recruiter is covering this intersection is itself a proof point. "No one else is doing this" is not a self-serving claim — it's an observable market fact. Keep publishing into the white space.

---

### Scenario 3 — Copycat move (medium-term risk, 3–6 months post-launch)

A competitor — most likely Searchlight, given their media sector relationships — announces an "AI practice" or begins using AI-integrated production language in their outreach. Less likely to be an AI-tech firm pivoting toward creative; they lack the relationships.

**FPZ stance:** Welcome it publicly, frame it as market validation. "Other firms are starting to recognise this talent category" is a stronger story than defensiveness. FPZ has six years of relationships and a track record of placements at this intersection. A copycat with a new landing page and no candidate network is not a threat — they'll fail on the first senior brief they take in. Don't accelerate their credibility by acknowledging them as competition.

---

## LinkedIn Post Angles (post-launch, April 24 onward)

### Angle 1 — The category definition post

Frame what a creative technology leader actually is: not an AI engineer, not a traditional EP, but someone who has managed production pipelines that use AI under real delivery pressure. Name the types of roles and environments (VFX, spatial computing, experiential studios) without making claims about who holds them. This establishes FPZ as the firm that understands the candidate profile — before competitors have even thought about it.

*Tone:* observational, not promotional. "Here's what I'm seeing in this hiring market" not "here's why FPZ is the best."

---

### Angle 2 — The misfit candidate post

Describe a specific pattern: a studio comes to a generalist AI recruiter for a VP of Production role, gets shortlisted candidates with strong AI credentials and no production floor experience. Or comes to a traditional media search firm and gets credible EPs who have never worked with real-time tools. This is a real problem Martyn has personally observed, and it signals the gap without naming competitors.

*Tone:* first-person, specific, no jargon. "This happened again last week" framing.

---

### Angle 3 — The six-year network post

Not a chest-beating post. A specific, grounded observation about why relationships at this intersection take time to build — why the senior creative technology talent pool is small, largely passive, and reachable only through direct industry relationships built before AI became a hiring priority. This defends tenure without arguing about it.

*Tone:* honest and direct. Avoid anything that reads as a sales pitch. No CTAs.

---

## What FPZ Should NOT Do

Do not monitor competitor LinkedIn pages and respond to their posts, even obliquely. Do not write posts that begin with "what the AI recruitment industry is getting wrong" — this frames FPZ as a challenger brand reacting to others, not an incumbent operating in its own space. Do not post polls about AI and hiring; they generate engagement from people outside the target audience and dilute the positioning signal. Do not pivot messaging in response to anything a competitor publishes; the positioning is grounded in FPZ's actual six-year track record, not in reacting to a competitor's GTM. Do not congratulate competitors publicly when they enter adjacent territory — this amplifies them.
