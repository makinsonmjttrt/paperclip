VFX studios have been asking the wrong question about AI hiring for two years.

The question has been: who do we hire?

The harder question is: what do we need them to actually do?

This sounds obvious. It isn't. Most AI briefs describe the person, not the problem. Strong Python. Diffusion model experience. Track record in ML pipelines. All reasonable requirements. None of them tell you whether the studio is ready to use that person effectively once they're in the building.

The pattern I keep seeing: a studio makes an impressive hire. Real credentials, genuine delivery experience, the right technical profile. Six months in, the role is struggling. Not **because the person is wrong. Because the organisation wasn't ready for them.**

No mandate. No infrastructure budget. Leadership not aligned on what AI production actually means for the business. The new hire spends year one building internal consensus that should have been established before the offer went out.

This conversation happens in debrief calls, not in press releases.

VFX is a reputation industry. Studios don't broadcast when an AI strategy isn't working. They quietly restructure, redefine scope, manage expectations down. The public narrative stays confident. The briefing call tells a different story.

The shift I'm seeing in 2026 briefs: some studios have done this cycle once already and are approaching the second hire differently. Less weight on credentials. **More weight on scope, mandate, and organisational readiness** before the search goes live.

The practical questions that change the brief: What does this person need to deliver in year one? Who are they accountable to? What budget and infrastructure will they have on day one? Has leadership aligned on what AI production means for this studio's output, or is that still being worked out?

A brief that can't answer those questions isn't ready to go to market.

The studios getting this right are treating the hire as a change management programme with a person attached. The ones still struggling are treating it as a talent acquisition problem.

**The talent isn't the hard part anymore.**
