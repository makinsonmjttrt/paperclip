The most interesting conversations from the first two weeks have not come from the contacts we expected. The studios with active AI programmes — the ones already building internal capability — have largely been quiet. The calls that have actually happened have been with leadership teams at an earlier stage: organisations who know they need to act, but have not yet agreed internally on what acting looks like.

That is a useful signal. The demand for CreativAI searches is not coming from studios that have already figured it out. It is coming from the ones who have not, and need someone who can help them get the brief right before the search opens.

## Why role definition is still the first obstacle

This was the founding observation behind CreativAI, and two weeks of conversations have confirmed it holds.

Studios are not struggling to find senior AI talent because the talent does not exist. They are struggling because the brief has not been resolved. The technology function writes a role that looks like a product or engineering hire. The creative function writes a role that looks like a creative director with a machine learning module bolted on. Neither version describes the actual job — and neither reliably finds the person who can do it.

The pattern is familiar from the shift to virtual production three or four years ago. The studios that hired well for that transition were not the ones with the best job descriptions. They were the ones who took the time to ask: what does this role actually decide, every day, and what does someone need to be to make those decisions well?

The same question applies now. Most organisations have not yet asked it in enough detail. That is where the most useful work CreativAI can do starts — before the search, not after it.

## What we calibrated after week one

One thing the first two weeks have suggested we may have underestimated: the interest from creative SaaS companies and technology vendors, rather than studios and agencies.

The case for placing AI leadership talent inside a production studio is intuitive. The same conversation is happening at the companies that build the tools those studios use — and those organisations face an equally sharp version of the brief problem, because the role at a technology vendor sits at an even more complex intersection of product, commercial, and creative credibility.

We are keeping that in view as the practice develops.

## What CreativAI looks like in the next 60 days

The focus for the next eight weeks is completing the initial BD conversations and moving the strongest pipeline discussions to formal brief stage. Alongside that, we are building out the candidate pool for the specific role profiles we are seeing most frequently — particularly at Head of AI Production and Creative Technology Director level. The goal by the end of that window is to have the first formal searches open and at shortlist stage.
