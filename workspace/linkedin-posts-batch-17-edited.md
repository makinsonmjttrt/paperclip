## Post P2 — Six Weeks In: What the Briefs Are Telling Us

**Pillar: Market Intelligence / Practice Observation | Audience: Client-facing | Schedule: Tue 3 Jun**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Observation-Pattern-Implication-CTA**

---

Six weeks of inbound briefs in. The pattern is clear.

The briefs that lead to strong shortlists share one thing: they describe what the person needs to deliver, not which tools they need to know.

The briefs that lead to difficult searches do something different.

They lead with tool proficiency. "Experience with X in production." "Working knowledge of Y at scale." The brief becomes a checklist of current capabilities.

That approach narrows the field to candidates who are fluent in today's tooling.

It doesn't find someone with the production judgment to adapt when that tooling changes in eighteen months.

And it will change.

The underlying need in almost every tool-led brief is something different. Usually it's production leadership under ambiguity. The ability to build process around capabilities that don't yet have established workflows.

A brief that leads with output capability and judgment finds that person.

A brief that leads with tool familiarity usually finds a different one, and the search takes longer.

Call it an observation about where searches stall.

If you're working on a brief right now, what are you leading with?

## Post P3 — The Question That Tells Me in Three Minutes

**Pillar: Talent Intelligence / Candidate Advice | Audience: Candidate-facing | Schedule: Thu 5 Jun**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Setup → Contrast → Signal → CTA**

---

There is a question I ask in almost every senior AI creative production conversation.

It doesn't look like a test. Most people don't notice what it's surfacing until the conversation is over.

The ones who answer it as a tool competency question tend not to get the role.

The ones who land the roles answer it as a production philosophy question.

The difference shows in the detail.

Strong candidates describe what the tooling couldn't do. Where the brief pushed beyond what the workflow could handle. Where the process failed and what they would approach differently next time.

That level of honest complexity is a strong signal of real production experience.

Most candidates answer the same question with a capabilities list and a clean success story.

The polished narrative is the tell.

Production at senior level doesn't deliver cleanly. Everyone working in it knows that. A candidate who describes only the wins hasn't yet built the thing at the level you're hiring for.

The candidates who land these roles have navigated the constraints. Not just cited the capabilities.

What does your answer look like?
