## 1. The 30-Second Answer

> CreativAI is FourPointZero's executive search practice for the leaders who run AI-integrated creative production. We place the senior operators at VFX studios, experiential agencies, and spatial computing companies where production delivery and AI workflow fluency are both on the job spec. We have been building this candidate network since 2019.

Use this when someone asks "what is CreativAI?" for the first time. It covers what it is, who it's for, and why we can do it — in under 30 seconds.

---

## 2. The Why Now Answer

AI is no longer in the R&D phase at creative studios. Production pipelines at major VFX houses are running AI tools under live delivery pressure. The problem is the hiring market hasn't caught up. Generalist AI recruiters know the technology but have no production sector relationships and no framework for assessing delivery competence. Entertainment and media search firms know the sector but can't evaluate AI workflow fluency. The result, which we see repeatedly in the briefs we take in, is shortlists that fail on the first credibility check: strong on AI credentials, no production floor experience, or strong EPs who've never worked with real-time tools. That gap, and the cohort of senior candidates who can actually bridge it, is what CreativAI was built around.

---

## 3. The Differentiator

Every competitor operates on one side of the boundary. AI tech recruiters assess AI credentials and miss the production delivery piece. Media and entertainment search firms know the sector and miss the AI competence.

FPZ has been placing senior creative tech talent since 2019, before AI was a hiring consideration at these studios. The senior AI-in-production talent at the major VFX houses and spatial computing companies know us. That took six years of placements, direct relationships, and industry presence to build. An AI recruiter who decides to cover creative production tomorrow cannot replicate it with a LinkedIn Recruiter licence. They'll produce a shortlist that looks credible on paper and collapses in the third interview.

We shortlist on both sides. Our candidates have run pipelines, managed crews, and delivered under production pressure. They understand where AI fits in that workflow.

## 4. Objection Responses

---

### "Isn't this too niche?"

The niche is the value. The senior candidates who can run AI-integrated production are a small population. A broad search across that population gets you a long shortlist of people who almost fit. A search that understands both sides of the profile gets you 8 to 12 people who actually do.

If the brief is director-level or above, at a studio with an AI production mandate, the level of specificity we bring is not a limitation. It's the reason the shortlist is credible.

---

### "I'm not sure I need a specialist."

You might not. If you have a well-defined senior role, a strong internal talent network, and the in-house capability to assess AI workflow competence in interview, a generalist search will probably work.

If any of those three conditions is uncertain, a 15-minute conversation before you commit to an approach costs nothing.

---

### "We'll check internally first."

Standard first step, entirely sensible. If the internal search produces the calibre of candidates you need, there's no reason to engage us.

If the shortlist comes back thin, or the candidates are strong on one dimension but not both, that's when we're useful. We don't pitch against internal searches. We extend them when they hit the boundary of what an internal network can reach.

---

## 5. The Soundbite

> "AI recruiters know the technology. Production recruiters know the studio. We know both sides."

Use in conversation, not in writing. It's a provocation, not a tagline. It will prompt the follow-up question, which is the point.
