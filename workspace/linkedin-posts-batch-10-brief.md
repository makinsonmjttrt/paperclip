## Format Guidance

- **Length:** 150–250 words per post. No longer. May audience is warm — they've been reading Martyn's posts for a month. Don't re-explain the landscape they already understand.
- **Hook style:** First line must stand alone as a complete thought. No questions as openers. No "Here's what I've noticed..." throat-clearing. State the observation directly.
- **CTA approach:** One of three patterns per post:
  - Soft close: "Curious what others are seeing." (invitation)
  - P.S. close: Anecdote that reinforces the post argument in one line
  - Direct prompt: A specific question relevant to the post's ICP
- **Format structure:** Short paragraphs. 2–3 sentences max. No bullet points unless the content is genuinely a list (3+ discrete items).
- **Tone check:** Practitioner voice, not pundit voice. Martyn is in these conversations — the posts should feel like observations from active work, not analysis of a trend.

## Post Prompts

### Post 1 — FMX Signal: What the event tells us (Both / market signal)

**Anchor:** FMX Stuttgart, 5–8 May. Post week of 5 May.

**Brief:** FMX is one of the best windows into where the industry's head is at. Studios present confident AI integration narratives on stage. Privately, the hiring conversations are more cautious — studios are asking how to build AI leadership capacity, not whether to. Post should contrast what the industry is saying in public with what it's actually doing in terms of roles, structure, and hiring decisions. The gap between the conference narrative and the talent reality is the post's argument.

**Angle:** Observation piece. Martyn is positioned as someone who sees both sides — the public narrative at events like FMX and the private conversations about who to hire and why. This reinforces authority without any direct self-promotion.

**Hook direction:** Start with what FMX (or conferences like it) reveal about the gap between AI aspiration and AI operational reality in creative production.

**CTA:** Soft invitation — "What are you seeing at FMX this year?" or "Curious what others in the room are taking away."

---

### Post 2 — The 90-Day Problem (Client-facing)

**Anchor:** No event tie. General scheduling — week of 12 May.

**Brief:** The failure point for AI creative leadership hires is rarely at the point of hire. It's in the first three months. Studios underinvest in onboarding senior AI roles because they assume a strong candidate needs less setup, not more. The post should name what actually causes the underdelivery in the first 90 days: unclear mandate, no internal champion, tool infrastructure not ready, senior leadership not bought in. Frame it as something Martyn sees from placement follow-up conversations.

**Angle:** Insight from post-placement experience. This reinforces FPZ's value as a recruitment partner who stays engaged after the placement, not just before. Subtle differentiation from transactional recruiters.

**Hook direction:** Open with the observation that the underdelivery isn't a hiring failure — it's a setup failure. Something like: the hire was right; what came after the offer wasn't.

**CTA:** P.S. close — one-line observation from an actual placement follow-up.

---

### Post 3 — The Two-Role Brief (Client-facing)

**Anchor:** No event tie. General scheduling — week of 12–16 May, pairs well near MPTS.

**Brief:** Many AI production leadership briefs bundle two fundamentally different roles into one job description: technical architect (who designs the AI infrastructure) and creative delivery lead (who uses it to make things). Candidates who are genuinely strong at one are rarely strong at both. Post should explain why studios write briefs this way (they can't find either, so they ask for both), what it signals about their actual maturity on AI, and what a better brief structure looks like. Not a lecture — an observation that gives studio heads something useful to take back.

**Angle:** Practical client intelligence. Positions Martyn as someone who can improve the process before search begins. Builds trust with pre-brief prospects.

**Hook direction:** Open with the specific observation about the two-role brief — "The most common structural error in AI production briefs is also the one most often presented as a feature, not a flaw."

**CTA:** Prompt — "If you're working on an AI creative production brief right now, happy to look at it."

---

### Post 4 — Why They Actually Move (Candidate-facing)

**Anchor:** No event tie. General scheduling — week of 19 May or later in May.

**Brief:** Senior AI creative professionals considering a move rarely say publicly what's actually driving them. In the conversations Martyn has with candidates evaluating an offer or a move, the real questions are about mandate (is this role genuinely senior or advisory?), infrastructure (will I have what I need to build with, or will I spend year one lobbying for budget?), and intent (is the studio building capability or buying credibility?). Post should surface these questions in a way that is useful to candidates thinking through a move, and that signals to studio decision-makers what their offer needs to address.

**Angle:** Candidate intelligence that is also client intelligence. Candidates who see this will share it. Studios who see it should take notes.

**Hook direction:** Open on the gap between stated motivators (compensation, title, project scope) and real motivators (mandate clarity, build vs. experiment, leadership buy-in).

**CTA:** Direct prompt — "If you're evaluating a move right now and want a candid view on what to ask in final conversations, I'm available."
