## Post 2 — AI-Native Studio Structures

**Pillar: Client Intelligence | Org Design | Audience: Client-facing (Studio Directors, Founders, Heads of Production, COOs) | Schedule: Wed 15 Apr**

---

Studios that hired for AI production in 2023 are restructuring in 2026.

The role worked. **The reporting line didn't.**

When creative studios first brought in AI capability, they placed it inside existing production structures. EP, Producer, Creative Director. The logic was sound. These were established roles with clear delivery expectations.

What they found: the AI function needs things those structures weren't built to provide.

Mandate. Workflow ownership. Pipeline access.

Bolting AI capability onto an existing role gives you a capable person in the wrong position.

The studios moving fastest have separated the function entirely. AI Creative Director, ML Pipeline Producer, AI Production Lead with their own team and real accountability for output.

That changes what hiring looks like. Not finding someone who knows the tools. **Designing a function around the person you hire.**

The question we're being asked most often now isn't "who should we hire."

It's "what does the role need to look like before we hire."

That's a structural question. It requires a different kind of conversation before the brief gets written.

If you're thinking through how to structure this, I'm having that conversation with studios most weeks. Happy to share what's coming up.

## Post 3 — What to Put on Your CV If You Use AI Tools

**Pillar: Talent Intelligence | Candidate Advice | Audience: Candidate-facing (EPs, Creative Directors, Heads of Production, Technical Producers) | Schedule: Flexible — any slot 14–17 Apr or defer to following week**

---

If you use AI in your production work but can't describe it beyond "familiar with MidJourney," your CV is underselling you.

The candidates landing senior AI production roles aren't the ones with the longest tools list.

They're the ones who can explain what those tools enabled.

**Name the workflow, not just the tool.**

"Used ComfyUI to build a still-to-video pipeline for a brand campaign" is more compelling than "familiar with ComfyUI." The project context and the output are what signal capability. The tool is the method. The problem it solved is the value.

**Show that you've led adoption, not just used tools yourself.**

Getting a team to change how they work because of something you built is a leadership achievement. It belongs in your experience section, not your skills block.

The gap between "AI tools I use" and "AI workflows I've designed" is bigger than it sounds. If you have both, make that distinction clear.

The studios hiring AI production leadership want people who identify a production problem and know which AI approach solves it. Not operators.

A list of tools without context isn't evidence of that. A project that changed how a team delivers something is.

If you're a senior AI creative production professional thinking about your next move, DM me. Happy to give feedback on how you're presenting this.

### Option B — The internal speed problem

The biggest challenge hiring AI talent in creative tech isn't skills. It's getting the organisation to move at the speed the market requires.

Senior AI production talent doesn't wait.

The candidate pool is small. The competition is real. The window between shortlist and offer is shorter than most studios expect.

Internal approval cycles were built for a different kind of hire. They weren't designed for a market where the best candidates have three conversations a week.

What's coming up for you? Curious what others are seeing.

### Option C — The signal vs. noise problem

The biggest challenge hiring AI talent in creative tech isn't skills. It's separating genuine production experience from people who can talk about it well.

The market has candidates who are fluent in the language of AI creative production.

Fewer of them have actually delivered a complex project using it at any real scale.

Knowing the difference requires context the job description doesn't give you. It requires conversations with people who have seen both sides.

That's the part of the process most studios underinvest in.

What's coming up for you? Curious what others are seeing.
