## Post 1 — Apr 18 (D+1): First Reactions

**Word count:** ~165
**Type:** Personal reflection
**Hook:** Anticlimactic as insight

---

Launch day is never what you picture it to be.

I spent weeks thinking about the announcement. The positioning, the framing, what CreativAI should actually say. What I didn't think about was the morning after.

Here's what it actually felt like: quieter than expected. And clearer.

By the end of the day I realised CreativAI isn't new. It's just named now. Everything that makes it worth building — the network, the sector knowledge, the ability to shortlist people most recruiters have never heard of — that's been running since 2019.

The launch didn't create something. It acknowledged something.

There's a particular kind of relief in that. I spent years positioning FourPointZero at the intersection of creative production and emerging technology. CreativAI is what that positioning was always pointing toward.

What surprised me most wasn't any specific reaction from outside. It was how clear the proposition felt once it was written down and public.

Sometimes the act of publishing forces you to understand your own thinking properly.

## Post 2 — Apr 20 (D+3): The Conversation

**Word count:** ~175
**Type:** Story post
**Hook:** A single conversation that clarified everything

---

One conversation decided it.

About a year ago, a studio Director called me about a senior search. They'd already worked with two recruiters. Neither had stuck. The briefs went out, CVs came back, nothing felt right. They were considering stopping the search altogether.

I asked to see the brief. Twelve lines. Strong on credentials, vague on context. Nothing about the production environment, the existing pipeline, or what success would look like at month six. It was written to attract candidates, not to describe a job.

We spent an hour rewriting it. Not the requirements — the reality of what this person would inherit and what they'd need to do to it. When the brief changed, the candidate profile changed completely.

The person we placed six weeks later had been passed over by both previous searches. Not because they'd missed them — because the original brief had made them look like the wrong fit.

That conversation is what CreativAI is for. Not finding different candidates. Understanding the search well enough to look in the right place.

## Post 3 — Apr 22 (D+5): Early Signal

**Word count:** ~175
**Type:** Observation + question
**Hook:** Pattern I didn't anticipate (framed as question since no verified data yet)

---

Five days in. A pattern I didn't anticipate.

I expected most of the inbound interest to come from studios actively hiring — brief ready, mandate confirmed, moving quickly. Some of that. But the more interesting conversations have been with companies who aren't there yet.

Not calling about a role. Calling to work out whether they need one.

That's a different conversation. Less structured, longer, often more valuable. A lot of creative production companies are sitting with the same question right now: we know AI is changing what production leadership needs to look like, but we haven't agreed internally what that means for us.

I didn't build CreativAI expecting that conversation. I built it expecting the brief to exist and to go find the right person. The reality is that a significant part of the value sits earlier — in helping the mandate take shape before the search starts.

Whether that's the market I built for or just where the market happens to be right now, I'm still working that out.

What's prompting these conversations at your studio?
