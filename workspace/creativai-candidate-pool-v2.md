**FourPointZero | CreativAI Practice | April 2026**

*Directional research for BD and sourcing strategy. No invented statistics. All market availability assessments are qualitative.*

## Role Taxonomy

Eight to ten active roles in the UK market. Listed from most to least constrained on supply.

---

### 1. Head of AI Production

Senior operational and strategic leadership over AI-integrated production workflows. Owns how AI tools enter the pipeline, governs quality and output standards, bridges creative vision with technical implementation. Often holds P&L accountability or direct pipeline ownership.

**Live title variants:** Head of AI Production, AI Production Director, Head of AI Creative Lab, VP of AI Production, Global Head of AI Workflows.

**Skills expected:** Generative AI tool evaluation and integration (ComfyUI, Runway, Sora, Adobe Firefly), cross-functional authority over technical and creative teams, budget and vendor management, IP and usage rights risk awareness.

**Supply:** The most constrained profile. The intersection of production delivery experience and genuine AI tool fluency is narrow. Big tech — Meta in particular — has been pulling senior production operators out of the studio ecosystem.

---

### 2. AI Pipeline Technical Director

Designs, builds, and maintains the AI components of a studio's production pipeline. Distinct from a traditional Pipeline TD: the work involves integrating ML models, managing inference infrastructure, building custom AI tooling, and connecting disparate AI services into coherent artist-facing workflows.

**Live title variants:** AI Pipeline TD, AI Pipeline and Tools Engineer, Gen AI Workflow Designer, ML Pipeline Engineer, R&D Pipeline Engineer (AI).

**Skills expected:** Python (required), C++ (common at senior level), PyTorch, diffusion model architecture knowledge (Stable Diffusion, ControlNet, LoRA fine-tuning), AWS or GCP, Docker/Kubernetes, Houdini, Nuke, Maya, USD (Universal Scene Description).

**Supply:** Arguably the single scarcest profile. The role requires simultaneous ML engineering competence, deep VFX software fluency, and production delivery experience. Senior engineers with all three are the target.

---

### 3. Generative AI Creative Director

Creative authority on AI-generated or AI-assisted output. Has a working technical vocabulary — can direct an AI system, not just direct human artists. Understands model behaviour, prompt engineering at a sophisticated level, fine-tuning concepts, and the aesthetic language of different generative systems.

**Live title variants:** Generative AI Creative Director, AI Creative Director, Head of AI Creative, AI Creative Lead (Art Director), Creative AI Lead.

**Skills expected:** Strong art direction background with portfolio demonstrating taste, deep prompt engineering fluency across multiple model families (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion variants, Runway, Sora, Firefly), image-to-image and video generation workflows, fine-tuning models on brand training data, ComfyUI.

**Supply:** More candidates exist here than in the technical archetypes, but genuine creative authority combined with AI fluency is still uncommon. The challenge is distinguishing real creative AI authority from surface-level adoption.

---

### 4. AI/ML Engineer (Creative Applications)

Builds and deploys AI systems that artists and creatives directly use — custom image generation pipelines, video synthesis tools, real-time AI processing for live events, or fine-tuned models for specific production tasks. Closer to creative production than a standard ML position.

**Live title variants:** AI/ML Engineer (Creative Applications), Applied ML Engineer, Generative AI Engineer, AI Software Engineer (VFX/Creative), Technical AI Artist, Principal Tech Artist.

**Skills expected:** Python (required), C++ and CUDA (for performance-sensitive work), PyTorch or JAX, diffusion models and GANs, real-time inference optimisation, REST APIs, model serving infrastructure, OpenUSD, Unreal/Unity plugins.

**Supply:** The broadest pool of the four primary archetypes, but still constrained at senior level. ML engineers with creative sector experience command a premium.

---

### 5. Head of Virtual Production

Leads virtual production capability at a studio or broadcaster. Increasingly expected to own the AI integration within the VP pipeline — particularly generative backgrounds, AI-assisted previs, and LED volume environment generation.

**Live title variants:** Head of Virtual Production, VP Supervisor, Virtual Production Director, Head of Immersive Production.

**Skills expected:** LED volume technology, Unreal Engine, real-time rendering, on-set workflow management, growing expectation of AI previs tool knowledge. Background typically comes from VFX supervision or practical production.

**Supply:** Specialist pool distinct from AI engineering. Most practitioners come from film and broadcast VFX leadership. VP as a discipline is growing faster than trained practitioners.

---

### 6. Real-Time Pipeline Supervisor (AI)

Manages the intersection of game engine pipelines and AI-generated content in live event, broadcast, or experiential contexts. Often spans Unreal Engine real-time delivery alongside generative AI asset creation.

**Live title variants:** Real-Time Pipeline Supervisor, Technical Director (Real-Time), Pipeline Supervisor (Unreal Engine), Real-Time Technical Director.

**Skills expected:** Unreal Engine (expert level), HLSL/GLSL shader programming, real-time rendering optimisation, increasing expectation of ComfyUI or similar AI asset generation integration. Often comes from games or live broadcast technical direction.

**Supply:** Small pool. The combination of live delivery experience (zero tolerance for failures) and AI integration is rare. Many candidates come from esports broadcast or experiential agency technical teams.

---

### 7. AI Research Engineer (VFX/Creative)

R&D-focused role at larger studios or technology companies. Develops novel AI methods with direct application to creative production — new diffusion architectures, real-time NeRF/3DGS applications, video generation for VFX, etc. Bridges academic research and production application.

**Live title variants:** AI Research Engineer, Research Scientist (Visual Computing), Principal Research Engineer (AI), Applied Research Scientist (Creative AI).

**Skills expected:** Strong research background (PhD common but not universal), PyTorch, computer vision and generative model expertise, CUDA, publication record or demonstrable open-source research output, track record of shipping research into production.

**Supply:** Smallest pool by volume. These candidates are contested most heavily between VFX studios, tech companies, and AI labs. Geographic concentration in London (ILM, Framestore), San Francisco, and Vancouver.

---

### 8. Spatial Computing Lead

Leads the design and delivery of spatial computing experiences — immersive environments for Vision Pro, AR, and XR platforms — with increasing expectation of AI-generated spatial content integration.

**Live title variants:** Head of Spatial Computing, Spatial Design Lead, XR Creative Director, Immersive Experience Lead, AR/VR Creative Director.

**Skills expected:** visionOS development (or ARKit/ARCore), USD spatial workflows, 3D spatial design, increasingly expected to have working knowledge of AI world generation tools, AI-assisted 3D asset creation. Often comes from XR agency or gaming backgrounds.

**Supply:** Emerging profile with a small but growing pool. The visionOS market is nascent enough that senior spatial computing practitioners with AI fluency are concentrated in a handful of agencies and product companies.

---

### 9. Technical AI Artist

A bridge role: part artist, part engineer. Creates tools and workflows that enable creative teams to use AI effectively. Sits closer to a TD than a traditional artist — writes scripts, builds ComfyUI pipelines, trains and fine-tunes models for project-specific use — but maintains active creative practice.

**Live title variants:** Technical AI Artist, AI Tools Artist, Creative Technologist (AI), Technical Artist (Generative AI).

**Skills expected:** Python scripting, ComfyUI or similar workflow tooling, Stable Diffusion fine-tuning (LoRA, DreamBooth), 2D/3D software fluency (Photoshop, Blender, Houdini at tool level), strong understanding of artist workflow and friction points.

**Supply:** One of the more accessible entry points to the AI creative talent market at mid-level. Senior Technical AI Artists with significant studio production credits are fewer.

---

### 10. AI Workflow Architect

A strategic and operational role at larger studios or agencies: responsible for designing the AI tool stack, managing vendor relationships, governing the AI integration roadmap, and enabling the whole organisation to work with AI effectively. Distinct from a Head of AI Production in being more systems and governance focused than delivery focused.

**Live title variants:** Head of AI Strategy (Production), AI Transformation Lead, Head of AI Enablement, VP of AI Innovation (Creative), Director of AI Workflow.

**Skills expected:** Strong working knowledge of the AI creative tool landscape, vendor assessment, change management, cross-functional stakeholder communication, data governance, IP and rights frameworks for AI-generated content. Often comes from senior production management, operations, or creative technology backgrounds.

**Supply:** Emerging title category. Many organisations are still building this role definition. The available candidates tend to have come from production management or creative technology rather than pure AI backgrounds.

## Candidate Personas

### Persona A: The Pipeline Veteran Gone AI-Native

**Name archetype:** "The Pragmatic Converter"

**Background:** Spent eight to twelve years as a VFX Pipeline TD at a major studio (DNEG, Framestore, MPC). Built traditional show pipelines in Python, managed artist tools, solved integration problems between DCCs. Around 2020 to 2022, started integrating ML models into pipeline tooling — initially as a side interest, eventually as primary work. By 2024, their role had effectively become an AI Pipeline TD even if the title hadn't caught up.

**Current situation:** Employed, stable, but likely underutilised. The organisation restructured around their skills without fully recognising or compensating for the specialisation. They have shipped AI tools that are actually in production use. Probably listed on internal Slack channels as "the AI person" for a team three times the size they can properly support.

**What would make them move:**
- A role that reflects what they actually do (title alignment matters)
- Salary recognition for the AI specialisation (a meaningful step up from pipeline TD rates)
- The chance to build a proper AI pipeline function from scratch or lead a team
- A studio or agency where AI is a strategic priority, not a bolt-on

**Where to find them:** LinkedIn Pipeline TD searches at target employers, The Pipeline Conference speaker and attendee lists, SIGGRAPH technical papers, GitHub contributors to production-facing AI repositories.

**What doesn't work:** Volume outreach with generic AI job descriptions. They know the difference between a role that understands what they do and one that has lifted keywords from their profile. The first message needs to demonstrate genuine familiarity with their actual pipeline context.

---

### Persona B: The Creative AI Pioneer

**Name archetype:** "The Early Adopter with a Portfolio"

**Background:** Senior art director or motion designer background. Started experimenting with generative AI tools in 2021 — not because a client asked for it, but because they found it technically fascinating. Built a visible portfolio on Instagram, Behance, or X. Got invited to speak at an event. Was hired as the "AI person" at an agency in 2023 or 2024. Now leading an AI creative team of two to four people. May have the title of Creative Director or AI Creative Lead.

**Current situation:** Often at a smaller or mid-size agency that doesn't have the infrastructure or budget to develop the practice at the pace they want to move. Has outgrown their current remit. Getting approached regularly but mostly for roles at companies where "AI" is a content marketing strategy, not a production capability.

**What would make them move:**
- A client base that gives them genuinely complex creative briefs — VFX, experiential, spatial computing rather than social content
- Autonomy and creative ownership over methodology
- A role where they're placed with clients who understand what they're buying (executive-level relationships, not procurement)
- A studio with existing technical infrastructure so they're not also doing the pipeline work themselves

**Where to find them:** Creative AI London meetup community, AI/art Instagram and X circles, conference speaker lists (SIGGRAPH, Adobe MAX, AI in Creative Production events), Hugging Face profiles with creative production context.

**What doesn't work:** Framing the role as "using AI to speed up existing workflows." They moved past that years ago. The pitch needs to acknowledge creative AI as its own discipline, not a productivity layer.

## Sourcing Channels

Specific communities and platforms in priority order.

### LinkedIn (primary)

Use employer-based sourcing alongside title search. Standard Boolean strings in the existing research document remain valid. The key layer to add: skills filters using PyTorch, ComfyUI, Stable Diffusion, Houdini, Unreal Engine. Geography filter to UK first.

### Creative AI London

Founded by Luba Elliott in 2016. The longest-running creative AI practitioner community in the UK. Monthly meetups. Attendees skew towards Archetype 3 (Generative AI Creative Director) and early-adopter technical artists. The Meetup.com event history and speaker lists are sourcing channels in their own right.

Community link: [Creative AI London — Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/creative-ai-london/)

### GitHub

Active contributors to generative AI repositories represent the highest-signal technical sourcing channel for Archetypes 2 and 4. Priority repos: ComfyUI, InvokeAI, Stable Diffusion WebUI (AUTOMATIC1111), ControlNet, AnimateDiff. Filter contributors with VFX software or production context in their profile bio.

### The Pipeline Conference

Annual conference for VFX and animation pipeline professionals. Speaker and attendee lists are a direct sourcing channel for Archetypes 1 and 2. Past talks on AI pipeline integration identify practitioners with delivered AI pipeline work.

Community: [thepipelineconference.com](https://thepipelineconference.com)

### SIGGRAPH / SIGGRAPH Asia

Annual graphics conference. Technical Papers and Production Sessions are where AI-in-production research and delivered pipeline work surfaces publicly. Authors of production-relevant AI papers are a sourcing signal for Archetype 2 and 7.

### NeurIPS Creativity and Design Workshop

Academic conference workshop focused specifically on creative AI research. Participants and paper authors are typically genuine AI-native practitioners rather than tool adopters. Sourcing signal for Archetypes 3 and 7.

### Hugging Face

Model authors and Space creators with VFX or production context in their profile bio. The community activity around diffusion models, ControlNet, and video generation surfaces practitioners who are building, not just using.

### Discord Servers

Specific servers with relevant practitioner populations:

- **Stable Diffusion** server (high volume — filter for production context)
- **ComfyUI** server (more focused on pipeline practitioners)
- **PromptHero / Civitai** communities for Archetype 3 creative practitioners
- **Unreal Engine** developer communities for Archetypes 5 and 6
- **XR Association Discord** for Archetype 8 (spatial computing)

### Industry Events (UK)

- **FMX** (Stuttgart, May) — VFX, animation, and AI in production
- **Develop: Brighton** — games and real-time pipeline talent
- **BAFTA AI events** — senior creative AI practitioners
- **AI Summit London** — cross-industry, filter for creative applications

## Outreach Hooks

What actually resonates with this audience. These are pull factors confirmed through the existing research and market context.

### "The studio culture argument"

The most consistent motivator for senior practitioners considering a move away from big tech: creative technology practitioners in VFX, experiential, and spatial computing often prefer studio culture over tech company culture — project variety, creative ambition, and proximity to craft. When Meta or Adobe approaches them, the work is often training data labelling or product support. The studio context offers production of actual output.

Use this when: the candidate is currently at a big tech company or known to be receiving tech company approaches. Frame FPZ's clients as the employers who will actually let them make things.

### "Your specialism finally has a title"

Many of the best AI pipeline practitioners have been doing this work under legacy titles. A Head of AI Production or AI Pipeline TD credit is genuinely meaningful for their career narrative — it formalises what they've been doing.

Use this when: the candidate's LinkedIn title doesn't match their actual work. This creates an opening: "We saw what you've actually been building. We have a client with a role where that's the explicit scope."

### "We search, not list"

FPZ runs retained searches with network-only sourcing. The candidate will not be one of forty who applied. The client engaged specifically to find someone like them.

Use this when: contacting practitioners who are highly employed and not actively looking. This separates FPZ from contingency recruiters.

### "They need someone who has shipped it, not studied it"

Client briefs at this level require demonstrated delivery — not theoretical familiarity. The candidate who has shipped AI tools into production at a known studio carries more commercial value than a candidate with credentials.

Use this when: the candidate has verifiable delivery history. The opening message can acknowledge specific work by name.

### "VFX sector disruption creates upside"

The 2023 to 2024 VFX industry contractions (DNEG Montreal, Framestore Vancouver) created real movement in the senior talent pool. Some practitioners who were displaced landed quickly; others are working freelance and open to the right senior opportunity. The disruption is also creating urgency in clients who recognise that the available pool won't stay available.

Use this when: relevant to the candidate's background or to a client's urgency framing.
