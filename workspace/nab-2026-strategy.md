## 1. What Martyn Says When Asked "What Does FourPointZero Do?"

Use this when meeting someone cold at NAB. It works for vendor reps, studio attendees, and press.

> "FourPointZero is a specialist recruitment firm for senior leadership in creative production and emerging technology. We've been placing talent at VFX studios, spatial computing companies, and creative software companies for six years. We just launched CreativAI — a dedicated practice for the leadership roles at the intersection of AI and production workflow. The people who run AI-integrated pipelines, not just the engineers who build them."

Adjust the last two sentences depending on whether they know what CreativAI is. If they do, skip the intro and go straight to the production leadership point.

**What to avoid:** Don't lead with "executive search firm." It closes the conversation. Lead with the sector and the problem. The search part is the mechanic, not the value.

## 2. How to Introduce CreativAI Without It Becoming a Pitch

The rule: ask first, name it second.

The pattern:
1. Ask what they're building or what's changed in their team structure recently.
2. Listen for the hiring tension — roles sitting open, internal capability gaps, "we don't have anyone who does both sides."
3. Only then introduce CreativAI as the context for why you're curious.

> "This is exactly the territory CreativAI was built for — we just launched a dedicated practice for this specific hiring challenge. Which is partly why I was keen to understand what you're seeing."

At NAB specifically, the natural hook is the AI tools on the show floor. Every major vendor is demonstrating AI workflow integrations. Use that as the opener:

> "There are a lot of AI tools being shown here this week. The question I keep coming back to is who's actually going to run these inside the studio — not the vendor, but the person on the client side who owns the integration decision. That's the hire."

This positions you as an intelligent observer, not a recruiter trolling for mandates.

## 4. Three Conversation Hooks

Use one of these to open. All three work as questions or observations, not pitches.

---

**Hook 1 — The tools vs. people gap**

> "NAB is the place where you see which AI tools studios are actually buying. But the harder question is who they're hiring to run them. There's a real gap between 'we've licensed the tool' and 'we have someone who can lead the integration.' That gap is widening, not closing."

Works with: anyone on the vendor or client side. Opens a genuine conversation about hiring readiness.

---

**Hook 2 — The dual-competency problem**

> "The brief we keep getting — from VFX houses, from software companies, from media groups — is 'we need someone who understands both the AI pipeline and the production workflow.' That person exists, but the pool is genuinely small. And most recruiters can only see half of them."

Works with: studio technology leads, HR and people leads at content companies, software vendor heads. Positions FPZ as the firm with the differentiated lens.

---

**Hook 3 — The show floor observation**

> "What's interesting about walking the floor here is you can see exactly which production problems AI is being thrown at. Every vendor has an answer. What none of them have is the leadership layer — who inside the studio actually governs this, makes the quality decisions, manages the risk. That's a hiring brief, not a technology brief."

Works with: press, analysts, creative software leaders. Signals sector intelligence, not a pitch.
