## Post 1 — What "Head of AI" Actually Means Right Now

**Pillar: Market Intelligence | Audience: Client-facing (Heads of People, Studio Directors) | Schedule: Tue 26 May**
**Hook type: Contrarian/Myth-Bust | Structure: Contrarian Rant**

---

"Head of AI" means something different in every brief I receive.

In some studios, it means integrating generative tools into an existing production pipeline.

In others, it means building a function from nothing: defining team structure, choosing tooling, establishing workflow, managing risk.

Those are not the same role.

The job title has outpaced the shared understanding of what the job actually is.

I have spent the last few months mapping this across real briefs. The pattern is consistent.

Studios that describe the output first find the right person faster.

They tell me what the function needs to deliver in twelve months. What success looks like. What failure looks like.

Studios that lead with the title spend longer shortlisting. Candidates self-select on job title and self-deselect for the wrong reasons.

The problem is not a shortage of AI talent.

It is a brief that over-specifies tooling and under-specifies accountability.

If your AI role has been open longer than expected, the brief is usually where to start.

What does your brief lead with?

*(~164 words)*

P.S. Happy to review a brief informally — no obligation. Sometimes a fresh perspective from outside the studio is enough to unblock the search.

## Post 2 — The Sleeper AI Hire Category of 2026

**Pillar: Market Intelligence / Talent Intelligence | Audience: Client-facing (Studio Directors, Founders) | Schedule: Thu 28 May**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Observation-Pattern-Implication-CTA**

---

Spatial computing roles are the most underestimated AI hire category in 2026.

Not because nobody wants them.

Because everyone is looking at the same small group of candidates.

The talent pool working at the intersection of spatial computing and AI production is genuinely narrow. Narrower than most studios realise until a brief lands.

The issue is not candidate quality. The people working in this space are exceptional.

The issue is that the pool has not grown proportionally to the demand.

Two years ago, spatial computing briefs came primarily from studios running AR and location-based experience projects.

Now I am also receiving briefs from post-production houses, broadcast technology teams, and enterprise product studios that have not historically recruited in this space.

The pool is the same size. The demand has expanded.

Candidates who understand both spatial computing and AI-integrated production workflows are already in conversation with multiple studios.

The studios with active candidate relationships in this category are filling roles.

The ones building that relationship from scratch are waiting longer.

Is spatial computing in your 2026 headcount plans?

## Post 3 — What Happens Between "We Have a Brief" and "We Made a Hire"

**Pillar: Practice Observation / FPZ Process | Audience: Both (credibility-building for clients, transparency for candidates) | Schedule: Fri 30 May**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Problem-Agitate-Solution**

---

Most people assume recruitment works like a job board.

Post the brief. Wait for applications. Pick someone.

That is not how senior creative tech placement works.

When a brief arrives, the first thing I do is not search a database.

It is to ask who already knows this type of work.

Seven years of placements across experiential, real-time, spatial computing, and AI-integrated production means the conversation starts in the network.

The shortlist comes from direct conversations with people who are not actively searching. Who may not have updated their LinkedIn in months. Who are delivering projects and have not yet thought about the next move.

That is the candidate most studios cannot reach independently.

Brief to first shortlist typically takes three to five weeks for senior roles. First shortlist conversation to offer accepted usually adds another two to four weeks, depending on notice periods.

Senior hiring is not fast. But it is more predictable when the network already exists.

If you have a brief you are sitting on, I can give you a realistic timeline before we start.

*(~190 words)*

P.S. The first conversation is always exploratory. The only question is whether the brief is one we would take on.

## Post 4 — What NAB 2026 Signalled About Where Talent Demand Is Heading

**Pillar: Market Intelligence / Industry Observation | Audience: Both | Schedule: Flex — Tue 2 Jun or later (⚠️ Martyn to review before publishing — see note below)**
**Hook type: Bold Personal Declaration | Structure: Observation-Pattern-Implication-CTA**

> ⚠️ **Publishing note:** This post is directional based on known trends around broadcast AI, virtual production, and real-time pipelines at NAB-era industry events. Martyn to add or confirm any specific observations from reviewing the NAB 2026 programme before publishing. Post stands without additions but will be stronger with one or two concrete specifics.

---

I was at NAB 2026 a few weeks ago. I have been processing it since.

I do not attend as a technology reviewer.

I attend as a recruiter mapping where production investment is concentrating.

The pattern this year was consistent with what I have been seeing in briefs.

AI in production was not the novelty track.

It was embedded in infrastructure conversations: broadcast workflow automation, virtual production pipelines, real-time rendering at scale.

The studios taking it seriously are not asking whether AI belongs in production.

They are asking who builds the function, who owns the workflow, and how you hire for a discipline that shifts every twelve months.

That is a talent question, not a technology question.

The gap between studios deploying AI in production and studios still evaluating it has widened since this time last year. The briefs reflect that.

The studios moving fastest have senior leadership who understand both the creative output and the production infrastructure underneath it.

That combination remains the hardest thing to hire.

If you are thinking about your AI production capability build, it is worth a conversation before the next brief is live.
