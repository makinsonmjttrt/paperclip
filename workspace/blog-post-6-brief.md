## Topic and angle

Spatial computing is the one area of creative technology where the AI hiring problem is compounded. In VFX or experiential production, studios can look for someone with production depth and AI skills developed on top. In spatial computing, the field itself was still maturing during the same years AI capability arrived. This means the candidate who has genuine spatial computing leadership experience AND real AI production fluency is genuinely rare — not just hard to find.

The angle: the "double frontier" problem. Both spatial computing and AI were emerging fields at the same time. The people who have real depth in both are a small cohort. They were building spatial experiences when no one was watching, before the commercial wave. Most studios don't know who they are. FourPointZero does — because the relationships predate the market.

This frames FPZ's spatial computing network (AIXR, VRARA, six years of placement history) as specific, demonstrable credibility — not a claim, a consequence of timeline.

## Proposed structure (H2s)

### H2 1: The spatial computing AI gap is different from the VFX AI gap

Establish the distinction early. VFX had decades of pipeline maturity before AI arrived. Spatial computing was itself a frontier technology at the same time as AI. Both disciplines were developing simultaneously — which means very few people have genuine depth in both. This is not a typical skills shortage. It is a structural consequence of timing.

### H2 2: What these roles actually require

Walk through what a senior AI role in spatial computing actually demands. Not a skills list — a picture of the work. Typical brief: someone who can own the AI integration layer in a spatial computing production (whether that is enterprise AR, location-based XR, virtual production with spatial delivery, or consumer platform content). They need to understand both the spatial layer (real-time engines, spatial audio, headset pipeline constraints) AND the AI layer (generative content, ML-assisted environment design, AI-driven personalisation in spatial contexts). The brief that lands on paper often looks like a technical director role. The brief that actually needs filling is a creative technology leadership role.

### H2 3: Why the candidate pool is smaller than studios assume

Return to the timing argument with evidence. The people who have both spatial computing and AI production depth were building spatial experiences 2019–2022 when commercial appetite was limited. They weren't building CVs — they were building things. Many of them are at the studios, technology companies, or research labs that were early in the category. They are not on job boards. The signal-to-noise problem in outreach to this pool is extreme because most outreach comes from recruiters with no recognisable presence in the community.

### H2 4: The AIXR and VRARA problem

The professional communities where spatial computing talent actually congregates — AIXR, VRARA, and the events ecosystem around them — are invisible to most AI recruiters and most generalist agencies. FPZ has been present in those communities since before the AI wave hit the sector. The consequence: relationships that predate the current demand. When a senior spatial computing AI leader gets an approach from FourPointZero, they are not being contacted by a stranger. The relationship usually already exists.

This section should not be a pitch. Frame it as "this is what makes sourcing in this space work" — and let the reader draw the conclusion.

### H2 5: What to do if you have this brief

Practical close. If you have a senior AI role in a spatial computing or XR production environment, three things are worth knowing: (1) The brief definition matters more than usual — roles written by technology functions look entirely different from roles written by creative teams, and neither reliably finds the right person; (2) the timeline is longer than most studios expect if the search starts from cold — 10–14 weeks for a qualified shortlist is realistic; (3) starting the conversation early matters more here than in most sectors because the candidate is likely already engaged with one or two other opportunities by the time a formal mandate opens.

CTA: reply or contact for a 15-minute scoping call.

What they do NOT cover:

The double-frontier timing argument.** No competitor is making the specific point that spatial computing and AI matured simultaneously, creating a structurally smaller candidate pool at the intersection. This is accurate, non-obvious, and positions FPZ's existing spatial network as a strategic advantage.

**2. AIXR/VRARA as sourcing channels.** Generalist AI recruiters have no presence in these communities. Naming them specifically signals credibility that cannot be faked. A studio reading this will immediately understand whether their recruiter knows these communities.

**3. The brief-writing problem is different in spatial computing.** Other posts (including FPZ's own) talk about how studios write bad briefs for AI roles. In spatial computing, the brief problem is more severe: the two most common brief-writers (technology function vs. creative production function) produce documents so different that they can look like different roles. This is a specific, recognisable pain point for studios that have tried to hire in this space.
