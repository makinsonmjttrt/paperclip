## 1. The Gap We Own

**One sentence no competitor can credibly say:**

> We are the only executive search practice that places the senior operators who run AI-integrated creative production — the talent that AI recruiters don't know and media recruiters can't assess.

**Why it's defensible:**
Every competitor operates on one side of the boundary. AI/tech recruiters (Digitalent, Xcede, DeepRec) understand AI credentials but have no production sector relationships and no framework for assessing creative delivery. Media/production recruiters (Searchlight, Talent Outpost) have sector depth but zero capability to evaluate AI workflow competence. No firm has six years of senior placements across VFX, experiential, spatial, and real-time *and* the ability to vet candidates on AI delivery under production constraints.

## 2. Proof Points

Three specific things FPZ does that no competitor can claim:

**1. Network depth in the right niche, built over time**
FPZ has been placing senior creative production talent since 2019 — before AI was a hiring consideration in these sectors. The Tier 1 AI-in-production talent at ILM, DNEG, Framestore, Adobe, and Foundry know us. That relationship base took six years to build. An AI recruiter pivoting to cover creative production can't replicate it by buying a LinkedIn Recruiter licence. No competitor in the analysis has placed a single senior role in VFX, spatial computing, experiential, or real-time production.

**2. Both-sides-of-the-market placement**
We fill roles at the organisations whose talent we also represent. That dual visibility — client-side and candidate-side — means our shortlists are built on genuine knowledge of who is available, what they're worth, and which organisations they'd move to. Competitors who source only from one side (tech recruiters who have never worked a studio brief; media recruiters who have never assessed AI tool fluency) produce shortlists that fail on the first credibility check.

**3. Sector embeddedness, not just sector presence**
APSCO, VRARA, and AIXR memberships, plus a weekly industry intelligence newsletter with active readership. This is industry infrastructure, not marketing. It means we see career moves, studio announcements, and technology shifts before they reach the public domain. Competitors who rely on scraping job boards and watching LinkedIn are always behind this information flow.

## 3. What We Never Say

Three competitor claims we deliberately avoid — and why:

**"We specialise in AI recruitment"**
*Rationale:* This is Digitalent and DeepRec territory. It positions FPZ as a technology recruiter placing engineers, which is the opposite of what we do. Using this framing invites comparison on metrics we can't win (fill rates for ML engineers, FAANG placements, scale). It also misrepresents the candidate profile — the people we place are production leaders, not AI researchers or ML engineers.

**"We have a database/community of [X]"**
*Rationale:* Searchlight claims 40,000+ contacts. DeepRec claims 100,000+ community members. Scale claims signal breadth, not depth. Our value is the opposite: a small, accurately-mapped pool of senior creative technology leaders who are known quantities, not a large database of passive scraped profiles. Claiming a big number invites volume comparison with firms that exist in a completely different market segment.

**"We place at all levels"**
*Rationale:* Talent Outpost does crew-level and HOD. Xcede does everything from researcher to VP. Positioning across multiple seniority bands erodes executive search credibility. Clients at director-level and above want to work with a firm that only operates at that level. The moment FPZ signals it also fills junior briefs, the premium positioning weakens. Every role in the CreativAI practice is director-level and above, £150K+ total comp.

## 5. Objection Responses

Top 3 "why not go with X instead" objections:

---

**"Why not use a specialist AI recruiter like Digitalent?"**

They place ML engineers and AI technical leaders in technology companies. If you're hiring for a VP of Production at a VFX studio, they'll shortlist on AI credentials and miss the production delivery piece entirely. Every candidate they send will have strong AI expertise and no production floor experience. We shortlist on both. The candidates we present have run pipelines, managed crews, and delivered under production pressure — and they understand where AI fits in that workflow. These are different people and a different search.

---

**"Why not use an entertainment search firm like Searchlight?"**

Searchlight knows the traditional media structure: broadcast, film, distribution. They have 25+ years of relationships with broadcasters and traditional studios. But they have no framework for assessing candidates on AI workflow delivery. If you need a Head of Production who can run a studio using AI-integrated tools — Unreal, Nuke, ComfyUI — they'll send you credible EPs who've never worked in that environment. You'll find that out in the third interview. We find it out before the first.

---

**"Why not just use our own network and save the fee?"**

This works if your brief is a well-defined senior role with a large candidate pool. At the AI-creative production intersection, the qualified senior candidates are a small population and largely passive. They're not responding to job adverts or checking LinkedIn. The people who can actually deliver — who've run AI-integrated production at a serious level — are employed, not looking, and reachable only through direct relationships. We've been building those relationships since 2019. Six years of placements, introductions, and industry presence means we can open doors your network doesn't reach.
