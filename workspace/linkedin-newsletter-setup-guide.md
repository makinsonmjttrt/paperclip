## Personal Profile vs Company Page — Read This First

LinkedIn Newsletters behave differently depending on where you create them.

**Personal profile (Martyn's profile) — recommended:**
Creator Mode must be active. When you publish your first newsletter issue, LinkedIn sends a notification to all your connections and followers inviting them to subscribe. Notifications for each subsequent issue go directly to subscribers. This is the highest-reach route and positions Signal as Martyn's practitioner voice with FPZ as the publishing brand — which is accurate and more effective.

**Company Page (FPZ page):**
Company Pages can create newsletters, but the notification mechanics are less prominent and availability varies by page follower count and engagement history. If the option isn't visible in your Page admin panel, the page doesn't yet qualify.

**Verdict:** Use Martyn's personal profile. Signal is written from Martyn's perspective. The personal profile is the better distribution vehicle and the simpler setup path.

## Step 1 — Enable Creator Mode

If Creator Mode is already on, skip to Step 2. To check: your profile's primary button should say "Follow" rather than "Connect."

1. Go to your LinkedIn profile
2. Scroll to the "Resources" section below your profile card
3. Click "Creator mode" and toggle it on
4. Confirm when prompted

Creator Mode unlocks newsletters, live audio events, and a dedicated analytics dashboard.

---

## Step 2 — Create the Newsletter

1. From the LinkedIn homepage feed, click **Write article** (alongside "Post" and "Find expert" at the top)
2. In the article editor, look for **Create a newsletter** in the top-left area, near the LinkedIn logo
3. A setup dialog will open — fill in the fields below before writing your first issue

1. Use one of the subject line options above as your article headline

## How LinkedIn Newsletter Differs from Beehiiv (Email)

Understanding this prevents formatting mistakes and sets realistic expectations.

| | **LinkedIn Newsletter** | **Beehiiv (email)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Format** | LinkedIn article (no custom CSS) | HTML email (full styling control) |
| **Distribution** | In-app notification + LinkedIn email notification | Subscriber's inbox |
| **Subscriber acquisition** | Opt-in from connections, followers, and LinkedIn search | Direct subscribe via landing page |
| **Analytics** | Views, reactions, comments, subscriber count | Opens, clicks, unsubscribes |
| **Audience** | LinkedIn users who follow or connect | Subscribers who gave their email |
| **Algorithm reach** | Articles can be surfaced to non-subscribers via LinkedIn feed | No organic algorithm uplift |

**Key adjustments when repurposing from Beehiiv to LinkedIn:**

- **Remove** Beehiiv tracking links and any unsubscribe/manage preferences language
- **Remove** preview text metadata (LinkedIn doesn't use it)
- **Replace** email-style dividers with H2 headers
- **Replace** Beehiiv subscribe CTAs with the LinkedIn newsletter subscribe CTA and Beehiiv cross-link (see Step 4 closing note template)
- **Keep** all substantive content, pull quotes, and analysis exactly as written — the voice and length translate directly

**These are parallel channels, not replacements.** The audiences overlap but are not identical. A subscriber on Beehiiv may not follow you on LinkedIn, and vice versa. Running both maximises reach without duplication of effort.

## Step 5 — Growth Tactics (Under 30 Minutes Per Month)

### At each issue (10 minutes per issue)

**Publish the LinkedIn Newsletter issue first**, then:

Post a feed post the same day with one key insight from the issue. End with:
> *Full issue in Signal — subscribe on LinkedIn (newsletter tab on my profile) or Beehiiv: [INSERT_BEEHIIV_LINK]*

The newsletter notification and the feed post are two separate distribution events. You get both from one piece of content.

### Monthly (15 minutes)

- Open Newsletter Analytics, check subscriber count and trend
- Reply to any comments on newsletter articles from the past month — comments on newsletter issues carry higher algorithm weight than comments on regular posts, and responding keeps the article in distribution

### One-time setup (5 minutes)

**About section:** Add to the closing paragraph:
> I publish Signal — weekly intelligence on AI, creative tech, and the people shaping both — on LinkedIn and Beehiiv. Follow this newsletter or subscribe via the link in my profile.

**Featured section:** Add the LinkedIn Newsletter link as the first slot (ahead of the Beehiiv link). LinkedIn users who find you organically can subscribe without leaving the platform.

## Step 6 — Metrics to Watch

LinkedIn Newsletter Analytics is in your profile under **Creator Mode > Newsletter**.

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Subscriber count** | Primary metric. Track the week-on-week growth rate, not just the absolute number |
| **Views per issue** | How many subscribers opened and read the article. A rough engagement quality signal |
| **Reactions and comments** | High comment counts drive algorithm distribution — LinkedIn surfaces active articles to non-subscribers |
| **Email notification open rate** | LinkedIn sends email notifications to subscribers with email preferences enabled. This appears in analytics and tends to run well above typical B2B email open rates because the subscriber opted in intentionally |

### Benchmarks

LinkedIn does not publish official benchmark data for newsletter performance. Directionally:

- LinkedIn notification open rates run significantly higher than traditional email newsletters — the notification goes into an inbox the subscriber actively uses
- Treat your own trend line as the benchmark. Consistent week-on-week growth in subscribers, even at single digits per week, is what success looks like in the first quarter

**What good looks like in the first 90 days:**
- Subscriber count growing each week (absolute number matters less than direction)
- Views per issue at 30–50% of subscriber count minimum
- At least one substantive comment per issue
