# LinkedIn Post: AI Hiring Trends in VFX Studios
**Brief:** FOU-268 (sub-task of FOU-263 VALIDATION E2E-01)
**Format:** Thought leadership post, ~310 words
**Target audience:** Senior leaders in VFX studios and creative tech studios
**Status:** Humaniser pass complete. Awaiting Quality Reviewer gate.

---

Something shifted in VFX hiring conversations over the last year. Twelve months ago, the question was whether to hire dedicated AI leadership at all. Now it's: we know we need someone, but we can't agree on what the role looks like.

That's progress. It's also where most of the difficulty starts.

After six months of working through these briefs, a few things keep coming up.

The titles are a mess. Head of ML, Director of AI Production, VP AI Engineering, Head of Generative Content. Same role, two competing studios, completely different briefs behind identical titles. Nobody's settled on a standard, which creates problems on both sides: candidates can't tell if the brief fits their profile, studios can't filter applications accurately.

There's also a real split between what different studios actually need, and a lot of them haven't worked it out before they start looking. Some need someone to build and own the AI infrastructure: training pipelines, tooling, the integration layer. Others need someone sitting closer to the creative team, making existing AI tools actually work inside real production workflows. Different problems, different candidates. Most people are strong on one side.

The people who matter most aren't advertising. The senior candidates who've shipped AI inside a working production pipeline, not in a lab, not a demo, but under an actual deadline, are employed. They're not on job boards. Most of them get contacted weekly by recruiters who can't assess whether the brief is even close.

And a lot of this is happening quietly. Post-WGA/SAG, studios got cautious about visible AI announcements. Several significant appointments over the last six months went through networks entirely. No posting, no announcement. The market is more active than it looks from outside.

The studios getting this right are starting before they're under pressure and getting the brief right before they open it. The ones struggling are either trying to hire generalists where they need specialists, or they've written a brief so specific it describes nobody who currently exists.

If you're working out what kind of AI hire your studio actually needs, happy to share what we're seeing.
