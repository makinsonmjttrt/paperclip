## 5. Top 3 Posts by Predicted Engagement Pattern

### #1 — Post 4: "The Creative Director role is quietly splitting into two different jobs" (Fri 11 Apr, P1)

**Why this will outperform:** The hook creates immediate professional identity tension — anyone in a CD-adjacent role stops to check which category they fall into. The bold role labels ("craft executor" vs "systems thinker") give people a vocabulary to use in replies. The binary CTA question ("hiring for the role that exists or the role that existed?") is impossible to answer neutrally, which drives comment volume. This is the only post in the batch that was independently validated before publication.

**Predicted outcome:** Highest comments and shares of the twelve. Strong save rate.

---

### #2 — Post 7: "The studios getting AI wrong aren't using too little of it" (Thu 17 Apr, P1)

**Why this will outperform:** The misconception flip in the first line works immediately — most readers would expect the opposite argument. "AI at the edges. Analogue at the core. That's not an AI pipeline. It's decoration." is structurally distinctive — short sentences, clear verdict, no hedging. The post stays anchored in a specific production reality (pipeline vs asset level) rather than abstract commentary. The question CTA is genuine, not rhetorical.

**Predicted outcome:** High save and share rate. Likely to attract VFX production professional comments with real specifics — which drives further distribution.

---

### #3 — Post 6: "Before you repost that job spec, ask yourself five questions" (Tue 15 Apr, P3)

**Why this will outperform:** Lists have the highest save rate on LinkedIn per the strategy's own benchmark data. The "Drop a number" CTA is the only gamified engagement mechanic in the twelve posts — it removes the effort barrier to a comment (one keystroke vs a full reply). Each of the five questions lands on a genuine pain point that a hiring decision-maker recognises without needing to be convinced. The P.S. ("I ask these in every retained briefing") frames FPZ as practitioner, not commentator.

**Predicted outcome:** Highest save rate of the twelve. Comment volume driven by "Drop a number" mechanic. Lower shares than Posts 4 or 7 but stronger net engagement-to-impression ratio.

## 6. Bottom 3 Posts by Predicted Engagement Pattern

### #10 — Post 5: "The CAIO title didn't exist at creative companies three years ago" (Mon 14 Apr, P4)

**Why this will underperform:** The hook is accurate but not tension-creating — it describes a trend rather than challenging an assumption. The post pivots to explicit CreativAI product positioning mid-body ("That gap is exactly why we built the CreativAI practice at FourPointZero"). LinkedIn's algorithm is known to suppress posts that read as promotional before the CTA. The implicit CTA ("that's a conversation worth having early") is the softest close in the batch — no question to answer, no number to drop.

**What to avoid repeating:** Mid-post brand mentions. Product positioning belongs in the P.S. line.

---

### #11 — Post 9: "Spatial computing has an industry. It doesn't yet have a talent pipeline to match." (Mon 21 Apr, P4)

**Why this will underperform:** Spatial computing is a narrower audience interest than VFX AI transformation or hiring strategy — the addressable pool of people who immediately recognise the problem is smaller. The CreativAI practice is named mid-post again. The hook is declarative rather than provocative. The post makes a good argument but doesn't ask the audience to reconsider an assumption they already hold.

**What to avoid repeating:** Two consecutive Monday P4 posts with explicit brand mentions and declarative (rather than contrarian) hooks.

---

### #12 — Post 1: "Studios are all looking for the same profile..." (Mon 7 Apr, P2)

*Note: Full copy not available; assessed from strategy brief description.*

**Why this may underperform:** The hook is strong and the "75 people globally" number is a genuine pattern disruptor. However, the closing CTA described in the strategy ("What's your experience been?") is the most generic question format in the batch. Without full copy review, the risk is that the post builds well but lands on a CTA that doesn't differentiate who should answer or why. Generic experiential questions produce fewer responses than specific, answerable challenges.

**What to avoid repeating:** Ending strong hooks with "What's your experience?" without a specific dimension to answer against.

## 7. Five Recommendations for Batches 4+

### 1. Keep the contrarian format as the Thursday anchor

Posts 4, 7, and 11 are all Thursday posts, all contrarian in structure, and all predicted to be top performers. The Thursday slot is earning its position as the highest-leverage post of the week. Do not shift contrarian content to other days. Build a reserve of 2–3 contrarian drafts ahead of schedule to protect the Thursday slot from calendar slippage.

---

### 2. Move all brand and product mentions to P.S. lines

Posts 5 and 9 both name CreativAI explicitly mid-post. Posts 7, 8, 10, 11, and 12 keep the brand close entirely to the P.S. The P.S. format performs better for positioning because it reads as an afterthought from the author rather than a pitch — which is exactly the tone that builds trust with senior buyers. Enforce this as a non-negotiable rule from Batch 4 onwards.

---

### 3. Increase personal story format from 8% to at least 20%

Post 8 is the only genuine personal story in the twelve posts. First-person narrative consistently produces higher comment-to-impression ratios on LinkedIn for professional audiences because it signals authenticity and invites identification. The strategy target is 10% — that should be treated as a floor, not a target. Batch 4 should include at least two personal story anchors: one mid-week (Wednesday or Thursday) and one Friday.

---

### 4. Replace Monday Pillar 4 with Monday Pillar 2

Both Monday posts (5 and 9) are P4 Industry Signal posts with declarative hooks and mid-post brand mentions — a combination that produces the weakest predicted outcomes in the batch. The strategy's Monday allocation is "Industry Intelligence — Pillar 2 or 4." Lean into Pillar 2 (Talent Market Intelligence) for Monday. Market intelligence posts have tighter audience fit and produce more specific engagement from the people FPZ most needs to reach: hiring decision-makers who recognise the talent problem being described.

---

### 5. Test the "Drop a number" mechanic on Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 posts

Post 6 is the only post in the twelve with a gamified CTA. The "Drop a number" or "Which applies to you: 1, 2, or 3?" format removes the cognitive load of forming a reply — which means more comments from people who would otherwise scroll past. This works best when the numbered options map to a genuine decision the audience recognises: hiring situation, pipeline stage, leadership style. A Pillar 1 post identifying three distinct CD archetypes or a Pillar 2 post categorising three types of hiring behaviour would fit this format naturally. Test one per batch until there is engagement data to confirm whether it outperforms the open-question format.
