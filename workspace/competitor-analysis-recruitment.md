## Competitor Profiles

### 1. Digitalent
**Website:** [digitalent.agency](https://www.digitalent.agency)
**Positioning:** "Specialist executive search for AI and Machine Learning teams" — serving VC-backed startups and high-growth companies.

**Sectors and roles:** AI/ML technical teams at all levels, from founding engineers through VP and C-suite. Operates across US, UK, and Australia. Clients include technology startups and scale-ups.

**Content/LinkedIn:** Active — founder Chris Morrow holds a LinkedIn Top Voice badge (2024), has been featured in The Guardian and CNN. Strong personal brand around AI hiring expertise.

**Differentiators:** Track record metrics (92% fill rate, NPS of 96), technical depth in AI/ML, advisory layer including an "AI Readiness Advisory" offering.

**Gaps:**
- Entirely technology-company focused — no studio, agency, or production house clients
- No understanding of creative workflows, production pipelines, or the studio model
- Sells AI engineer hiring, not the placement of leaders who manage the human-AI creative transition
- No experiential, spatial computing, or real-time sector presence

### 2. Xcede
**Website:** [xcede.com](https://www.xcede.com)
**Positioning:** "Technology recruitment specialists" covering data, AI, machine learning, cloud, cyber, and software engineering since 2003.

**Sectors and roles:** Financial services, defence, healthcare, telecoms. Technical AI roles — machine learning engineers, data scientists, computer vision researchers, AI product managers.

**Content/LinkedIn:** Moderate presence; thought leadership around data and AI trends.

**Differentiators:** 20+ years sector history; claims to have been the first recruiters in the UK to specialise in data science; academic institution relationships.

**Gaps:**
- Serves regulated enterprise sectors only — no creative or media presence
- Volume-driven contingency model unlikely to suit boutique creative studio briefs
- No understanding of production craft, experiential formats, or emerging creative technology
- Positions AI as a functional discipline, not as a workflow change across creative industries

**Sectors and roles:** Broadcast, film, TV, distribution, gaming, sports, music, live/immersive experiences, digital media platforms. Dual service: mid-level recruitment (Searchlight Recruitment) and senior executive search (Searchlight Executive).

**Content/LinkedIn:** Established presence; trade press visible; reputation in traditional media circles.

**Differentiators:** 40,000+ contact database; longest-standing specialist in media and entertainment; integrated recruitment and executive search.

**Gaps:**
- Explicitly "media and entertainment" framing covers traditional structures — not AI-integrated production, spatial computing, or real-time workflows
- No capability to vet candidates on AI pipeline fluency or emerging tech delivery
- Client base is primarily broadcasters, studios, and publishers — not the experiential agencies and immersive studios FPZ serves
- No tech-sector crossover; would not know who the creative technology talent is in the AI/spatial/real-time niche

## FPZ/CreativAI Positioning Gap

**What no competitor is saying:**

Every firm in this landscape either recruits AI/ML engineers for technology companies, or recruits creative and media talent for traditional entertainment structures. Not one positions itself at the specific intersection of:

1. **Senior leadership placement** (not crew, not engineers)
2. **Creative production expertise** (studios, agencies, experiential, immersive, spatial, real-time)
3. **AI workflow fluency** (candidates who understand how AI is entering production pipelines — not just "can code AI")
4. **Emerging format sectors** (spatial computing, real-time production, experiential, virtual production, AI-integrated post)

The gap is specific and ownable: **the executive search firm that places the production leaders running the transition from traditional craft to AI-integrated creative workflows.**

No one is talking about the *creative technology leader* as a distinct talent category — the person who can manage a VFX pipeline that uses AI on-set, or lead an experiential studio building with real-time and AI-generated assets, or run production at a spatial computing studio. AI recruiters assume this person is an engineer. Media and entertainment recruiters assume this person is a traditional producer. Neither has built a network of candidates who are both.

---

## Recommendation

Position CreativAI as the only executive search practice that recruits for **production leadership at the AI-creative frontier** — not "AI talent" (engineers) and not "creative talent" (traditional media roles), but the senior operators who sit where both disciplines converge.

The one claim to own: **"We place the leaders running AI-integrated creative production."**

This is defensible because:
- Six years of relationships in spatial, real-time, and experiential production already built
- APSCO, VRARA, and AIXR memberships demonstrate sector embeddedness no tech recruiter can match
- Competitors in AI recruitment have never hired for studio or agency roles; competitors in media recruitment have never assessed candidates on AI workflow delivery
- The candidate pool FPZ has access to (creative technology leaders in spatial computing, VFX, immersive, real-time) is not visible to any of the five firms above

CMO should use this gap as the core CreativAI GTM message: the briefest version is **"Executive search for the leaders building AI-creative studios."**

## Post-Launch Competitive Snapshot (April 2026)

**Horizon scan conducted:** 2026-04-09 | **Issue:** [FOU-492](/FOU/issues/FOU-492)

### No material change to the competitive landscape

Research across web sources and industry news (April 2026) confirms:

- None of the five firms profiled above have launched AI creative or production-specific divisions
- No new specialist AI creative tech recruitment firm has entered the UK market
- Generalist AI recruitment agencies (Harnham, Huxley) remain focused on technical AI/ML roles in enterprise tech, not creative production

### New entrant to monitor: IN2-AV Recruitment

**Website:** [in2avrecruitment.com](https://www.in2avrecruitment.com)
**Positioning:** Specialist AV and immersive/experiential AV recruiter — UK, EMEA, USA. Part of IN2 Resourcing Group, active since 2015.

**What they do:** Technical AV staffing — systems designers, solutions architects, project managers for leisure and themed systems integrators and experiential agencies. 40,000+ registered AV professionals database.

**Why they matter to FPZ:** They recruit into experiential agencies, which overlap with FPZ's client sector. However:
- Their roles are technical installation and systems — not senior leadership or production executives
- No AI workflow positioning; entirely hardware/systems integration focused
- Not a direct competitor for the creative technology leadership mandates FPZ handles
- Worth monitoring if they attempt a pivot to senior/creative roles

**Verdict:** Peripheral. Not a threat at FPZ's level today, but visible in the same client organisations.

### Market signal: gap remains intact

The absence of new entrants reinforces that the AI creative production leadership niche remains unclaimed. This is a window, not a permanent state — as AI adoption in studios accelerates, expect larger staffing groups to attempt this positioning. FPZ's advantage is the six-year network already built; speed to claim the positioning publicly (via CreativAI) is the priority.
