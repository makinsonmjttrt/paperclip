VFX studios keep writing AI briefs backwards.

The spec says: Python proficiency, ML pipeline experience, diffusion model knowledge.

What the brief actually needs: someone who understands how a VFX pipeline breaks when you introduce AI tools halfway through a show.

Those two profiles have almost no overlap.

The hires I see thriving in VFX AI roles right now didn't come from an AI-first background. They came from production.

Houdini TDs who built their own ComfyUI workflows.

Nuke compositors who reverse-engineered AI cleanup pipelines.

Unreal Engine operators who saw where real-time rendering and AI diffusion models intersect.

They absorbed the AI stack on top of ten years of production reality.

The hires that struggle came from the other direction. Strong ML credentials. Genuine AI delivery experience. Zero patience for the production politics, the legacy pipeline constraints, the "we've always done it this way in ShotGrid" conversations that slow everything down.

Year one becomes an education in why VFX is different from every other AI application environment.

The studios getting AI production right in 2026 didn't hire up. They looked inside their existing teams for the person who'd already started learning on their own time, then gave them the mandate, the budget, and the seniority to run with it.

Sometimes that's a promotion. Sometimes it's a lateral from a comparable studio.

Either way, the profile that works is 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻-𝗳𝗶𝗿𝘀𝘁, 𝗔𝗜-𝗳𝗹𝘂𝗲𝗻𝘁. Not AI-first, production-learning.

The brief hasn't caught up with that yet.

Are you hiring AI-first or production-first right now?

P.S. If your brief still starts with "strong Python preferred" and no mention of pipeline experience, it's worth a conversation before it goes to market.
