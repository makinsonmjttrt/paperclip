## Post 1 — CreativAI Practice: Soft Launch

**Pillar 1 | Role Evolution | Soft Launch Announcement**

---

For the past few months, the briefings coming in have been unlike anything from the first five years.

The titles are newer. Head of AI Production. CAIO. VP AI Workflows. AI Production Supervisor. The briefs are more specific. The candidate pool is the same 50 to 75 people globally it has always been.

What has changed is the urgency. Studios and platforms that spent two years watching the space are now hiring into it. The window they thought they had is closing faster than expected.

The problem we kept running into was framing. An AI search firm does not know production. A VFX search firm does not know AI leadership. Most mandates were falling between the two.

So we built something to sit squarely in that gap.

**CreativAI** is FourPointZero's dedicated practice for senior AI leadership in creative production. Our searches, candidate conversations, and market intelligence all focus on one intersection: where AI meets creative workflows.

Not AI broadly. Not creative production broadly. The specific overlap where the hardest roles to fill actually live.

If you are building a brief for a senior AI production role, or if you are a leader working at this intersection and want to stay connected, I would welcome the conversation.

P.S. We have been placing in this exact territory since 2019. The only thing that changed is now we have named it.

## Post 2 — FPZ 6-Year Network Story

**Pillar 2 | Talent Market Intelligence | Personal Credibility**

---

When a senior AI production search opens, the first question that matters is not "can we find candidates?"

It is "do we already know them?"

In six years of placing creative tech leadership, the mandates that close in eight weeks instead of six months share one consistent characteristic: the search firm had already mapped the candidate pool before the brief arrived.

That sounds straightforward. Very few firms are doing it.

AI leadership in creative production is not a large market. The people who have genuinely shipped AI in a Nuke, Houdini, or Unreal pipeline, at senior level, with a real delivery track record, are known individually across the industry.

They are not posting CVs. Most are not actively looking. The only way to reach them is through relationships built over years in the same events, conversations, and communities.

We have been at SIGGRAPH, VRARA, and AIXR from the beginning. Not as observers. As people who know the space and keep building the map.

The gap between a warm outreach and a cold InMail shows directly in response rates. When a mandate opens in AI creative production, that difference determines how long the search takes.

How early are you involving your search partner before a role opens?

P.S. The first placement I made into a real-time AI production role came from a candidate I had known for three years before the brief existed.

## Post 3 — AI Leadership Titles Evolving

**Pillar 1 | Role Evolution | Market Observation**

---

Three years ago, the senior AI production role at most creative companies did not exist.

Today it has at least eight titles.

Head of AI Production. CAIO. VP AI Workflows. AI Production Supervisor. Head of AI Strategy and Creative. Director of AI Integration. Chief Creative AI Officer. AI Executive Producer.

Most describe roughly the same job. A few describe something genuinely different. Some exist before the role itself has been defined.

The signal is clear: studios and agencies are hiring at senior level into this space now. The title disagreement is a side effect of how fast it is moving.

For candidates, the fragmentation creates noise. A search for senior AI production roles returns different postings depending entirely on which terminology a company chose that week.

For companies writing a brief, the title question matters more than it looks. The label signals who you are recruiting from, what status the role carries internally, and what kind of candidate you are likely to attract.

I have started asking studios what problem they are solving before we agree on a title. The answer usually changes the search.

What is the AI leadership title currently in use at your company?

P.S. The fastest searches happen when the brief describes the work. The title follows from that.

## Post 4 — Why Hiring AI Talent Fast Is Often Wrong

**Pillar 1 | Role Evolution | Contrarian Take**

---

The loudest advice in AI hiring right now is to move fast.

It is usually the wrong advice.

Speed in hiring solves one problem and creates two. You close the vacancy. You also increase the probability of hiring someone who cannot deliver in your specific environment, which takes another six months to surface.

The reason AI production searches fail is almost never candidate shortage.

It is brief misalignment from week one. Studios write for the role they imagined. Candidates apply for the role they project onto the title. Nobody tests whether those two descriptions match until interview stage three.

The searches that close well, and stay closed, start with a longer conversation about what the role actually is. Not what the title says. Not what the org chart requires. What the person hired into this role will need to deliver in the first twelve months.

That process takes two extra weeks at the front end.

It saves three months at the back end.

**Hiring fast when you have a clear brief** is entirely reasonable. Hiring fast when the brief is vague is a different calculation.

The pressure to move quickly on AI leadership is real. It should not skip the one step that determines whether the hire lands.

What does a well-written brief for a senior AI role look like in your experience?

P.S. The most expensive searches I have seen did not fail because of the fee. They failed because no one stopped to ask what the brief was actually for.
