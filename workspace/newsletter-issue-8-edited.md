The most interesting conversations from the first two weeks haven't come from the contacts we expected. The studios with active AI programmes, the ones already building internal capability, have largely been quiet. The calls that have actually happened have been with leadership teams at an earlier stage: organisations that know they need to act, but haven't yet agreed internally on what acting looks like.

That is a useful signal. The demand for CreativAI searches isn't coming from studios that have already figured it out. It is coming from the ones who haven't, and need someone who can help them get the brief right before the search opens.

## Why role definition is still the first obstacle

This was the founding observation behind CreativAI, and two weeks of conversations have confirmed it still holds.

Studios aren't struggling to find senior AI talent because the talent doesn't exist. They are struggling because the brief hasn't been resolved. The technology function writes a role that looks like a product or engineering hire. The creative function writes a role that looks like a creative director with a machine learning module bolted on. Neither version describes the actual job, and neither reliably finds the person who can do it.

The pattern is familiar from the shift to virtual production three or four years ago. The studios that hired well for that transition weren't the ones with the best job descriptions. They were the ones who took the time to ask: what does this role actually decide, every day, and what does someone need to be to make those decisions well?

The same question applies now. Most organisations haven't yet asked it in enough detail. That is where CreativAI's most useful work starts: before the search, not after it.

## What we calibrated after week one

One thing the first two weeks surfaced: stronger-than-expected interest from creative SaaS companies and technology vendors, not just studios and agencies.

The case for placing AI leadership talent inside a production studio is straightforward. The same conversation is happening at the companies that build the tools those studios use, and those organisations face an equally sharp version of the brief problem. The role at a technology vendor spans an even more complex mix of product, commercial, and creative credibility.

We are keeping that in view as the practice develops.

## What CreativAI looks like in the next 60 days

The focus for the next eight weeks is closing out the initial BD conversations and moving the strongest ones to formal brief stage. Alongside that, we are building the candidate pool for the role profiles we are seeing most often, particularly at Head of AI Production and Creative Technology Director level. By then, the aim is to have the first formal searches open and at shortlist stage.
