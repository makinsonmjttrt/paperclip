Three days in. Here's what's become clear.

Over the past six months I've spoken with more than 50 creative tech leaders about AI and hiring. I expected the conversation to be about speed of change, skills gaps, the pace of tool development. What I didn't expect was how many people said the same thing: nobody knows what these roles should look like.

Not just the title. Not the level, or what a sensible brief should actually say. Studios with 20 years of production experience are hiring an AI lead for the first time with nothing to reference. No predecessor. No function to inherit.

The person taking this role doesn't step into something that exists. They build it while running it. Which changes the brief completely. Technical skills matter, but they're secondary to the ability to operate in real ambiguity, earn credibility with a creative team who are watching, and make calls that will shape how the studio works for years.

I keep hearing the same line from different companies: "We know we need someone. We just don't know how to describe them."

That's what most of the CreativAI briefs look like right now.

If you're at that point, what's the hardest part of getting the brief onto paper?
