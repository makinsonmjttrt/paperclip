## The Event Season

There's a version of conference attendance that's about visibility. Stand, branded merchandise, a slot on the exhibition floor. That's not what these events are for FPZ.

The reason I go to NAB, Cannes Lions, and SIGGRAPH is simpler. These three events, spread across spring and summer, concentrate the decision-makers who are actively building AI-augmented production teams. Not all of them have briefs yet. Some don't know what they're building until they're standing in a corridor between sessions, describing a gap to someone who knows how to hear it. That conversation doesn't happen over email.

So the event season is, first and foremost, a listening exercise. What roles are forming? Which studios have moved from AI experimentation to committed headcount? Where are the briefs that haven't been written yet because no one has put a name to what they actually need?

That intelligence matters more than any market report. And CreativAI changes what we do with it.

---

**What CreativAI changes about the event-to-hire cycle**

Before CreativAI, the cycle worked like this: meet someone at an event, the relationship develops over months, eventually they have a brief, they call, we search, and the hire takes another eight to twelve weeks. Good outcome, but slow. The event was a relationship seed. The yield came later.

CreativAI compresses that. The talent pool is active and mapped before the event season starts. When a production technology director at SIGGRAPH describes the senior ML-to-production bridge role they've been trying to fill for six months, I'm not starting a search in response. I'm already thinking of three people in that pool who fit the profile. The meeting becomes a trigger, not a starting point.

That matters most for the roles that don't have settled titles yet. The person who bridges AI research capability and creative production leadership. The VP of AI in post-production who can hold the trust of a colourist and an ML engineer simultaneously. These aren't roles you find through a job board. They come from sustained proximity to the talent, which is what CreativAI is designed to build.

Events are where that proximity converts into real pipeline, faster than it did before.

**NAB 2026 — Las Vegas, 19–22 April**

NAB has expanded its AI footprint significantly for 2026, adding a second AI Pavilion. Every major toolmaker is positioning AI as workflow infrastructure rather than experimentation. What that signals for hiring is straightforward: the tools are accelerating, and the senior talent to run them at production scale is not keeping pace.

The gap between "we've bought the tooling" and "we have someone who can actually lead this" is precisely where FPZ operates. NAB is a useful week to see exactly how large that gap has become across the broadcast and production community.

**If you're at NAB and want to talk about AI production hiring, reach out directly.** I'll be on the ground all four days. Message me on LinkedIn or reply to this newsletter. I'll make time.

**Cannes Lions — Cannes, 22–26 June**

Cannes Lions added AI Craft as a formal subcategory for 2026, which is a signal worth taking seriously. When AI-generated creative work starts entering the awards conversation, studios and agencies face a specific leadership question: who owns the quality decision? Who is accountable for the output when the production chain includes generative tools?

That's a hiring brief. It's one that advertising and brand creative studios are beginning to articulate, often imprecisely, which is when it benefits from someone who knows how to translate the real need into the right search.

I'm not going to Cannes to chase advertising business. I'm going because that question is forming in those rooms, and the creative production and advertising worlds share more senior talent than either side usually acknowledges.

---

**SIGGRAPH 2026 — Los Angeles, 19–23 July**

SIGGRAPH is the global credibility room for this industry. The Production Sessions track is where VFX supervisors and pipeline leads present what they've actually built, not what they're planning to build. It's the most reliable read on where senior hiring needs are forming in high-end production.

The question I'm carrying into SIGGRAPH is whether the AI specialist roles being created inside major studios have begun to stabilise. Early in a technology cycle, studios invent roles before they understand them. We're three or four years into serious AI integration in high-end production. There should be enough evidence now to see which structures are working and which ones studios are quietly redesigning.

That's worth a week in LA.
