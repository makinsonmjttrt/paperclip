## 2. Gaps vs Best Practice

### 2.1 URL Slug — Critical

**Current:** `linkedin.com/company/xrjobs`
**Problem:** The URL slug `xrjobs` is the firm's legacy XR recruitment identity. Every link shared, every Google result, every DM that includes the company page URL signals "XR Jobs" — not FourPointZero, not CreativAI, not AI executive search. This is a branding and SEO mismatch that compounds over time.
**Algorithm signal:** LinkedIn uses the company URL for indexing. A slug misaligned with your current specialisation reduces searchability for "AI executive search", "creative production recruitment", and "FourPointZero."
**Fix:** Request a URL change through LinkedIn's Help Centre (custom URL changes require admin request; LinkedIn approves them — not instant, but straightforward for a legitimate rebrand). Target slug: `fourpointzero` or `fourpointzero-recruitment`.

### 2.2 Tagline — Weak Positioning Signal

**Current:** "We're FourPointZero, the recruiters for creative production"
**Problem:** "Recruiters for creative production" is a category description, not a positioning claim. It tells visitors what you do, not what you do that no one else does. It reads like a generic recruitment page — which is precisely what FPZ's entire market position argues against.
**LinkedIn context:** The tagline appears in search results, ad impressions, and the top of the company page. It is the first 5-10 words a decision-maker reads. At 8,400+ followers, that's real volume of first impressions being underserved.
**What it should do:** Signal the intersection (AI + creative production), signal seniority (executive search / senior leadership), and create a reason to read the About section.

### 2.3 About Section — XR-Era Language Anchoring

The current About section still uses "experiential, interactive, spatial computing, and real-time" as primary descriptors. These are accurate but they anchor FPZ in its 2019–2023 XR identity, not its 2024-onwards AI executive search positioning. Decision-makers arriving from CreativAI content, VFX Voice, or a post about AI-in-production hiring will find a page that looks slightly behind where the actual practice is.

Specific language gaps:
- "Executive search" does not appear prominently
- "VFX" is absent despite DNEG, Framestore, ILM being Tier 1 target clients
- "CreativAI" is not mentioned
- The 89% 18-month tenure rate and 87% fill success rate are buried on the website — they should be in the About section as social proof

### 2.4 Specialties — Missing High-Value Search Terms

**Current specialties include:** data science (too generic, not primary practice), generative AI (good), creative technologist (useful)
**Missing:** executive search, VFX recruitment, virtual production, CAIO, head of AI production, spatial computing recruitment, retained search, AI leadership
**LinkedIn uses specialties for:** People search (hiring managers searching for specific recruiter types), company page search, and LinkedIn's internal recommendation algorithm.

### 2.5 Featured Section — Not Currently Visible

The page does not appear to have a curated Featured section. This is a missed conversion opportunity: the Featured section sits above the fold on the company page and is prime real estate for directing visitors to one specific action (reading a case study, submitting a brief, viewing a relevant article).

### 2.6 Content Mix — Curation vs Authority

**Current pattern:** Weekly AI news round-ups with brief commentary.
**Gap:** Curation demonstrates awareness; it does not demonstrate expertise. Decision-makers at DNEG or Foundry already see these news items from 20 other sources. The posts that build search and referral authority are original observations: "The CAIO title is arriving at studios. The hiring infrastructure hasn't." or "We placed 3 of these roles last year. Here's what the briefs had in common."
**Algorithm note (2026):** LinkedIn now heavily penalises posts with external links (approximately 60% reach reduction). News round-up posts that link out to sources are structurally penalised under the current algorithm. Native text posts and document carousels outperform curated link posts by a significant margin.

### 2.7 Employee Advocacy — Under-Activated

Martyn Makinson has 18,000 personal followers — more than double the company page count. Three employees are showing on the page. No evidence of systematic cross-tagging or resharing of company posts from employee profiles. At the current follow ratio (personal: ~2.2x company), the company page is reaching a fraction of the audience already engaged with FPZ's personal brand.

### 2.8 CreativAI Sub-Page — No LinkedIn Presence

CreativAI has copy, a one-pager, and is referenced across content, but has no LinkedIn presence. Decision-makers introduced to CreativAI through outreach or content who search LinkedIn will find nothing for "CreativAI" and will land on the FPZ parent page (with XR-era positioning).

### Recommendation

**Message:** The banner should do one job: confirm you're in the right place. For FPZ's audiences (studio directors, VFX heads, AI-native company founders), the banner should signal expertise at the intersection, not generic recruitment branding.

**Visual approach:**
- Dark background (industry-appropriate; avoids the bright-gradient look common in generalist recruiters)
- Single bold statement, large type: **"Executive search for AI in creative production"**
- Secondary line in smaller type: "FourPointZero and CreativAI — placing the leaders building AI into production pipelines"
- Optional: small logo lockup of both FPZ and CreativAI marks
- No stock imagery of handshakes or office environments — the audience has seen all of that and it signals mid-market generalist

**What to avoid:**
- Featuring the company name large (it's already on the page)
- Logos without supporting text (wasted on visitors who don't already know you)
- Tagline alone without the context of what you actually do

**Spec:** LinkedIn company page banner = 1,128 x 191px (minimum). Design at 2x (2256 x 382px) for retina displays.

## 6. Employee Advocacy — Recommendations

**The leverage:** Martyn has 18,000 personal followers vs 8,431 on the company page. Every team member who reshares a company post effectively doubles its reach into networks not yet following FPZ.

**Recommended actions:**

1. **Consistent reshare protocol:** When a company page post goes live, Martyn and any active team members reshare it to personal feeds within 24 hours — not a copy-paste, but a native reshare with a one- or two-line personal comment ("This was from a conversation I had last week..."). LinkedIn's algorithm treats the reshare as a second distribution event.

2. **Employee profile optimisation:** Each team member's LinkedIn profile should list FourPointZero as current employer (this is already happening with 6 employees linked), but also include "CreativAI" somewhere in the title or about section for those involved in that practice. Currently no team member profile explicitly signals CreativAI practice affiliation.

3. **Personal post tagging:** Martyn's personal posts already drive strong reach. When topics align with company content pillars, tagging `@FourPointZero` in personal posts directs engaged readers to the company page. Use sparingly (2–3x per month maximum) to avoid promotional feel.

4. **Follower ask — once:** A single personal post from Martyn explicitly asking his network to follow the company page, with a specific reason ("We're posting weekly intelligence on AI leadership hiring — if you're building a production team or navigating an AI mandate, the company page is worth following"), is worth doing once. These one-time asks reliably generate 100–300 new followers when the personal following is 18K+.

5. **Client and candidate follow asks:** At the close of each placement and at the conclusion of each BD conversation, one of the post-engagement steps should include: "If you'd find it useful, FPZ's company page posts weekly signals on AI creative production hiring — worth following if that's useful." Low friction, high intent audience.

## 7. CreativAI Sub-Page: Separate Company Page vs Showcase Page

**Recommendation: LinkedIn Showcase Page, not a separate Company Page.**

**Rationale:**

A separate Company Page for CreativAI would require independent follower acquisition from zero, its own admin management, and its own content cadence. For a practice within a 6-person firm, that's resource overhead that would split effort without strategic benefit.

A LinkedIn **Showcase Page** is a child page attached to the FourPointZero company page. It inherits the parent's credibility and discoverability, allows CreativAI to have its own URL (linkedin.com/showcase/creativai), its own About section, and its own follower base — but without the overhead of managing a fully independent company page.

**What a CreativAI Showcase Page enables:**
- Dedicated space for AI executive search content separate from FPZ's broader creative production focus
- Its own URL to feature in CreativAI outreach, the one-pager, and any future creativai.io website
- Searchability for "CreativAI" on LinkedIn — currently returns no results
- Targeted follower acquisition from the AI-in-production hiring audience specifically

**When to set it up:** Before the LinkedIn content strategy goes live (i.e., before 7 April 2026 if possible). The Showcase Page can be created by any company page admin in under 10 minutes via the Page Admin tools.

**Content approach for CreativAI Showcase Page:** Subset of the FPZ content strategy focused on Pillars 1 and 3 (role evolution and leadership at the AI/creative intersection). Repost the most relevant 50% of FPZ posts. Add 1–2 original CreativAI-specific posts per month covering CAIO trends, AI executive salary data, and search methodology.
