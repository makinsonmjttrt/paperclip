#### NAB 2026 — Pre-Show Intelligence Brief
*Compiled: April 2026. 10 days before exhibits open.*

---

**Context**

NAB 2026 (19–22 April) has expanded to a second AI Pavilion — the first time in NAB's history. This signals that AI has moved from speculative to operational across broadcast, post production, and creative tech. Every major toolmaker is positioning AI as workflow infrastructure, not experimentation. For FPZ, the signal is simple: the tools are accelerating, and the talent gap is widening.

---

**1. AI Creative Tools — Expected Announcements**

*All directional. Based on confirmed exhibitor previews and pre-show press.*

**Avid — Content Core with AI media workflows**
Avid has confirmed it will demo Content Core at NAB, focusing on AI-powered media discovery, metadata generation, logging, and clip generation. New parallel processing engine also confirmed. Primary audience: broadcast news and studio infrastructure buyers. Talent signal: Avid-certified editors and media asset managers will need AI workflow skills alongside traditional MediaComposer competencies.

**FOR-A — First fully AI-native NAB**
FOR-A has stated this will be its first fully software-defined, AI-powered NAB since its debut in 1980. Core product: AI real-time 9:16 auto-cropping and tracking for mobile-first distribution, plus AI-powered visual mix and effects creation. Talent signal: operators who understand AI-assisted live production workflows are a growing hire profile.

**Eluvio — Agentic AI for title libraries and live sports**
Exhibiting at AI Innovation Pavilion (Booth W2300E). Launching Eluvio AI, described as the first commercially available system to run multimodal AI analysis inline within streaming distribution without file re-transcoding. Designed for large title libraries and live sports. Talent signal: media ops and content engineering roles at streaming platforms will require AI-native candidates.

**Adobe — AI-augmented post production (Creator Lab co-presenter)**
Adobe is co-presenting the expanded Creator Lab with Blackmagic Design. Adobe Premiere 26.0 (launched January 2026) is the current version — key features include AI Object Masking (one-click rotoscope tracking), Generative Extend, and Firefly Boards integration. NAB 2026 is likely a showcase for these features rather than a major new release. Talent signal: editors who cannot demonstrate AI-augmented workflows are losing ground on briefs.

**Boris FX — AI VFX tools**
Boris FX confirmed for NAB 2026, showcasing latest AI tools and workflow additions for VFX and motion graphics. Watch for Continuum and Silhouette updates with expanded AI-driven rotoscoping and compositing.

**Chyron — AI headshot automation**
LUCI Crop Tool in beta. AI headshot centring and scaling, reducing a multi-step process to a single click. Niche but signals direction for broadcast graphics operators.

**Google Cloud + Google DeepMind — Keynote: "The Augmented Studio"**
Keynote session examining how AI is reshaping creative workflows across production, editing, and visual storytelling. Presenters: Anil Jain (Google Cloud) and Márcia Mayer (Google DeepMind). This is significant positioning — big tech is now speaking directly to creative production buyers about AI infrastructure.

**Microsoft — Keynote: "From AI Experimentation to Real-World Impact"**
Silvia Candiani (VP, Telecoms, Media & Gaming) presenting on how media organisations are moving AI from pilots to production deployment. Same signal as Google: enterprise AI is arriving in creative studios.

**3. FPZ Talking Points — Post-NAB LinkedIn**

Use within 48 hours of each relevant announcement. Directional opinions, not sourced statistics.

**Talking Point A — "The second AI Pavilion signal"**
NAB doubled its AI floor space for 2026. That is the clearest possible statement about where creative production is heading. It is no longer a question of whether studios adopt AI — it is a question of whether they can hire the people to run it. Every pavilion at NAB this year has an AI workflow. The talent infrastructure to support those workflows is 18 months behind. That is the gap CreativAI operates in.

*Angle: Use after opening day coverage. Frame as an observation on market speed, not a product endorsement. Good for a short post (3–5 lines) or a response comment on another post.*

**Talking Point B — "Tools are compressing, talent gaps are widening"**
Blackmagic, Adobe, and Avid are releasing AI features on a near-monthly cadence. The question is no longer "which tools support AI" — every tool does. The question is "who in your team can actually use them at a senior level." Script-to-edit in DaVinci Resolve, AI masking in Premiere, AI-assisted grading pipelines — these require judgment, not just familiarity. Studios hiring for these roles are still using job descriptions written for 2022. The candidates who close that gap are rare and they know it.

*Angle: Post after Blackmagic or Adobe show coverage. Works well as a 5–8 line post or as a LinkedIn article introduction.*

**Talking Point C — "The Blackmagic DaVinci cycle and what it means for your next hire"**
Every NAB, Blackmagic releases something significant. Editors, colourists, and post supervisors track it closely — not because they are gear enthusiasts, but because it changes what the role requires. If DaVinci Resolve 21 ships at NAB 2026 with AI audio separation or new spatial media tools, studios will need post supervisors who know the update. The talent requirement shifts at NAB speed; most HR hiring processes run 6–8 weeks behind. The studios that brief FPZ before the show closes are better positioned than those who wait for the dust to settle.

*Angle: Post specifically if a major DaVinci Resolve announcement is confirmed. Strong differentiation play for FPZ — positions the firm as operationally close to the tooling cycle.*
