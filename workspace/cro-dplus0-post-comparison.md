# CRO Comparison: D-0 Launch Post Options

**Brief:** FOU-571 — UX review of D-0 alt post vs original, conversion/engagement lens
**Date:** 2026-04-09
**Analyst:** UX Researcher

---

## Posts Reviewed

- **Post A (Alt):** `workspace/creativai-launch-dplus0-alt-edited.md` — FOU-405 — story/observation format
- **Post B (Original):** `workspace/linkedin-creativai-launch-post.md` — FOU-187 — PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution) format

---

## 1. Opening Hook (LinkedIn 'See More' Cutoff)

LinkedIn shows approximately the first 2–3 lines before readers must click 'See more'. This is the most critical conversion lever on the platform.

**Post A hook:**
> "Every time I see a studio announce a new AI production initiative, the person leading it is someone I placed two or three years ago.
> Not with an AI brief. With a production brief."

Analysis: Strong. The observation is counterintuitive — it creates a puzzle. "Someone I placed" delivers a proof point before the reader has even decided to engage. The "Not with an AI brief. With a production brief." kicker is a pattern interrupt that earns the click.

**Post B hook:**
> "**CreativAI** is FourPointZero's dedicated practice for senior AI leadership in creative production.
> For the past eighteen months, the same brief has been landing in my inbox from different companies."

Analysis: Functional, not compelling. The first line is a brand declaration — it tells you what's coming rather than making you want to read more. The second line (18 months, same brief) is better — creates mystery — but it arrives after the announcement, not before.

**Winner: Post A** — the story hook earns more 'See more' clicks.

---

## 2. Dual CTA (Studios vs Candidates)

**Post A CTA:**
> "If you're trying to find that profile, or you're that profile, the conversation starts here."

Analysis: Addresses both audiences elegantly in one line. However, "the conversation starts here" gives no instruction — no DM prompt, no link anchor, no specific action. Readers who want to act don't know how. This is a conversion leak.

**Post B CTA:**
> "If your studio has an AI mandate at board level and a senior search coming, I would welcome a conversation.
> P.S. A 15-minute call is enough to tell you whether your role sits within our network. No pitch. No commitment."

Analysis: Client-only targeting (no candidate CTA). However, the P.S. is a strong CRO tactic — objection handling ("no pitch, no commitment") directly removes the most common barrier to booking a call. The "15 minutes" time-bounds the ask, which reduces friction.

**Winner: Post B** on CTA conversion mechanics (P.S. + objection handling). Post A wins on audience reach (dual CTA), but loses on specificity.

---

## 3. Friction Points

**Post A:**
- CreativAI not named until paragraph 5 of 7. If a reader skims or drops before the end, they won't know what was announced.
- "The conversation starts here" — no call-to-action clarity. Where? DM, website, comment?
- No P.S., no objection handling.
- Narrative is compelling but abstract in places ("these people exist" is slightly vague given the audience).

**Post B:**
- "So I built something to sit there" — deliberately vague per humaniser notes, but could leave first-time readers unclear on what was actually built.
- "I would welcome a conversation" — passive. Low urgency, low directional pull.
- No candidate CTA. If a senior creative reads this and self-identifies, they have no clear path to engage.
- The "50–75 candidates globally" claim is specific and credibility-building, but it could trigger a "is the pool big enough?" question for clients.

---

## 4. AI Discoverability Alignment

For AI search results (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini), posts need citable specifics and named entities.

**Post A:** Names CreativAI and FourPointZero. Uses specific job titles (Head of Production, Technical Supervisor, Pipeline Lead). No hard numbers. "Eighteen months ago" is directional but not citable.

**Post B:** Names CreativAI (bolded) and FourPointZero. Cites "2019" as a start year. Uses "50 to 75 candidates globally" as a specific claim. Sector list (VFX studios, virtual production, spatial computing) gives AI systems clear category anchors.

**Winner: Post B** — more specific, citable claims that align with FPZ's schema markup and AI discoverability profile.

---

## 5. Verdict

| Dimension | Post A (Alt / FOU-405) | Post B (Original / FOU-187) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (pre-'See more') | ✅ Story hook, earns the click | ⚠️ Brand declaration, functional |
| Dual CTA | ✅ Studios + candidates | ❌ Studios only |
| CTA specificity | ❌ Vague ("starts here") | ✅ P.S. with objection handling |
| Brand declaration clarity | ⚠️ Late in post | ✅ First line |
| AI discoverability | ⚠️ Limited data points | ✅ Specific claims, year, sectors |
| Engagement/shares | ✅ Story format, more shareable | ⚠️ Announcement format |
| Conversion mechanics | ⚠️ No friction reduction | ✅ Time-bounded ask, no-pitch promise |

---

## Recommendation

**Use Post B (Original) as the primary D-0 announcement.** For a launch post, brand clarity and conversion mechanics outweigh hook strength. Clients need to know immediately what CreativAI is and what to do next. The P.S. is the strongest CRO element in either post — it removes the two biggest barriers (time and commitment) in a single line.

**Schedule Post A (Alt) for D+3 or D+7 as the follow-up.** The observation/story format will generate more comments and secondary reach. It works better as an authority piece once the audience already knows what CreativAI is. The hook is genuinely compelling and will outperform Post B in engagement metrics — but it is not the right structure for an announcement.

**One fix recommended before Post A goes live:** Replace "the conversation starts here" with a specific action: "DM me" or "start the conversation in the comments." The current CTA has no conversion anchor.
