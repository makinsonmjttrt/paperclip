# Testimonial Outreach Package — Homepage Social Proof

*Created: 2026-04-03 | Owner: Customer Success | Source: [FOU-74](/FOU/issues/FOU-74)*

---

## Objective

Source one named, approved client testimonial for the FourPointZero homepage. Target: short format (2-liner), named, from a recognisable studio or agency the ICP will know.

**Urgency:** Quick win. One quote changes the trust signal on a currently zero-social-proof page.

---

## Who to Approach First

**Ideal profile:** Repeat client with a completed senior placement in VFX, virtual production, experiential, or AI-integrated workflows. The stronger the outcome story, the better. Prioritise someone who:

- Has placed more than one role through FPZ (repeat = warm relationship)
- Had a measurable outcome: role filled faster than expected, unusual brief solved, candidate from outside their usual network
- Works at a name the ICP will recognise (DNEG, Framestore, a named creative SaaS company, a recognised experiential agency)
- Is likely to respond to a short, direct request — a COO, Studio Director, or Head of People level contact you have an existing rapport with

**Starting recommendation:** Pick the most recent successful repeat placement where the client expressed satisfaction. The best testimonial candidate is whoever sent you the warmest thank-you email in the last 12 months.

---

## Outreach Email — Option A: Let Them Choose

Use this when you want to keep friction low.

```
Subject: Quick favour — 30 seconds of your time

Hi [Name],

Enjoyed working together on [role / brief]. Really pleased with how [outcome in one sentence — e.g., "how quickly we got [Name of placed candidate] in front of you"].

We're refreshing the FourPointZero website and have zero client testimonials on it — which is a gap I'm fixing now.

Would you be open to sharing a short quote I could use on the site? No pressure. If yes, easiest options:

A) I send you three quick questions — you answer in a few sentences
B) I write a draft for you to approve or edit

Whatever takes less of your time.

Martyn
```

---

## Outreach Email — Option B: Draft on Behalf (Faster Approval)

Use this when you already know what the client would say. Write a draft and offer to adjust.

```
Subject: Quick favour — draft quote for your approval

Hi [Name],

We're updating the FourPointZero site with client quotes. Based on our work together on [role/project], I drafted something below — very happy for you to edit, shorten, or rewrite entirely.

---

"[Draft quote — see framework below]."
— [Name], [Title], [Company]

---

If you're happy with it as-is, just say the word. If you'd like to adjust anything, let me know and I'll make the change. Equally, if you'd prefer not to be named, no problem.

Martyn
```

---

## Draft Quote Framework

Use this to write the Option B draft. Fill in from what you know about the placement:

```
"[Client situation in one sentence — what the challenge was].
[What FPZ delivered — concrete, specific].
[One outcome: speed, quality, candidate was unusual, brief was difficult].
[Optional: recommendation or what made FPZ different]."
— [Name], [Title], [Company]
```

**Example structure (fill with real details):**

> "We needed someone who understood both the technical depth and the production environment — not just one or the other. FPZ came back with a shortlist of three candidates, all relevant, within [X weeks]. [Placed candidate name] has been in the role [X months] and it's working exactly as we hoped."
> — [Name], [Title], [Studio name]

**Short format target (homepage):**

> "[One punchy outcome sentence]."
> — [Name], [Title], [Studio name]

---

## Question Set — Option A Route

Send these if the client prefers to write their own response:

1. What was the situation before we worked together on this? (What made the role difficult?)
2. What did the process look like — what stood out?
3. Would you recommend FPZ to a studio or agency in a similar situation? Why?

One specific number or outcome makes a testimonial credible and reusable — if you don't get one naturally, ask: "Even a rough estimate — was it a question of speed, quality, or access to candidates you couldn't reach yourself?"
