# CRO Audit: FourPointZero Homepage Concept

**Auditor:** UX Researcher
**Date:** 2026-04-03
**Issue:** [FOU-79](/FOU/issues/FOU-79)
**Skill applied:** page-cro (from cro-suite)
**Page type:** Service business homepage — specialist recruitment
**Primary conversion goal:** Inbound client enquiry ("Submit a Brief" / "Get in Touch")
**Secondary conversion goal:** Candidate applications for live roles

---

## Hypothetical Page Concept

This audit is based on a representative FPZ homepage concept using:
- Typical recruitment firm homepage structure
- FPZ's documented positioning (specialist creative production + emerging tech)
- Target audience personas from product marketing context

**Assumed current headline:** "Specialist Recruitment for Creative Production and Emerging Technology"
**Assumed current CTA:** "Get in Touch"
**Assumed page structure:** Hero → About → Services → Live Roles → Footer

---

## 5-Second Test

**Verdict: Immediately confusing for a first-time visitor.**

A studio director landing on the page cold can parse *what FPZ does* but cannot immediately understand *why FPZ beats their current option*. The phrase "creative production and emerging technology" is accurate but requires sector fluency to decode. A Head of People at a VFX studio would not immediately see themselves in the headline.

## Recommendation 1: Rewrite the Headline to Lead with the Differentiator

**Issue:** The current headline describes the category, not the advantage. "Specialist Recruitment for Creative Production and Emerging Technology" is what FPZ does, not why FPZ wins.

**Impact:** High — the headline is the single highest-leverage conversion element on any page. A stronger headline reduces bounce and immediately qualifies relevant visitors.

**Fix:**

| | Copy |
|--|--|
| **Current** | "Specialist Recruitment for Creative Production and Emerging Technology" |
| **Why it's weak** | Describes the service category. No differentiation. Could be any niche recruiter. |
| **Rewrite A** | "We place the senior talent who understand both sides — creative vision and emerging tech stack" |
| **Rewrite B** | "Senior production leadership for studios building at the creative/tech intersection — placed faster because we've spent six years building this network" |
| **Why it's stronger** | Leads with the specific outcome (faster, more relevant placement) and the credibility anchor (six-year network) — both of which directly address the two biggest client anxieties |

**Priority:** Do This Week
**Metric to track:** Time on page, bounce rate, enquiry form start rate

---

## Recommendation 2: Replace "Get in Touch" with a Value-Oriented CTA

**Issue:** "Get in Touch" is the weakest possible CTA for a high-consideration B2B service. It communicates effort (they have to initiate) with no promised outcome in return. For a studio director weighing whether to engage a new recruiter, this CTA provides no reason to act now.

**Impact:** High — CTA copy directly affects click-through rate. Weak CTA copy is common but consistently underperforms against specific, outcome-oriented alternatives.

**Fix:**

| | Copy |
|--|--|
| **Current** | "Get in Touch" |
| **Rewrite** | "Submit a Brief" (primary) / "See How We Work" (secondary) |
| **Why it's stronger** | "Submit a Brief" frames the interaction as the start of a process, not a general enquiry — reducing ambiguity about what happens next. The secondary CTA captures visitors still in research mode without competing with the primary action. |

**Supporting copy to add near CTA:** "We typically respond within one working day. First conversation is always a no-commitment briefing call."

**Priority:** Do This Week
**Metric to track:** CTA click rate, form start rate, enquiry volume

---

## Recommendation 3: Add Social Proof Before It's Perfect

**Issue:** The marketing context explicitly marks client logos and testimonials as "TO FILL." Every day the page runs without social proof is a day the page works against FPZ's credibility positioning. Boutique recruiters are automatically viewed with more scepticism than established agencies — social proof is the primary trust mechanism to overcome this.

**Impact:** High — for service businesses, testimonials placed near CTAs and after benefit claims are among the highest-impact conversion elements. The absence of proof is an active negative signal to sophisticated buyers.

**Fix:**

1. **Immediate (this week):** Add industry membership logos — APSCO, VRARA, AIXR. These exist and are documented. They signal embedded credibility without requiring client permission.
2. **Short-term (this month):** Source two or three specific, attributed testimonials from existing clients. Format: "[Quote about outcome]" — [Name, Title, Studio]. Photos if possible.
3. **Medium-term:** Add a placement counter or a years-established statement ("Placing senior creative production talent since 2019") as a proxy social proof until hard metrics are available.

**Priority:** Do This Week (memberships) / This Month (testimonials)
**Metric to track:** Time on page, enquiry form completion rate

---

## Recommendation 4: Add an Explicit Client vs Candidate Path Immediately Below the Hero

**Issue:** FPZ serves two distinct audiences with different conversion goals — clients (hiring studios) and candidates (senior talent). A homepage that tries to speak to both simultaneously risks diluting the message for each. Currently, there is no clear early signal directing each visitor to their relevant path.

**Impact:** Medium-High — path clarity reduces cognitive load and bounce rate for both audiences. Candidates arriving via job listings have different intent to clients arriving from LinkedIn content.

**Fix:**

Add a two-column path selector immediately below the hero (before any copy-heavy section):

```
┌────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┐
│  HIRING?                       │  YOUR NEXT ROLE?               │
│  We build shortlists of        │  Senior creative production    │
│  vetted senior production      │  and emerging tech roles —     │
│  talent, fast.                 │  placed by people who know     │
│  [Submit a Brief →]            │  the space.                    │
│                                │  [View Live Roles →]           │
└────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

This is a low-code change (two cards, two CTAs) with measurable segmentation impact.

**Priority:** This Month
**Metric to track:** CTA click split (client vs candidate), page exit rate, engagement depth by traffic source

---

## Recommendation 5: Make the "Why FPZ vs a Generalist" Argument Explicit on the Homepage

**Issue:** The biggest objection FPZ faces — "we've already used [big agency]" or "we'll just post on LinkedIn" — is never addressed on the homepage. The page describes FPZ's focus but does not directly tackle why that focus produces better outcomes than the default alternatives.

**Impact:** Medium-High — for a boutique firm competing against habit and familiarity, addressing the switching argument on the homepage reduces friction for decision-makers who are comparing options but haven't yet been burned by a generalist.

**Fix:**

Add a short three-column comparison or "Why Specialist Matters" section:

| | Generalist Recruiter | LinkedIn / Job Boards | FourPointZero |
|--|--|--|--|
| Candidate relevance | Low — broad pool, sector-unaware | Variable — mostly active, junior skew | High — pre-vetted, passive candidates, sector-specific |
| Time to shortlist | 4-8 weeks | Ongoing, time-intensive | Faster — network already built |
| Sector knowledge | None | None | Deep — creative production + emerging tech only |

Use language from the objections section: "If you've been burned by recruiters who send irrelevant CVs, this is why it happened — and why it won't happen with us."

**Priority:** This Month
**Metric to track:** Scroll depth past this section, time on page, enquiry quality (reduce irrelevant inbound)

## Test Ideas (A/B Candidates)

- **Headline specificity:** Test "We place senior production talent at the creative/tech intersection" vs "We placed 40+ senior roles in experiential, real-time, and spatial computing since 2019" — specificity and social proof in headline
- **CTA framing:** "Submit a Brief" vs "Tell Us About Your Role" — action-oriented vs conversational
- **Hero visual:** No image vs a visual representing the sector (virtual production set, spatial computing installation) — professional context vs clean layout
- **Social proof placement:** Trust bar above the fold (memberships + years established) vs dedicated section below hero

## Self-Critique Notes

- This audit is based on a representative concept, not a live page URL. Scores are calibrated for a typical recruitment firm homepage at FPZ's current maturity stage. Actual page should be scored against a screenshot or live URL.
- The absence of hard metrics (placement numbers, client count, time-to-hire data) weakens the audit's specificity. When FPZ compiles these, they become the highest-priority additions to the homepage.
- Recommendation 3 (social proof) is rated critical despite being marked "TO FILL" — the gap is a known risk, not a future consideration.
- No mobile-specific recommendations made due to absence of mobile mockup. Mobile CTA stacking and card layout for Recommendation 4 should be validated separately.
