## **How Creative Production Hires at Senior Level**

Network and in-house bias is structurally dominant in VFX and production. Senior roles are routinely filled through SIGGRAPH, VES events, and personal referrals without ever being posted publicly. VFX Hire's founding rationale describes senior opportunities being kept in-house and 'leaning towards who they know' — a consistent pattern across all sources. Major studios with parent company infrastructure (Disney for ILM, Fimalac for Framestore) route senior hires through internal TA alongside targeted search outreach for confidential mandates.

At large media and entertainment groups, posted VP-level AI roles with published pay bands (FOX's AI production VP roles being the clearest example) indicate internal TA-managed recruitment. At the true C-suite level — CAIO, CSO — external search is the norm. The Sinecure.ai acquisition of Grace Blue, Spencer Stuart's media and entertainment practice, and Korn Ferry's confirmed media and entertainment leadership appointments all evidence retained search as standard buying behaviour for C-suite placements in this sector.

## **Search Model Breakdown**

Three commercial models appear consistently across sources. Retained search (staged payments, typically 25–33% of base or first-year cash compensation) is the standard for C-suite and SVP/EVP mandates where confidentiality and passive candidate access are required. Contingent search (success-only, non-exclusive) remains dominant for mid-to-senior levels in VFX and animation. Container or hybrid search (small upfront engagement fee plus success component) is the model most relevant to FPZ's transition from contingent to retained — it lowers financial risk for clients while securing focus and demonstrating process rigour.

## **The Confidentiality Dynamic (Confirmed Signal Across Sources)**

Multiple sources independently flag that creative production companies are advancing AI capabilities discreetly, partly due to union sensitivity following the 2023 SAG-AFTRA and WGA strikes. This creates search assignments that are confidential by design — organisations are reluctant to signal AI hiring publicly for fear of triggering union grievances or alerting competitors. This dynamic strongly favours retained executive search over public postings, and gives a specialist search partner with established trust relationships a significant structural advantage over contingent or job-board-based approaches.

## **Where This Talent Sits Today**

Senior AI-creative talent clusters across four employer types. VFX and post-production studios hold ML leadership (Head of Machine Learning, R\&D Director) and visual development / creative technology leaders who explicitly integrate generative AI tools. Creative agencies hold CAIO-designated leaders who are overwhelmingly internal elevations: existing CTO, Head of Production, or SVP Operations given an AI mandate rather than external AI hires. This matters because it narrows the external candidate pool further, but also signals that companies filling their second or third AI leadership role — when the internal elevation model runs out — will need external search. Media owners and broadcasters hold dedicated VP-level AI production roles (FoxNXT model) that span production, R\&D, and enablement. Creative software companies hold the deepest and highest-compensated AI leadership pool, with the most experienced candidates in applied research, VFX engineering, and AI solutions.

The dominant career path into the most senior AI-in-production roles is: academic ML research, then corporate R\&D (Adobe Research, Autodesk Research, a games studio), then creative industry. A secondary path runs through VFX pipeline engineering into ML specialisation into AI leadership. Very few candidates have made the reverse journey from creative production into AI leadership, which is why the pool is small and why a specialist recruiter with access to both communities has a genuine advantage.
