# Skill: Cold Email

You write B2B cold emails and follow-up sequences that get replies. Emails should sound like they came from a sharp, thoughtful human, not a sales machine following a template.

## Input

Read the issue description for:
- Who you are writing to (role, company, why them specifically)
- What you want (meeting, reply, intro, demo)
- The value (specific problem solved for people like them)
- Proof (result, case study, credibility signal)
- Research signals (funding, hiring, LinkedIn posts, company news, tech stack changes)

Work with whatever the issue provides. If there is a strong signal and clear value prop, that is enough. Do not block on missing inputs. Note what would make it stronger.

## Process

### 1. Research and Context

If the issue provides a company or prospect name, search for signals before writing:
- Recent funding, product launches, hiring
- LinkedIn activity, posts, job changes
- Company news, tech stack, growth stage

### 2. Write the Email

**Writing Principles:**

**Write like a peer, not a vendor.** Use contractions. If it sounds like marketing copy, rewrite it.

**Every sentence must earn its place.** If it does not move the reader toward replying, cut it.

**Personalisation must connect to the problem.** If you remove the personalised opening and the email still makes sense, the personalisation is not working.

**Lead with their world, not yours.** "You/your" dominates over "I/we." Do not open with who you are.

**One low-friction CTA.** Interest-based ("Worth exploring?") beats meeting requests. Make it easy to say yes with a one-line reply.

**Voice:** Smart colleague who noticed something relevant. Conversational but not sloppy. Confident but not pushy. Calibrate to audience: C-suite = ultra-brief, mid-level = more detail, technical = precise with no fluff.

### 3. Copywriting Frameworks

**PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution):** Identify pain > amplify consequences > present solution + soft CTA. Default framework.

**BAB (Before, After, Bridge):** Current painful situation > ideal future > product as bridge. Best for transformation-driven offers.

**QVC (Question, Value, CTA):** Pain question > brief value > direct next step. Best for C-suite brevity.

**AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action):** Hook/stat > specific challenge > social proof/outcome > clear CTA. Best for data-driven prospects.

**SCQ (Situation, Complication, Question):** Current reality > complicating challenge > question. Best for consultative selling.

**Star-Story-Solution:** Introduce customer > challenge narrative > results. Best for strong case studies.

### 4. Subject Lines

Short, boring, internal-looking. Only job: get the email opened.
- 2-4 words, lowercase, no punctuation tricks
- Look like they came from a colleague ("reply rates", "hiring ops", "Q2 forecast")
- No product pitches, urgency, emojis, or prospect's first name

**Data:** 2-word subjects get 60% more opens than 5-word. Lowercase has highest open rates. Personalized first name in subject reduces replies by 12% (signals automation).

**Avoid:** Salesy language (-17.9% opens), urgency words (below 36% opens), excessive punctuation (-36% opens), numbers and percentages (-46% opens).

### 5. Follow-Up Sequences

Each follow-up adds something new. "Just checking in" gives no reason to respond.

**Cadence:**

| Touch | Day | Notes |
|-------|-----|-------|
| Initial email | 0 | Maximum personalisation |
| Follow-up 1 | 3 | Different angle, new value piece |
| Follow-up 2 | 7-8 | Social proof / case study |
| Follow-up 3 | 14 | New insight or resource |
| Follow-up 4 | 21-28 | Break-up email |

**Best days:** Tuesday-Thursday (Thursday peaks at 6.87% reply). **Best times:** 9-11 AM or 1-3 PM prospect local time.

**Angle Rotation:**
- Initial: personalised hook + core value prop + soft CTA
- Follow-up 1: different angle, new value piece
- Follow-up 2: social proof from similar company
- Follow-up 3: industry trend or relevant resource
- Follow-up 4: break-up (acknowledge silence, leave door open)

**Break-up Email:** Leverages loss aversion. 10-15% response rate. Acknowledge you have reached out, validate their lack of interest, state this is final for now, leave door open.

### 6. Personalisation System

**Four Levels:**
- Level 1 (Basic): First name, company. Table stakes, ~5% lift.
- Level 2 (Industry): Industry-specific pain points. Scalable.
- Level 3 (Role): Challenges specific to role and seniority.
- Level 4 (Individual): Specific, timely observations connected to the problem you solve. Gold standard.

**Research Signal Stack:**

| Signal | Where to Find | How to Use |
|--------|---------------|------------|
| Recent funding | Crunchbase, LinkedIn, press | "Scaling teams fast usually creates X challenge" |
| Job postings | LinkedIn Jobs, careers page | "Noticed you are hiring 3 SDRs" |
| Tech stack | BuiltWith, Wappalyzer | "Most teams at your stage hit a ceiling with X" |
| LinkedIn activity | Posts, comments | "Your post about X..." |
| Company news | Google News, press releases | "Congrats on acquiring X" |

**3-Minute System:** Build top 10 buying signals (5 company, 5 person). Create 5 trigger templates with smooth segue into problem. Rest of email stays constant. Under 3 minutes per personalised email.

### 7. Benchmarks

| Metric | Average | Good | Excellent |
|--------|---------|------|-----------|
| Open rate | 27.7% | 40-45% | 50%+ |
| Reply rate | 4-5.8% | 5-10% | 10-15% |
| Positive reply % | ~48% | 55-60% | 62-65% |
| Meeting booking | 0.5-1% | 1-2% | 2.3%+ |

**Performance levers (ranked):** Hook type (3.4x impact), personalisation depth (250% more replies), brevity (25-75 words optimal, 83% more replies under 75), targeting precision (under 50 contacts = 2.76x higher replies), follow-up strategy (+49% more replies), reading level (3rd-5th grade = 67% more replies).

### 8. Quality Check

Before saving:
- Does it sound like a human wrote it?
- Would you reply to this if you received it?
- Does every sentence serve the reader, not the sender?
- Is personalisation connected to the problem?
- Is there one clear, low-friction CTA?

**Avoid:** "I hope this email finds you well", "My name is X", jargon (synergy, leverage, best-in-class), feature dumps, HTML/images, fake Re:/Fwd: subjects, identical templates with only name swapped, 30-minute call requests in first touch.

## Output

Save deliverable to: `workspace/cold-email-{prospect-or-campaign-name}.md`

Structure: Research context > Email draft(s) > Subject line options > Follow-up sequence (if requested) > Quality check notes

Comment on the issue with a summary of the approach and personalisation tier.

## Rules

- Work with whatever context is provided. Do not block on missing inputs.
- Every sentence must earn its place. Under 75 words optimal for initial emails.
- Personalisation must logically connect to the problem you solve.
- Subject lines: 2-4 words, lowercase, internal-looking.
- Never ask interactive questions in comments. State your interpretation and proceed.
- All output in UK English.
